## Title writing reference

### Structural patterns that work

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Number + hidden element | "5 Hidden CAM Overcharges Your Landlord Hopes You Ignore" |
| Without painful alternative | "How to Audit Your CAM Statement Without Hiring an Accountant" |
| Specific money amount | "How This Tenant Recovered $28,000 in CAM Overcharges" |
| Landlord-doesn't-want | "The CAM Clause Your Landlord Hopes You Never Read" |
| Diagnosis + implication | "Why Your CAM Reconciliation Is Probably Wrong" |
| Direct comparison | "CAM Audit Software vs. Hiring a Forensic Accountant" |

### Title avoid list
- Generic: "Understanding CAM Charges" (no hook)
- Question-only: "Are You Being Overcharged?" (too vague, low click intent)
- Corporate: "CapVeri Product Overview" (screams ad, zero organic pull)
- Feature-led: "CapVeri's AI Detection Engine Explained" (nobody searches this)

## Competitor channel landscape (CRE YouTube)

Top commercial real estate YouTube presences focus on **landlord-side content** (investor returns, property management, acquisition). Almost nobody creates **tenant-side** content at depth. This is the whitespace:

- Tenant-side CAM education: near-zero competition
- Triple net lease explainers from tenant perspective: sparse
- Automated lease audit software demos: no direct competitor channel exists

The SEO opportunity is high because the keyword set is low-competition while being high-commercial-intent.

## Algorithm notes

- Primary keyword in the first 3 words of the title (YouTube indexes position-weighted)
- Spoken transcript is heavily indexed — naturally weave "CAM reconciliation," "pro-rata share," "gross-up methodology," "base year" into the script dialogue
- Timestamps/chapters rank independently as Google rich snippets — name them with secondary long-tail keywords, not generic labels like "Part 1"
- Mid-roll CTAs outperform end-only CTAs because significant viewer drop-off happens before the final seconds
- Slingshot Strategy: adjusting thumbnail + title alone (no re-edit) can lift CTR by 50–87% on underperforming videos — test packaging before re-shooting content
