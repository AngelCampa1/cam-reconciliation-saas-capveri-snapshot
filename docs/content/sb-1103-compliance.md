# SB 1103 CAM Compliance: What California Landlords Must Have Ready Before a Tenant Asks

**Updated: February 2026 Â· For controllers and property managers at California commercial PMCs**

California Senate Bill 1103 â€” the Commercial Tenant Protection Act â€” took effect January 1, 2025. For landlords with qualifying small-business tenants, it changed the default rules on CAM documentation, lease notice, rent increase timing, and language access. None of these provisions are triggered by court action. They activate the moment a qualifying tenant sends a written documentation request or the moment a lease change occurs.

This guide covers what Civil Code Â§1950.9 actually requires, how the 30-day clock works, which five documentation gaps are generating the most liability exposure, and what a compliance-ready documentation package looks like before anyone asks for one.

## 1. What SB 1103 Actually Requires

### The Qualified Commercial Tenant Definition

SB 1103 protections apply only to Qualified Commercial Tenants (QCTs). A tenant qualifies under one of three categories:

- **Microenterprise**: A commercial tenant that qualifies as a microenterprise under Business & Professions Code Â§18000(a) â€” meaning it has **five or fewer employees**, including the owner
- **Restaurant**: A food service business with **fewer than ten employees**
- **Nonprofit**: An organization holding Â§501(c)(3) status with **fewer than twenty employees**

**Self-attestation requirement**: The law does not obligate landlords to independently verify headcount. Under Civil Code Â§1950.9(a), a tenant establishes QCT status by providing the landlord with a written self-attestation. Once a landlord receives that attestation, all SB 1103 protections activate for that tenancy. Landlords cannot challenge the attestation absent evidence of fraud.

### Statutory Provisions in Play

SB 1103 amended or added four Civil Code provisions relevant to CAM and leasing operations:

- **Civil Code Â§1950.9**: The primary new provision. Establishes CAM documentation production obligations and the 30-day deadline. Requires itemized primary-source invoices, explicit allocation methodology, and a signed landlord attestation.
- **Civil Code Â§827**: Restricts rent increases for QCT tenants to one increase per 12-month period. Requires 90 days' advance written notice (rather than the standard 30) for any increase exceeding 10% of lowest rent charged in the prior 12 months.
- **Civil Code Â§1632**: Language access requirement. If a commercial lease was negotiated primarily in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, or Korean, the landlord must provide a written translation of the lease before execution.
- **Civil Code Â§1946.1**: Restricts automatic renewal and holdover. For QCT tenants with leases of 12 months or more, termination requires 60 days' written notice. Continued rent acceptance after lease expiration triggers month-to-month continuation under QCT protections â€” not a standard commercial holdover.

## 2. The 30-Day Clock

### What Triggers It

Under Civil Code Â§1950.9, the 30-day production clock starts when a QCT submits **any written request** for CAM documentation. The law does not require a formal legal demand. An email from a tenant asking for "the invoices behind our CAM charges" qualifies. A text message to the property manager asking for "the reconciliation backup" qualifies. The trigger is a written request, not a specific form or legal process.

**Once the clock starts, it cannot be stopped by offering a partial production or claiming the documents are being assembled.**

### What Must Be Produced Within 30 Days

Civil Code Â§1950.9 specifies three mandatory components. All three must be present; a package missing any one component is legally deficient:

1. **Itemized primary-source invoices from licensed contractors**: Summary ledgers, internal accounting printouts, and management system reports do not satisfy this requirement. The law requires the actual invoices â€” line-itemized, from the vendor or contractor who performed the work, showing the property address, work description, and amount charged.

2. **Explicit allocation tabulation showing numerator and denominator**: The CAM charge documentation must show, for each expense line, the total pool amount and the fraction applied to the requesting tenant. This means producing the pro-rata calculation: the tenant's rentable square footage as numerator, the total rentable square footage (or the contractual denominator in the lease) as denominator.

3. **Signed landlord attestation**: A written attestation, signed by the landlord or an authorized representative, confirming that the documented expenses are accurate and that the allocation methodology conforms to the lease. The attestation requirement is not satisfied by unsigned boilerplate. It must bear a signature.

### Temporal Scope

The production obligation extends:

- **18 months retrospectively**: A QCT tenant can request documentation for CAM charges assessed up to 18 months before the written request date. Landlords must retain and be able to produce primary-source invoices for all CAM charges billed to QCT tenants for at least 18 months.
- **12 months prospectively**: If a QCT tenant requests documentation for future projected CAM charges or estimates, the landlord must provide the projected allocation for the 12-month period following the request.

## 3. The 5 Documentation Gaps That Create Liability

The following gaps appear repeatedly in early SB 1103 compliance reviews. Each one is independently sufficient to block expense recovery or expose the landlord to a penalty claim under Civil Code Â§1950.9's treble damages provision.

### Gap 1: No Pre-Execution Notice of Right to Inspect

Civil Code Â§1950.9 requires landlords to provide written notice of QCT documentation rights **before lease execution**. This notice must inform the tenant that they have the right to request itemized CAM records and that the landlord must produce them within 30 days.

If the pre-execution notice was never given, the tenant may argue that the landlord cannot enforce CAM charges at all â€” because the tenant was not informed of the right to verify them at the time of signing. Several California attorneys representing small-business tenants are already using this argument. New leases with QCT tenants executed after January 1, 2025, without this notice are exposed.

### Gap 2: Summary Ledger Instead of Itemized Primary-Source Invoices

The most common gap. Property managers routinely produce Yardi or MRI-generated CAM ledger printouts in response to tenant requests, labeling them as "documentation." These are not the invoices. They are accounting entries reflecting what was billed â€” not what was contracted for and performed.

Civil Code Â§1950.9 requires the underlying invoices. A tenant or their attorney who receives a ledger printout instead of contractor invoices can reject the production as non-compliant and restart the 30-day clock â€” or argue that no compliant production was ever made.

### Gap 3: Missing Landlord Attestation

The attestation requirement is new and has no analog in standard CAM documentation practice. Most legacy reconciliation packages prepared before SB 1103 do not include a signed attestation. Producing a complete invoice set without the required attestation leaves the package legally incomplete.

The attestation must be signed by the landlord or an authorized management company representative. A cover letter from an accounts receivable clerk does not satisfy this requirement.

### Gap 4: Automated Rent Acceptance Post-Lease-Expiry

Under Civil Code Â§1946.1, accepting rent from a QCT tenant after the lease expiration date â€” without issuing a 60-day termination notice or executing a new lease â€” triggers automatic continuation of the tenancy under QCT protections. This happens regardless of intent.

The mechanism is automated rent acceptance: ACH debits, auto-pay arrangements, and property management system auto-charges that continue pulling rent after lease expiration are treated as the landlord accepting performance and electing to continue the tenancy. Month-to-month QCT tenants then have full Â§1946.1 and Â§827 protections, including notice requirements for rent changes and the rent increase frequency restrictions.

Landlords with QCT tenants whose leases are within 90 days of expiration should audit their payment processing settings immediately.

### Gap 5: No Translation for Leases Negotiated in a Covered Language

Civil Code Â§1632 has applied to residential tenancies for decades. SB 1103 expanded its application to commercial leases with QCT tenants. If the lease was **negotiated primarily** in Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, Vietnamese, or Korean, the landlord must have provided a written translation in that language before the tenant signed.

The consequence is not limited to a fine. Under Civil Code Â§1632(k), a tenant who did not receive the required translation may **rescind the contract** â€” not just challenge individual provisions, but void the entire lease. There is no time limit on the rescission right tied to the failure to translate. A five-year-old lease that was negotiated in Spanish without a Spanish translation is potentially voidable today.

## 4. How to Achieve Compliance Before a Tenant Asks

The liability exposure under SB 1103 is largest for landlords who wait for a tenant request before assembling documentation. The 30-day clock is short; primary-source invoices from contractors can take weeks to recover if not retained in real time. The five-step process below creates a defensible compliance position before any request arrives.

### Step 1: Audit Every Current Lease for QCT Status

Pull your full tenant roster and identify every tenant that could plausibly qualify under the 5/10/20 employee headcount rule. Do not rely on your recollection of what kind of business each tenant runs. Use written self-attestations: send a form letter to every commercial tenant with 20 or fewer employees asking them to confirm their headcount under penalty of perjury, or to confirm they do not qualify as a QCT.

Once a tenant returns a signed self-attestation confirming QCT status, annotate the lease file and activate SB 1103 protocols for that tenancy. Track attestation receipt dates; these establish when protections became active.

### Step 2: Add Pre-Execution Notice to All New Leases

Draft a one-page QCT Rights Notice modeled on the Civil Code Â§1950.9 language. It should state that the tenant has the right to request itemized CAM documentation, that the landlord must respond within 30 days, and that the response must include primary-source invoices, allocation calculations, and a signed attestation.

Require tenants to sign and date the notice as a lease exhibit. Retain the signed notice in the lease file. This eliminates Gap 1 for all new leases.

### Step 3: Digitize and Attach Invoices Real-Time to Tenant Ledger

The most operationally significant change SB 1103 requires is moving from year-end batch invoice collection to real-time invoice capture. Every time a vendor invoice is processed for a property with QCT tenants, the invoice must be:

- Received in original form (PDF from contractor, not scanned summary)
- Tagged to the property and expense period
- Retained in a system that allows retrieval by tenant and date range

A property manager who waits until reconciliation season to collect backup invoices will not be able to produce a compliant package within 30 days if a QCT tenant requests documentation mid-year.

### Step 4: Build the 30-Day Production Kit

Before any tenant asks, prepare a template production package for each QCT-occupied property that includes:

- Folder structure: invoices organized by month and GL category
- Allocation matrix template: pre-formatted spreadsheet showing the pro-rata calculation for each tenant
- Signed attestation template: drafted and awaiting only the current date and signature

When a tenant request arrives, producing a compliant package should take hours, not weeks.

### Step 5: Set Calendar Alerts for Statutory Notice Deadlines

Calendar the following alerts for every QCT tenancy:

- **90-day alerts** before any planned rent increase exceeding 10% (Civil Code Â§827 requires 90 days' written notice for increases above this threshold)
- **60-day alerts** before lease expiration (Civil Code Â§1946.1 requires 60 days' termination notice to avoid automatic month-to-month continuation)
- **30-day production target** for any CAM documentation request (calendar the request date and the 30-day deadline immediately upon receipt)

## 5. Frequently Asked Questions

**Does SB 1103 apply to all California commercial leases?**

No. SB 1103 protections apply only to Qualified Commercial Tenants â€” defined as microenterprises with 5 or fewer employees (including the owner) under B&P Â§18000(a), restaurants with fewer than 10 employees, and Â§501(c)(3) nonprofits with fewer than 20 employees. Standard commercial tenants â€” corporations, partnerships, larger businesses â€” are not covered by these provisions.

**What happens if we miss the 30-day production deadline?**

Civil Code Â§1950.9 creates a private right of action for QCT tenants who do not receive compliant documentation within 30 days of a written request. Courts may award actual damages, statutory damages, and attorney's fees. The treble damages provision applies if the court finds the landlord's non-compliance was willful. Missing the deadline once can generate liability exceeding the total CAM charges in dispute.

**Can a tenant self-certify QCT status or do we need to verify it?**

The law allows self-attestation: the tenant provides a signed written statement confirming they meet the headcount threshold. You are not required to independently verify the attestation. However, once you receive a signed attestation and act on it as though the tenant qualifies, you cannot later deny QCT protections to that tenant without evidence of fraud in the attestation.

**Are property management companies considered the "landlord" for purposes of Civil Code Â§1950.9?**

Yes. A property management company acting as the landlord's authorized representative bears the same obligations as the property owner. The 30-day clock runs against whoever controls the documentation and bills the CAM charges. Property managers who do not have primary-source invoice retention procedures in place are personally exposed â€” and their clients are as well.

**Do the SB 1103 provisions apply to leases signed before January 1, 2025?**

Yes. The documentation production obligations, notice requirements, and rent increase notice requirements apply to existing QCT tenancies, not just new leases. The only provision that applies only prospectively is the pre-execution notice requirement â€” you cannot retroactively provide a notice that was due before signing. But all other obligations, including the 30-day response requirement and the Civil Code Â§1632 translation requirement, apply to current QCT tenants regardless of when the lease was signed.
