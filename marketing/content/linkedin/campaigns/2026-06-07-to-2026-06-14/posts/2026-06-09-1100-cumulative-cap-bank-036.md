Cumulative caps need a ledger, not a memory.

The cap bank is easy to explain:

If actual controllable expenses are below the allowed ceiling, unused capacity may carry forward when the lease says the cap is cumulative.

The hard part is proving the balance later.

Each year needs the same basic row:

Base amount.
Annual cap ceiling.
Actual controllable amount.
Amount billed.
Unused capacity.
Bank balance.

When that schedule is missing, the next reconciliation starts with a trust problem.

If the lease says non-cumulative, the unused capacity disappears. If it says cumulative, the carry-forward needs to survive ownership changes, staff changes, and spreadsheet cleanups.

That is the control.
