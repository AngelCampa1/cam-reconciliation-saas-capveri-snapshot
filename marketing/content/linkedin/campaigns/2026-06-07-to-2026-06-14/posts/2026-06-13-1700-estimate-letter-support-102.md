Estimate letters should not be treated as a January admin task.

They are the first version of the reconciliation conversation.

If the estimate is based on prior-year actuals, inflation assumptions, utility rate changes, and new vendor contracts, the file should show that. When the year-end true-up lands, the team can explain the path from projection to actuals.

The formula at year-end is direct:

Adjusted expense pool x pro-rata share, minus estimated billings collected.

The messy part is the expectation gap.

A tenant is less surprised by a true-up when the estimate letter already made the basis clear and the team reviewed run-rate drift before year-end.

CapVeri does not make estimates magically right. It gives the team a cleaner way to compare the projection against the GL reality before the gap becomes a tenant call.
