One cap error hides until expenses spike:

Compounding from prior actuals instead of the prior cap ceiling.

In a quiet year, actual controllable expenses may sit below both numbers. Nobody notices.

Then costs jump.

Now the ceiling matters, and the wrong base has been dragging the allowable path down for years.

The control is to preserve two separate numbers:

Actual controllable expenses.
Cap ceiling.

They are related. They are not interchangeable.

For cumulative caps, the bank adds another layer. For non-cumulative caps, unused capacity disappears. In both cases, the ceiling history needs to survive the annual workbook refresh.

The cap schedule should read like a ledger, not a set of overwritten cells.
