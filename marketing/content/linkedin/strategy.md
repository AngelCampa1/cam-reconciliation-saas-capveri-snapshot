# CapVeri LinkedIn Content Strategy

## Position

CapVeri is the CRE FinOps platform for commercial landlords. We automate CAM reconciliation by reading ERP exports directly - no Yardi replacement, no API integrations, no implementation consultants. Deterministic BOMA 2024 calculations. Every dollar traceable.

**One-sentence positioning:** CAM reconciliation software that catches billing errors and closes the 3–5% recovery gap, without replacing your ERP.

## ICP

Primary buyer roles:
- VP Asset Management / Director of Asset Management (owns NOI, cares about recovery leakage)
- Property Controller / Senior Property Accountant (does the work, drowns in Q1)
- VP Property Management (manages team throughput, responsible for tenant disputes)
- CFO / Finance Director at PMCs with 15–200 properties
- Portfolio Manager / Acquisitions (evaluates CAM quality at underwriting)

Portfolio profile: 15–500 units of commercial (office, industrial, retail, mixed-use). Mid-market PMCs and regional REITS. Yardi, MRI, or RealPage users who aren't replacing their ERP.

## Voice

**Write for:** a 12-year property accountant who has seen every CAM mess there is. She reads fast, trusts specifics, ignores hype. She will save a post with a real formula. She will ignore a post with a generic headline.

**Tone:** Operator-confident. Direct. Specific. Occasionally blunt. Never cheerful.

**Use:**
- "Deterministic" / "auditable" / "traceable"
- "Without replacing Yardi/MRI" (say it often, it's load-bearing positioning)
- Real operator vocabulary: GL export, recovery pool, gross-up, base year, pro rata, occupancy denominator, BOMA 2024, cap bank, cumulative vs. non-cumulative
- Specific numbers from the source content (e.g., "$6,000 in overbilling on a $200K pool at a 1.03 factor")
- Hedging when appropriate: "in our experience," "we've seen," "across the portfolios we work with"

**Avoid:**
- Em-dashes ( - ) completely. Use periods or parentheses.
- "Revolutionize," "game-changer," "unlock," "leverage" (verb), "delve," "seamless," "cutting-edge," "best-in-class," "world-class," "comprehensive solution," "synergy," "ever-evolving"
- "It's not X, it's Y" rhetorical pattern
- Generic hooks ("Most property managers make this mistake")
- Three-item parallel lists as closers ("faster, smarter, better")
- First-person singular "I" (this is the company page, not Angel's personal page)
- "Excited to announce" or "thrilled to share" openers
- Hashtag spam (max 3, lowercase, at the end)

## Post Structure by Format

### text-short (80–250 words)
Line 1–2: specific hook (a number, a scenario, a counterintuitive fact)
Body: 2–4 short paragraphs, 1–3 sentences each
Close: optional one-line observation or soft CTA (not mandatory)
Hashtags: 0–3 lowercase at end

### text-long (400–700 words)
Line 1–2: hook - must be specific and concrete
Body: numbered list or short sections (no headers, LinkedIn doesn't support them)
Close: one practical takeaway or framing
Hashtags: 0–2

### carousel (outline only - user designs the visuals)
Slide 1: hook/title slide - one punchy line
Slides 2–8: one idea per slide, concise headline + 1–2 support lines
Last slide: takeaway or soft CTA
Note: carousel outlines use "SLIDE 1:", "SLIDE 2:" labels

### image-quote (under 80 words + image brief)
Very short caption
IMAGE BRIEF: what text goes on the image and any visual direction

### poll
QUESTION: the poll question
OPTION A:
OPTION B:
OPTION C:
OPTION D:
SHORT CAPTION: 1–2 lines setting up the poll

### video (60–90 second talking-points script)
Hook line (say this on camera, first 3 seconds)
Talking points (bulleted, casual spoken language)
Closing line
ON-SCREEN TEXT: what to show during key points
