Here's what a $47,000 problem looks like before anyone notices it.

A mid-size retail portfolio. Eight tenants. Each lease has a gross-up provision allowing normalization to 95% occupancy. The property was operating at 96% during the base period.

The ERP was configured with the gross-up factor at 1.02. No one flagged it because the report ran clean, the statements went out, and the tenants paid.

At 96% actual occupancy against a 95% threshold, the gross-up factor should be 1.0. Exactly. Not 1.02. The building was already above target. There was nothing to normalize.

Applied across eight tenants over three years, the 0.02 factor excess added approximately $47,000 in charges that the leases didn't support.

The error was found by a tenant auditor in Year 4. The landlord had to issue credits to all eight tenants, recalculate three years of reconciliations, and spend two months in dispute resolution.

The original ERP configuration was a single-field entry. The annual check that would have caught it takes about two minutes: compare the actual occupancy percentage to the gross-up threshold in the lease.

That check didn't exist in the workflow.
