The most common CAM estimate forecasting error is applying a flat 3% to every expense category.

Looking across Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI and Producer Price Index series for building services, plus commercial property insurance and broker market reporting:
Janitorial: tracking close to general inflation in recent years. That's close to a flat 3%.
Repairs and maintenance: running higher than headline CPI per BLS PPI series for building maintenance. Different speed.
Commercial property insurance: ran through a multi-year hard market with double-digit renewal increases per Marsh and Lockton market index reporting before easing in 2024. Very different speed.
Property taxes: tied to local reassessment cycles that vary by jurisdiction. Not correlated with CPI at all.

A flat 3% estimate on a portfolio where insurance is a large line item will systematically undershoot. By year two, the true-up on insurance alone is 12-20% of that category's estimate. The tenant receives a true-up statement that doesn't match what they budgeted, questions it, and sometimes disputes it.

The contrarian point: the goal of setting estimates is not accuracy for its own sake. It is minimizing true-up surprise. A $2.00/SF true-up on a 15,000 SF tenant is a $30,000 surprise bill that their CFO did not budget for. True-ups exceeding 15-20% of the annual estimate are the primary trigger for tenant audits.

The practical fix: forecast by category, not by total. Use the trailing 3-year average for each expense type. Apply specific inflation rates: actual utility rate changes, actual insurance renewal rates, known property tax assessments. Mid-year adjustments are permissible in most leases when actual expenses track 15% above estimates. use them to close the gap before year-end creates a large one-time charge.

The goal is a true-up that looks like a rounding error.
