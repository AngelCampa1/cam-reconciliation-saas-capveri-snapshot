Something Angel Campa noticed while scoping the problem CapVeri was built to solve: the most dangerous CAM errors are the ones that run cleanly.

A gross-up factor set to 1.04 when actual occupancy was already at 97%. The system doesn't throw an error. The report runs. The statement goes out. The tenant pays.

Two years later, the tenant's auditor asks for documentation supporting the gross-up factor applied in Year 1. There isn't a good answer, because the factor shouldn't have been above 1.0 in that scenario.

The payment was made. The audit finding is real. Now the landlord is negotiating a credit from a position of weakness.

What makes this category of error persistent is that no one is looking for it. The ERP isn't designed to flag configuration against lease terms. The property accountant ran the numbers they were given. The statement looked normal.

The check that catches it is simple: compare actual occupancy to the gross-up threshold before running the reconciliation. One line. Two numbers. But it only happens if someone builds the step into the process.
