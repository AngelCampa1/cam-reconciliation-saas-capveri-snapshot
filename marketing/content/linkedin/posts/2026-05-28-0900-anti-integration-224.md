Excel doesn't charge per reconciliation, which makes it feel free. It is not.

The real cost sits in three places: labor hours nobody tracks, errors nobody catches until they are expensive, and audit exposure nobody prices until it arrives.

Labor that does not scale:
A 10-property NNN portfolio can consume roughly 250 controller hours a year for GL exports, manual classification, lease lookups, gross-up math, cap checks, statement generation, and review.

Those hours land in Q1, when the team is also closing books, preparing tax workpapers, and building budgets.

Errors that compound:
Spreadsheets have no validation layer. Excel does not know that a gross-up threshold changed at renewal, or that a cumulative cap bank disappeared in a copied worksheet.

Material error classes include gross-up applied to fixed expenses, cap escalators not carried forward, wrong cell references in pro-rata calculations, and excluded GL accounts that should have been recovered.

Audit exposure with no defense:
When a tenant asks for the calculation methodology, Excel cannot prove when a formula was set, who changed it, or whether the workbook shown is the same one used to bill.

Illustrative annual cost model for a 10-property portfolio:
Excel: $0 software cost, $16,250 reconciliation labor, $3,250 QA labor, $8,000-$30,000 in error-related costs, and $4,000-$8,000 per tenant audit event.

Purpose-built CAM software: $5,000-$25,000 software cost, materially lower labor, cleaner QA, and lower error exposure.

When to stay with Excel: 1-3 properties, simple NNN leases, no gross-up clauses, no cumulative caps, and a stable controller who owns the workbook.

When to move: four or more properties, any gross-up clause, any cumulative cap, recent tenant audit findings, or reconciliation consuming more than 100 hours per year.
