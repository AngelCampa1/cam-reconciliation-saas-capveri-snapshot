Property managers are not accountants. But they control the inputs that make or break the CAM reconciliation.

The math belongs to the controller. The lease abstract accuracy, occupancy records, move-in and move-out dates, and contractual deadline tracking belong to the property manager. Get those inputs wrong and the controller runs perfect math on wrong data.

The most destructive input error: running the reconciliation from a superseded lease abstract. A tenant who expanded in April, increasing from 4,200 to 6,800 RSF, whose abstract was never updated. the entire year's CAM was calculated using the wrong numerator.

The most common communication error: verbally agreeing to an adjustment without lease authority. A tenant says "this seems too high, can you take $3,000 off?". The PM says "let me see what I can do." That response is a binding commitment. The correct response is to document the concern, route it to accounting with the lease documents, and commit to a written response by a specific date. not to negotiate in real time.

The one deadline that forfeits collection: statement delivery. In some leases, delivery past the contractual window permanently waives the landlord's right to collect the true-up balance. The PM owns the delivery calendar. Start building the reconciliation data package 60 days before the deadline, not 10 days before.

The five things to deliver to accounting before the reconciliation runs: current lease abstract index, occupancy log with move-in and move-out dates, amendment index, mid-year events log, and tenant roster for delivery. These are the PM's deliverables. Accounting cannot produce an accurate reconciliation without them.
