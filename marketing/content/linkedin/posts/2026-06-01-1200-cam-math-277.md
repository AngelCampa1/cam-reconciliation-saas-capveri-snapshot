Seven specific errors account for most of the dollar damage in commercial CAM reconciliation. The numbers behind them are worth knowing.

Error 1: Gross-up applied to buildings already at occupancy target. On a $200,000 variable expense pool, a factor of 1.03 when actual occupancy is at or above target adds $6,000 in charges that shouldn't exist. No error message. The statement reconciles cleanly.

Error 2: Cap rate entered as a whole number instead of a decimal. A 5% cap entered as 5 instead of 0.05 sets a 500% cap, which is no cap at all. The tenant's statement shows a number. The math is correct. The protection the tenant negotiated is gone.

Error 3: Zero prior year locking out new tenants. Non-cumulative cap math: max allowed = prior year CAM × (1 + cap rate). New tenant, no prior year: max allowed = $0 × 1.05 = $0. Tenant owes nothing regardless of actual expenses. Systems that don't skip the cap for first-year tenants produce this error automatically.

Error 4: Cumulative cap bank treated as unlimited. A 5% cumulative cap with a 3-year bank of unused capacity doesn't allow unlimited future recovery. The bank is drawn once and gone. Landlords who misunderstand this over-collect in spike years. Tenants who don't know it exists dispute bills that look like they exceeded the cap.

Error 5: Occupancy miscalculation from overlapping or reversed lease dates. Overlapping leases push occupancy above 100%, breaking the denominator for every tenant. Reversed dates cause systems to skip leases silently, making the building look nearly vacant.

Error 6: Admin fee calculated before cap is applied. Admin fee on the gross expense pool versus after the cap reduces the tenant's share: $4,500 vs. $3,300 on a $30,000 pre-cap share. That $1,200 repeats every year, for every capped tenant in the building.

Error 7: Base year set during a vacancy anomaly year. A 2020 or 2021 base year sets an artificially low floor. Every subsequent year, normal occupancy-level expenses look like dramatic increases. The tenant pays for the normalization, not the inflation.

These seven errors share a characteristic: they don't look wrong until someone runs the math against the lease. A statement that reconciles internally can contain all seven of them simultaneously.

The resolution: pre-send verification that runs each of these checks before the statement is delivered, not after the tenant's auditor finds them.
