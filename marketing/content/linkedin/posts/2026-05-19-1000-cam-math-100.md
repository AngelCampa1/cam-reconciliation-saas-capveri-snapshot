A 100,000 SF Class A office building remeasured under BOMA 2024 gains 4,800 RSF without a single square foot of new construction. Rooftop terrace: 2,800 SF. Fitness center: 1,400 SF. Single-tenant mechanical closets: 600 SF. Newly measured, newly recognized.

That 4.8% increase creates a recovery dilution problem for every existing lease. Here's the math on Tenant A, who holds 15,000 SF.

Before remeasurement: 15,000 / 100,000 = 15.00% pro-rata share. CAM charge: $187,500.
After remeasurement: 15,000 / 104,800 = 14.31% pro-rata share. CAM charge: $178,875.

Tenant A's annual CAM drops by $8,625. not because expenses decreased, but because the denominator grew. With 82,000 SF of existing leases still referencing the old 100,000 SF denominator, the landlord's total annual recovery shortfall is approximately $47,000. At a 6.5% cap rate, that $47,000 translates to $723,000 in lost property value.

This is the core tension: remeasurement increases the building's competitive position and supports higher rents on new leases, but it dilutes recovery on existing leases until those leases roll. The break-even point on a building with 20% annual lease rollover is near the end of Year 3.

Do not update existing lease denominators without tenant agreement. The financial benefit of remeasurement comes from new leases, not from forcing a denominator change on tenants whose leases don't permit it.
