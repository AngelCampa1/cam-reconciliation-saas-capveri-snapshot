Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"Institutional investors buying commercial real estate don't just look at NOI. They look at the recovery ratio. And A 5-point gap in that ratio can represent millions of dollars in suppressed asset value."

Talking points:
- The recovery ratio is the percentage of total operating expenses that is recovered from tenants through CAM, taxes, and insurance. A 90% recovery ratio means 10% of operating expenses come out of ownership's pocket. A 85% recovery ratio means 15% does. On a $3 million expense base, that 5-point gap is $150,000 per year in unrecovered cost. - At a 6% cap rate, $150,000 in annual NOI is $2.5 million in asset value. Institutional buyers price this gap into the acquisition price. The seller may not even know the gap exists until due diligence surfaces it. - The three most common sources of recovery ratio gaps: unmapped GL accounts. recoverable expenses coded to accounts not included in the reconciliation pool; CapEx over-classification, capital improvements incorrectly coded as operating expenses, which inflates the apparent pool but lacks lease support;. Stale lease abstracts. leases that were amended but whose amendments were never reflected in the reconciliation configuration. - The stale abstract problem is particularly common in portfolios that have been held for 5+ years. A lease executed in 2018 may have been amended in 2021 and 2023. Each amendment may have changed gross-up thresholds, exclusion lists, or pro-rata denominators. The reconciliation still runs on 2018 terms. - Recovery ratio analysis at the portfolio level is how institutional buyers find these gaps. They compare the recovery ratio trend across years for each property. A declining trend without a vacancy explanation is a signal to dig deeper. - The window where these gaps can be addressed is before the sale process starts. Once a buyer's team is in the data room, the gaps become price negotiation items. Before then, they are operational problems with operational solutions.

Closing line:
"The recovery ratio gap in your portfolio is priced in by the buyer. It should be found by you first."
