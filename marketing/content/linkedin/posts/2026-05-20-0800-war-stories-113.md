SLIDE 1:
CAM leakage benchmarks by property type
(How much recovery you're leaving on the table)

Based on industry estimates derived from tenant audit firm published findings and operating-expense benchmarks, typical annual leakage by property type runs in these illustrative ranges:

SLIDE 2:
Office buildings
Leakage range: 1.0-2.5% of annual operating expenses.
Most common cause: gross-up applied incorrectly in high-vacancy years.
Class A urban office faces the most auditor scrutiny. Class B suburban has the most systematic leakage.

SLIDE 3:
Retail properties
Leakage range: 1.5-3.5% of annual operating expenses.
Most common cause: expense classification errors, management fees on the wrong base, tenant-specific costs billed to the common pool.
Retail leases often have the most complex exclusion lists. Complexity creates leakage.

SLIDE 4:
Industrial properties
Leakage range: 0.5-1.5% of annual operating expenses.
Most common cause: pro-rata denominator errors in large multi-tenant parks.
Lower leakage rate than office or retail, but higher dollar impact per percentage point given the large expense pools.

SLIDE 5:
Medical office buildings (MOB)
Leakage range: 2.0-3.5% of annual operating expenses.
Most common cause: expense categorization between medical and standard office components.
MOB leases are among the most complex. The categorization errors that create leakage are hard to catch without systematic lease comparison.

SLIDE 6:
Dollar impact: Class A office example
$2M annual CAM pool. 1.5% leakage rate.
Annual underrecovery: $30,000.
At a 6% cap rate: $500,000 in suppressed asset value.
Three years without detection: $90,000 in cumulative leakage.

SLIDE 7:
Dollar impact: Regional retail example
$3.5M annual CAM pool. 2.5% leakage rate.
Annual underrecovery: $87,500.
At a 6% cap rate: $1.46M in suppressed asset value.
This is the property type where leakage most often shows up at disposition.

SLIDE 8:
Leakage is invisible until someone looks.
The comparison is: what does the lease permit, versus what was actually billed?
Running that comparison before each reconciliation season is how leakage gets caught before it compounds.
