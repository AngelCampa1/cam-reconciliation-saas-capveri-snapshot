Configuration errors set at lease execution compound for the full 7-to-10-year lease term. Here is where the risk concentrates by year, and when correcting it matters most.

Not all reconciliation risk is equal across a portfolio. The configuration drift that creates errors tends to cluster around specific lease events. Here's how to think about where to focus your verification effort.

Year 0-1: The implementation risk window
The lease was just executed. The ERP configuration was just set up. This is the highest-risk moment for foundational errors: wrong cap type, wrong base year, missing exclusions. Errors set here will compound for the life of the lease. A configuration review within the first reconciliation cycle is the most valuable check in the entire workflow.

Year 2-3: The amendment drift window
Most leases have at least one amendment or modification in the first three years. Expansion of space, rent abatement extension, exclusion modification. Each amendment should trigger a configuration review of the affected terms. In practice, the amendment gets executed and the property accountant is notified, but the ERP update doesn't always follow. This is the most common source of multi-year errors.

Year 4-6: The personnel transition window
Property accounting teams turn over. The person who set up the original configuration is often gone by Year 4. Their successor works from what's in the system, not from the original lease. Documentation quality determines whether the handoff preserves accuracy. If lease summaries are incomplete or the ERP configuration notes are missing, this is where errors from Year 1 get perpetuated indefinitely.

Year 7+: The audit trigger window
Tenants on long-term leases with upcoming renewal decisions often hire auditors in the year before the renewal conversation. A seven-year tenant reviewing whether to renew is also reviewing whether to raise the CAM history as a negotiating point. Errors that have been running since Year 1 are fully compounded by this point.

The implication for portfolio risk management: configuration verification is most valuable at Year 0-1 (catch foundational errors early) and at the amendment trigger point (catch drift before it compounds). Verification at Year 7+ is still worthwhile, but the errors are already expensive by then.
