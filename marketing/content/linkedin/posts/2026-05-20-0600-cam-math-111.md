An $11,637 CAM true-up balance with no supporting schedule is an invoice with no basis. Tenants are not required to pay it without backup.

A defensible true-up starts with the formula:

Actual CAM obligation minus estimated payments made equals true-up amount.

Positive means the tenant underpaid. Negative means the tenant overpaid and receives a credit or refund.

The statement should show more than the final number:

- Total recoverable expense pool
- Excluded items and why they were excluded
- Gross-up calculation, if used
- Tenant denominator and pro-rata share
- Cap calculation, if the lease has a cap
- Estimates paid during the year
- Final balance due or credit owed

Three errors generate many audit findings.

1. Wrong pro-rata denominator.
If the building square footage in the system was never updated, every tenant share can be wrong.

2. Gross-up applied to fixed expenses.
Gross-up belongs on variable expenses. Property taxes, insurance, and many management fees do not vary with occupancy.

3. Cap not applied or applied incorrectly.
A 5% cumulative cap is not the same as a 5% non-cumulative cap. The distinction compounds across years.

Deadline note: many leases require delivery within 90 to 180 days after fiscal year-end. In some leases, missing that window can forfeit collection rights.
