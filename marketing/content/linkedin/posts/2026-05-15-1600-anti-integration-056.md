Industry standard is 90-120 days after fiscal year-end. Most mid-size property accounting teams land in Q2. Portfolios without dedicated reconciliation processes regularly push into Q3.

That's not just an operational inconvenience. It costs money in ways most teams don't fully price.

The direct cash flow impact is real: a portfolio of 20 buildings with $15,000 average true-ups represents $300,000 in delayed receivables for every month late. At a 5% cost of capital, that's $2,500 per month in carrying cost.

The bigger problem is what delay does to tenant behavior. True-up bills that arrive eight months after year-end face higher dispute rates than bills arriving within 90 days. Tenants who budgeted for a Q1 true-up don't have budget authority for a surprise bill in August. Their first instinct is to question the charges. Late delivery didn't cause the errors. But It created the adversarial dynamic that turns errors into disputes.

And in California, late reconciliation has statutory teeth. SB 1103 requires landlords to respond to qualified small-business tenant documentation requests within 30 days. Miss the window and treble damages are on the table.

Five bottlenecks account for most timeline slippage.

Siloed spreadsheets with no shared logic. A gross-up formula fix has to be manually replicated across every building. There is no single version of the calculation. there are as many versions as there are properties.

Manual AP data entry. The average invoice cycle takes 9.2 days from receipt to posting, according to PredictAP. Multiply by hundreds of vendor invoices per building and the AP backlog alone pushes reconciliation start dates into late Q1.

Waiting for stragglers. Property tax assessments and insurance true-ups often arrive in November or December. Teams that wait for every final figure before starting reconciliation are starting in February at the earliest.

Lease complexity. A 40-tenant building may require 40 separate calculation paths. different cap types, different gross-up provisions, different exclusions, different denominators. Manual reconciliation handles each one sequentially with no way to parallelize.

What automation changes: the 9.2-day invoice cycle compresses to 3.1 days with automated OCR coding (PredictAP data). Spreadsheet-per-property logic becomes shared calculation logic that runs across the portfolio simultaneously. Formula changes apply everywhere. Calculations that take days in spreadsheets become minutes of reviewable output.

The timeline improvement doesn't come from working faster. It comes from eliminating the steps that consume 65-80% of calendar time without adding value.
