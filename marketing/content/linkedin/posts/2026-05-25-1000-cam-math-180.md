The most common misconception about gross-up: it increases what tenants pay when the building is empty.

That's not what it does.

Gross-up prevents tenants from getting a windfall reduction because the building has vacancies. At 80% occupancy, utility bills are $120,000. At 95% occupancy, they would have been $142,500. The 20% vacancy lowered the actual bill by $22,500. But That reduction doesn't belong to the 80% of tenants who are present. Gross-up removes the vacancy windfall.

The contrarian point: gross-up is not a landlord-favorable adjustment. It's an occupancy-neutral adjustment. Done correctly, it produces the same per-occupied-SF cost regardless of whether the building is 80% or 95% occupied.

Done incorrectly, it does two things property managers should know about. First: applying gross-up to fixed expenses like insurance or property taxes. Fixed costs don't vary with occupancy. grossing them up is a systematic overbilling. Second: applying gross-up when the building is already at or above the threshold. Any factor above 1.0 when actual occupancy >= target is wrong.

These two errors together account for the majority of gross-up disputes. Both are detectable in 15 minutes before the statement goes out.
