The non-cumulative CAM cap calculation has one critical distinction that most leases don't make explicit: does the cap apply to the prior year's actual expenses, or to the prior year's capped amount?

In a year where the cap was triggered, they produce different Year 2 ceilings.

Example: $140,000 controllable base. 5% non-cumulative cap.
Year 1 actual: $158,000. Cap ceiling: $147,000. Tenant pays $147,000.

Year 2 ceiling if cap applies to prior year actual ($158,000): $147,000 × 1.05 = $154,350. Wait. no. Prior year actual was $158,000. $158,000 × 1.05 = $165,900.

Year 2 ceiling if cap applies to prior year capped amount ($147,000): $147,000 × 1.05 = $154,350.

The difference is $11,550 in Year 2's cap ceiling, which flows directly into whether the tenant pays actual expenses or the cap amount.

The tenant-favorable reading: the cap applies to the prior year's capped amount (the lower number), which is what they actually paid. The landlord-favorable reading: the cap compounds from prior year actual, which produces a higher ceiling in years where the cap was triggered.

The lease controls. When it's silent, this becomes a dispute. Most leases written before 2015 are silent on this point.

Before the reconciliation runs: pull the cap clause, identify the base, and use the number the lease specifies. When the lease doesn't specify, document which interpretation you applied and why.
