A 15-property portfolio spending 65-80% of its reconciliation cycle on verification and rework, not on calculation, does not have a staffing problem. It has a sequencing problem. In practice, most reconciliation time isn't spent doing the work. It's spent waiting, re-checking, and fixing.

For a 15-property portfolio, a typical cycle looks like this: 20-25% waiting for GL close and data gathering, 25-30% manual ERP-vs-lease verification, 20-25% rework from errors discovered late in the process, 15-20% statement drafting, 10-15% review and approval.

The first three categories. waiting, manual verification, and rework. are 65-80% of total time. None of these require adding staff. They require process redesign.

The highest-value change: validate before you draft, not after. The time cost of a validation pass before drafting is 30-60 minutes. The time cost of discovering a pool-level error after drafting ten of fifteen tenant statements is 3-5 hours of rework, because a pool error affects every tenant in that pool.

Pre-close preparation in Q4 cuts the waiting category almost entirely. If lease abstracts are confirmed, ERP configuration is audited, and denominator updates are done before January 1, reconciliation can start the day GL close happens.

Parallel processing, starting multiple properties simultaneously rather than finishing one before starting the next. compresses calendar time without requiring more total hours.

And batching statement delivery, rather than sending as each property is completed, creates a cleaner operational rhythm with less overlap between drafting and responding to questions.

These are process changes, not technology investments. Though the validation step gets dramatically faster when a tool runs the independent calculation before drafting rather than manually.
