Houston's office vacancy rate is 26.5%. At that occupancy level, the gross-up multiplier on variable CAM expenses is 1.293x.

That number matters because it amplifies every error in the expense pool. A $100,000 fixed expense incorrectly included in the gross-up base does not generate $100,000 in overbilling. It generates $129,300. because the multiplier inflates the error along with the legitimate variable expenses.

In Houston's office market right now, the single most common CAM error is grossing up property taxes. Property taxes are fixed. They do not vary with occupancy. Grossing them up is a lease violation. At 26.5% vacancy, the multiplier is nearly 1.3x, which makes this error more expensive than in any other major market.

The second problem is stale occupancy data. The gross-up factor should be set using the occupancy as of the date the lease specifies. not last month's number, not an annual average. In a market where occupancy moved several points during the year, using the wrong measurement date produces a different multiplier and a different billed amount.

Houston's vacancy numbers are a market story. The CAM implications are an operational story. Property managers handling Houston office portfolios this reconciliation season need to verify three things before statements go out: that property taxes are excluded from the gross-up base, that insurance is excluded from the gross-up base, and that the occupancy percentage used reflects the lease-specified measurement date.

Three checks. Each one worth $10,000-$50,000 per tenant depending on pool size.
