CPAs verifying CAM charges for tenants follow a seven-step process. Most property managers have never seen it laid out this way.

Step 1: GL reconciliation. The CPA ties the total CAM pool on the statement back to the GL. Any variance between the statement total and the GL is finding number one.

Step 2: Expense categorization. Every GL account in the CAM pool gets mapped against the lease's operating expense definition. Accounts that appear recoverable on their face but are excluded by the lease get flagged.

Step 3: Gross-up verification. The CPA checks whether actual occupancy cleared the gross-up threshold. If yes, the multiplier should be 1.0. They also verify that property taxes and insurance were excluded from the gross-up base.

Step 4: Denominator check. The tenant's square footage is verified against the lease exhibit. The building denominator is checked for consistency year over year.

Step 5: Cap application. If a CAM cap applies, the CPA reconstructs the cap bank from the base year forward. Over-cap charges that weren't caught in prior years compound into the current year.

Step 6: Exclusion list. Line items the lease explicitly excludes. legal fees, leasing commissions, capital improvements, are checked against the billed pool.

Step 7: Statement tie-out. The final billed amount is recalculated from the verified inputs and compared to what the tenant was charged.

The seven steps take an experienced CPA 4-6 hours per lease. They have been doing this long enough to know where errors concentrate. The findings they produce are rarely wrong.

Property managers who want to reduce audit exposure run the same seven checks before the statement leaves the building.
