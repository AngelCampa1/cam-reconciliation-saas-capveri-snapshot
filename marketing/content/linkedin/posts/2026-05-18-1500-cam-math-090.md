A flat 3% estimate increase every year is not a forecast. It's a guess that gets more expensive every time you're wrong.

Insurance premiums in commercial real estate have risen 8-15% annually since 2023. A flat 3% forecast against 12% actual insurance growth creates a 9-point variance in just one line item. Across a 50,000 SF building with $180,000 in annual insurance, that's a $16,200 miss per year, compounding.

The large true-up bills that generate tenant disputes are almost always the result of estimate accuracy failures, not reconciliation math errors. A tenant who paid $8,500 per month in estimates and receives a $35,000 true-up bill for a calendar year will dispute the reconciliation even if every number in it is correct. The problem isn't the reconciliation. The problem is that the estimates were set to be comfortable, not accurate.

Forecasting by category, not by total, is the only method that holds up. Insurance follows a contract renewal cycle: you know the renewal date and can get a rate indication 60 days before. Property taxes follow assessment cycles with published schedules. Utilities are weather and rate sensitive. Controllable expenses like janitorial and landscaping typically follow market rates with some predictability.

A 15% or more year-over-year increase in any single category should trigger a mid-year estimate adjustment, not a wait-and-see approach until the true-up. The lease audit rights provision starts running when the statement is delivered, not when the estimate first diverged from reality. But the tenant complaint starts the moment the true-up arrives.

Set estimates to be accurate. The discomfort of a higher monthly estimate is far cheaper than the dispute cost of a large lump-sum true-up.
