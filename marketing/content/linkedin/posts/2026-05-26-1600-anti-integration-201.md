A 12-property portfolio running an AI-powered reconciliation tool received a tenant audit claim in Q1 2025. The platform couldn't reproduce its own output from 18 months earlier. The difference between useful and dangerous comes down to one question: is the AI doing extraction or calculation?

As an illustrative scenario for a 12-property portfolio: a 12-property portfolio generates roughly 3,600 vendor invoices per year. At 8 minutes per invoice for manual data entry, that is 480 hours of staff time. AI extraction with human spot-checking reduces that to roughly 120 hours of review, saving $27,000 annually at $75 per hour fully loaded. Internal estimates suggest a 180-page commercial lease takes 2-4 hours for an experienced paralegal. AI cuts that to 15-30 minutes of review time.

This is where AI earns its place. Not in the calculation.

The reason is not that AI gets the math wrong most of the time. AI may get most extractions right. The problem is that you cannot prove it is right, and you cannot guarantee it will produce the same answer twice.

A gross-up calculation on a $2.1 million expense pool at 73.4% occupancy with a 95% gross-up cap has exactly one correct answer. A deterministic engine produces that number every time. An AI model may produce a different number. And Cannot show its work. When a tenant's auditor asks to see the gross-up methodology, "the AI calculated it" does not survive a deposition.

The same logic applies to lease interpretation. AI can extract the clause that reads "Tenant shall pay its proportionate share of Operating Expenses, excluding capital expenditures with a useful life in excess of five years." It should not decide what that clause means when applied to $85,000 in parking garage waterproofing. That requires legal and business judgment. A confident-sounding AI answer is not the same as a legal determination, and treating it as one is a liability trap.

Three questions to ask any vendor making AI-powered claims: Does the AI perform the actual calculation, or just the extraction? Is there a human verification step before extracted data enters the calculation? Can you export the full calculation ledger. every input, every formula applied, every intermediate result?

If the answer to the first question is yes (AI performs calculations), the audit trail is broken. If the answer to the third question is no or unclear, you cannot defend the reconciliation in a dispute.

The right architecture: AI for extraction. Classification, human review before data enters the pipeline, deterministic calculation for the math, immutable audit trail for everything. Each stage uses the tool appropriate to the task. The controllers who get this right will process reconciliations faster, catch more errors, and produce cleaner statements. The ones who hand the entire workflow to an AI model will discover the problem the first time a tenant's auditor asks to see the math.
