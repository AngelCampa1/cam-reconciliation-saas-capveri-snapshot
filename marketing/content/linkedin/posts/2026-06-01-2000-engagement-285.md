A property accountant found a gross-up factor that hadn't been updated after a 2023 lease amendment. Correcting it required four steps and two approvals. The statement went out wrong instead.

One underappreciated reason CAM reconciliation errors persist: the person who finds a potential error rarely has the authority to correct it.

A property accountant doing year-end close notices that the gross-up factor doesn't match the threshold in the lease amendment from 18 months ago. To correct it, they need to: confirm their interpretation with the property manager, get sign-off to reopen the reconciliation, potentially issue corrected statements to affected tenants, and document why the original calculation was wrong.

That's three or four steps, multiple approvals, and a conversation that might get uncomfortable. The accountant's job description doesn't explicitly include initiating that process.

So the error doesn't get flagged. The statement goes out with the wrong factor. It runs for another year.

This is not a technology problem. It's a process authorization problem. The verification step is only effective if the person running it has a clear path to corrective action when something is found.

Does your team have a defined owner for that path? The person who runs the reconciliation, the person who approves corrections, and the process for getting from one to the other?
