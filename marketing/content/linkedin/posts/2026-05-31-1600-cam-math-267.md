The contrarian view on reconciliation checklists: the value is not in the 22 steps that everyone gets right. It's in the three that everyone skips under deadline pressure.

Step 11: denominator verification. Check the denominator from the signed lease against what's in the system. On a 500,000 SF building where five tenants have anchor-style exclusions, the effective denominator might be 420,000 SF. Using 500,000 overbills every tenant by 19%.

Step 14: gross-up calculation. Variable expenses only. Correct occupancy from the rent roll, not from memory. If actual occupancy is at or above the lease threshold, no gross-up applies. Any factor above 1.0 when actual >= target is an overbilling.

Step 17: cap carryforward. For cumulative cap leases, the carryforward bank must be updated after every reconciliation year. A landlord who loses the schedule cannot claim banked capacity in a high-expense year without documentation. The bank is real money. it's a contractual right to recover above the stated cap rate when prior years came in under.

Every checklist looks manageable at 25 steps. The three that matter are the ones where the math is invisible until an auditor runs it.

These three steps account for most of the dollar value in CAM disputes. Running the full checklist takes half a day. Running just these three catches the majority of the exposure.
