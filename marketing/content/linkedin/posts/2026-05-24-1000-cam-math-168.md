A lease renewal is not an administrative event in CAM. It can be a full base year reset. And If the ERP doesn't reflect that, the tenant has been overpaying from the first day of the renewal term.

The contrarian view: most base year errors don't live in the original lease setup. They live in renewals that no one updated.

The renewal option specifies what happens to the base year. Three possibilities: it resets to the renewal commencement year, it carries forward the original base, or the lease is silent. Silent is the dangerous one. it gets interpreted inconsistently and creates exposure either direction.

When the base year resets and the system doesn't know, the calculation uses a multi-year-old baseline. CAM expenses have grown since then. The tenant's obligation is calculated against a floor that's too low, so their apparent excess is too high. Every dollar of overpayment is recoverable by the tenant during the audit window.

What to do before each renewal: find the base year provision in the renewal option section. not in the original CAM section. Update the lease abstract explicitly. Then update the ERP before the first estimate calculation for the renewal term.

Two minutes of lease review at renewal prevents three years of overpayment calculation.
