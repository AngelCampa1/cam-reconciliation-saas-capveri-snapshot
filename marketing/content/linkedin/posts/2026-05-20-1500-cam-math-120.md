SLIDE 1:
The pro-rata share formula
(And the four ways landlords get the denominator wrong)

SLIDE 2:
The formula:
Tenant RSF / Total Building GLA = Pro-Rata Share %
Pro-Rata Share % × Adjusted CAM Pool = Tenant CAM Liability
Two steps. The complexity is in what goes into each number.

SLIDE 3:
Usable vs. rentable SF: the denominator uses rentable.
Rentable SF = Usable SF × Load Factor.
Example: 10,000 usable SF × 1.20 load factor = 12,000 RSF.
Rent and pro-rata share are calculated on the rentable number, not the usable.

SLIDE 4:
Denominator mistake 1: using leased area instead of leasable area.
Leasable area includes vacant suites. Leased area does not.
Using leased area shrinks the denominator at every vacancy.
A 10% vacancy forces remaining tenants to absorb costs from empty space they have nothing to do with.

SLIDE 5:
Denominator mistake 2: using a stale measurement.
A BOMA remeasurement that reduced certified GLA from 90,000 SF to 85,000 SF in 2019, with the system still using 90,000 SF. that understates every tenant's share by 5.9% for the next five years.

SLIDE 6:
Denominator mistake 3: applying one denominator to all tenants when leases differ.
Some leases say "total building area of 175,000 RSF" (fixed). Others say "as measured from time to time." These tenants have different denominators. The system must apply each tenant's lease-specified denominator, not a building-wide default.

SLIDE 7:
Denominator mistake 4: missing anchor exclusions.
A 100,000 SF center with a 40,000 SF anchor excluded from the denominator leaves 60,000 SF to absorb all CAM. An inline tenant at 6,000 SF holds 10% of the effective denominator. not 6%. The exclusion is in the lease. The system may not reflect it.

SLIDE 8:
The check before every reconciliation:
Compare the system denominator to the denominator specified in each tenant's lease.
Any difference is either an overbilling risk or an under-recovery risk.
It takes 30 minutes per property. It prevents the most common audit finding.
