Most CAM dispute calls happen within 48 hours of statement delivery. Almost all of them are about the cover letter, not the math.

A reconciliation statement that shows up with a $9,400 balance due and no explanation gets a call. The tenant isn't disputing the number yet. they just don't know what it is or why it changed from last year. That call costs your property manager 45 minutes and the tenant 45 minutes of irritation.

A cover letter that leads with the math prevents the call.

Four elements, under 400 words: the bottom-line number first (balance due or credit, due date), the primary expense drivers (specific categories and dollar amounts, not "operating costs increased"), payment instructions, and a contact name with a direct phone number.

What not to write: "We are pleased to present your annual CAM reconciliation." No one is pleased. The tenant wants to know what they owe and why.

The variance explanation is where most cover letters fail. "Insurance and maintenance increased year over year" is not an explanation. "Property insurance increased $0.31/SF ($13,200 total) due to policy renewal in March 2025 at market rates" is an explanation. Specific. Documented. Closed.

The cover letter is not a legal document. It's a customer service document that exists to reduce friction. Write it like the conversation you want to have, not like the conversation you're trying to avoid.
