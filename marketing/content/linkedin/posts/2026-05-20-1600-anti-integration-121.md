As an illustrative scenario for a 10-property portfolio:

Spreadsheet-based CAM reconciliation costs $31,500-$72,250 per year.

Purpose-built software for the same portfolio costs $12,500-$36,300 per year.

Excel does not charge per reconciliation. That is why it feels free. But the actual cost is buried in three places: labor hours nobody tracks, errors nobody catches, and audit exposure nobody prices until it arrives.

The labor math: 250 hours per year at $65 fully loaded is $16,250. That is before errors. Raymond Panko's spreadsheet error research (published via EuSpRIG) found errors in at least 86% of audited corporate spreadsheets, with formula cells being the primary failure point. In CAM reconciliation, a wrong cell reference in a pro-rata calculation costs $2,000-$15,000 per tenant. A gross-up applied to fixed expenses costs $10,000-$50,000 per property. A cap escalator not carried forward costs $1,000-$5,000 per tenant per year.

The software pays for itself when it eliminates 170-plus hours of labor and prevents one or two material errors per year. For most portfolios above five properties, that happens in year one.

The breakeven point for staying with Excel: three properties or fewer, simple NNN leases, no gross-up clauses, no cumulative cap provisions, and a stable team that will not lose the person who maintains the workbook. That is a narrow set of conditions.

Above five properties, the cost of "free" exceeds the cost of software.
