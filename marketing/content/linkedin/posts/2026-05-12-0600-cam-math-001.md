A 20-property portfolio with $500,000 walking out the door every year. Not from fraud, but from configuration drift between Yardi and the lease. It's a math problem that costs them $25,000 per property per year in uncollected revenue.

Here's what actually happens: at year-end, the landlord pulls the GL, applies each tenant's pro-rata share, adjusts for gross-up and caps, subtracts what they paid in estimates, and issues a settlement. Do it right and the true-up is modest. Do it wrong and you're either leaving money on the table or handing your tenants a dispute they'll win.

The five-step process is not complicated: collect and clean the GL, calculate the adjusted expense pool, compute pro-rata shares, apply lease-specific adjustments (gross-up, caps, exclusions), then issue statements before the contractual deadline.

What makes it hard is doing all five steps correctly, simultaneously, for every tenant in the building, using the correct lease terms for each one. Yardi or MRI will run the math. Neither one knows if the gross-up threshold in the system still matches what 47 leases actually say.

On a 20-property portfolio at $25,000 per building in average errors, that's $500,000 walking out the door every year. Not from bad intentions. From configuration drift between the system and the lease.
