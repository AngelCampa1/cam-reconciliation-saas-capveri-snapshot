A tenant who wants to dispute CAM charges needs six documents and four calculations. Most landlord responses to dispute letters address neither completely.

The six documents a tenant's advisor will request: the reconciliation statement, the GL detail for the CAM pool, the lease's operating expense definition and exclusion list, the gross-up calculation with the occupancy percentage used, the pro-rata denominator documentation, and the cap history if a cap applies.

The four calculations they will run independently: the gross-up factor (is it above 1.0 when actual occupancy cleared the threshold?), the pro-rata share (does the tenant's SF divided by the building denominator match what was billed?), the cap application (was the cap tracked correctly year by year?), and the management fee base (is the fee calculated on gross revenue, net expenses, or some other definition that the lease specifies?).

The gap between what the tenant's advisor expects to receive and what the property manager typically sends is where disputes get expensive. Sending a summary statement when detailed GL backup was requested signals that the detail doesn't exist or won't support the charges. Either way, the dispute escalates.

Property managers who respond with complete, organized documentation, all six documents, calculations shown, consistently resolve disputes faster and for less than property managers who respond defensively or incompletely.

The question "what would the tenant's advisor need to verify this charge?" should be answered before the statement is sent, not after the dispute letter arrives.
