A 30-minute GL review before the reconciliation runs is not optional. It's the cheapest QA step in the process. Here's how to structure it.

Phase 1 (0-5 minutes): Flag large transactions. Sort the GL by transaction amount and review every entry above $10,000 in the maintenance and repair accounts. A single roof replacement or HVAC system overhaul coded to operating expenses is the highest-dollar, highest-risk error in any CAM reconciliation. It's also the first thing tenant auditors check. Finding it in five minutes is exponentially cheaper than finding it after the statement goes out.

Phase 2 (5-10 minutes): Year-over-year variance by account.
Compare current year totals to prior year totals for each GL account. Flag any account that moved more than 15% without a known cause. Unexplained variances in janitorial, landscaping, or security often indicate misclassified invoices or duplicate entries, not genuine expense changes. A 55% increase in HVAC maintenance is almost always a classification problem, not a cost spike.

Phase 3 (10-15 minutes): New GL account codes. Any GL account that appears in the current year but not the prior year is a signal. It might be a legitimate new expense category. It might be an account someone created to code a capital project that didn't fit anywhere else. Either way, it needs a quick check before it goes into the CAM pool.

Phase 4 (15-20 minutes): Management fee verification.
Confirm the management fee amount against the lease cap. Pull the fee calculation base. Verify the base matches the lease definition, not the property management agreement. Verify that the fee amount doesn't exceed the lease percentage ceiling. This takes ten minutes and catches one of the three most common audit findings.

Phase 5 (20-25 minutes): Insurance and property tax cross-check.
Pull the actual insurance policy declarations page and the tax bill. Confirm the amounts match what's in the GL. Insurance and taxes are almost always passed through in full, which means any mismatch between the GL and the actual invoices creates a billing error that carries straight through to the tenant statement.

Phase 6 (25-30 minutes): Related-party transactions.
Any vendor payment to an entity with a common ownership relationship to the landlord requires documentation that the charge reflects market rate. The vendor's contract and the market rate evidence should be in the file before the reconciliation runs, not after the tenant asks.

internal analysis of CAM pools suggests roughly 3-8 contain contain misclassified transactions. On a $2 million base, that's $60,000 to $160,000 in potential dispute exposure. Thirty minutes of focused review before the reconciliation runs eliminates most of it.
