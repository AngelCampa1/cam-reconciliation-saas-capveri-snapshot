SLIDE 1:
National office vacancy hit 22.4% in Q1 2026
What that number does to every CAM reconciliation

SLIDE 2:
The gross-up mechanism
Leases allow landlords to gross up variable expenses to what the building would cost at full occupancy.
A building at 75% occupancy applies a gross-up multiplier of 1.267x to variable CAM expenses.
At 22.4% national vacancy, many buildings are operating well below gross-up thresholds.

SLIDE 3:
Where the errors enter
The gross-up calculation requires separating variable expenses from fixed ones.
Fixed expenses. property taxes, insurance, base-contract maintenance, do not vary with occupancy.
Grossing up fixed expenses is a lease violation. It happens most often when occupancy is low and the pressure to recover costs is high.

SLIDE 4:
The amplification problem
At a 1.267x multiplier, a $100,000 misclassified fixed expense becomes $126,700 in billed CAM.
The overcharge is not $100,000. It is $26,700 per year per tenant who receives the miscalculated statement.
Multiply by three years and multiple tenants.

SLIDE 5:
What auditors check in high-vacancy buildings
1. Was the gross-up multiplier above 1.0? (If actual occupancy cleared the threshold, it should be exactly 1.0)
2. Were property taxes excluded from the gross-up pool?
3. Was insurance excluded?
4. Were any fixed-contract service agreements excluded?
All four questions. Every reconciliation.

SLIDE 6:
The stale occupancy problem
Some reconciliations use prior-year occupancy data to set the gross-up factor.
In a market where vacancy moved 3-4 points in a single year, a stale factor can be materially wrong.
The lease defines when occupancy is measured. Use the date the lease specifies, not the most recent number available.

SLIDE 7:
Dollar impact at scale
Class A office, $2M CAM pool, 75% occupancy.
Gross-up multiplier on the variable pool: 1.267x.
If 15% of the pool is misclassified fixed expenses included in the gross-up base: $300,000 in fixed expenses × 0.267 overcharge = $80,100 overbilled per year.

SLIDE 8:
High vacancy means the gross-up matters more, not less.
The higher the multiplier, the larger the error on any misclassified expense.
Run the occupancy check and the fixed/variable split before the statement goes out.
