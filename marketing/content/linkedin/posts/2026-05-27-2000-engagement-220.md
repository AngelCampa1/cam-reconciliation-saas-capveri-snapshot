A property manager found a 3-year gross-up misconfiguration that cost $72,000 in under-recovery. The question wasn't how to fix it. It was how to explain it to ownership without it sounding like negligence.

The framing is usually: we found an error, the error ran in the tenant's favor, we're owed money. But the conversation with ownership becomes uncomfortable because the implication is that the team missed something for an extended period.

Worth separating two things.

The error existing is not evidence of negligence. Configuration drift is normal. Lease amendments happen without full ERP follow-through. Personnel change. Base years from anomaly periods don't get flagged. These are systemic gaps in most property management workflows, not failures by individual accountants.

Finding the error is evidence that the verification process is working. The fact that the audit found it now is better than the tenant's auditor finding it in Year 6.

The framing for ownership: "We ran an independent verification of our CAM configuration against the executed leases and found a $X underrecovery on [property]. We're issuing corrected statements. Going forward, this verification step will run annually."

That's a corrective action story, not a negligence story.
