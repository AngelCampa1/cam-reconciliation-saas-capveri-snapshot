Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"CAM overbilling is not a billing dispute. It is a breach of contract. And In California, it can cost three times the original overcharge."

Talking points:
- CAM overbilling happens when a reconciliation statement charges a tenant more than the lease permits. Intent does not matter legally. Accidental and deliberate carry the same legal exposure. - The four most common causes: gross-up applied to fixed costs like insurance. Taxes; cumulative cap bank miscalculated; a vendor invoice double-coded in AP; a capital project billed as an operating expense. - Material billing errors appear in a significant portion of CAM reconciliations, per tenant audit firm data. A meaningful share of tenants find discrepancies on their own, without hiring a professional auditor (according to JLL 2023 research via PredictAP). - In California, SB 1103. effective January 2025. authorizes treble damages for willful overbilling against qualifying small-business tenants. Microenterprises with 5 or fewer employees. Restaurants under 10. Nonprofits under 20. If a court finds the overbilling was willful, the landlord owes three times the overcharge plus attorney's fees. - Four steps before every statement goes out: GL scrub to remove non-recoverable expenses; separate fixed and variable pools before applying any gross-up; flag any expense category growing more than 5-10% year over year; run the gross-up factor, denominator, cap type, and admin fee sequence before the statement is finalized. - Catching a $15,000 overbilling internally takes minutes. Defending it in arbitration takes months.

Closing line:
"The statement is your representation that the charges are accurate. Run the checks before you make that representation."
