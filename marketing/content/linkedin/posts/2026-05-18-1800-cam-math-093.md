Twelve checks before you send anything. That's the number that separates a clean reconciliation from a dispute.

The checklist isn't theoretical. Each item maps to one of the error categories that tenant auditors find first: CapEx in the pool, gross-up on fixed costs, wrong denominator, missed exclusions, cap structure errors, admin fee sequence mistakes.

Thirty percent of all CAM disputes trace to CapEx inclusions. One roof replacement coded to the wrong GL account contaminates every tenant's statement. Check 1 is a CapEx scrub before any other calculation runs.

Check 4 (pro-rata denominator reconciliation) is the one that causes the most systemic damage when skipped. A denominator error creates a percentage variance that runs through every line item for every tenant. A 5% denominator error produces 5-10% systemic variance across the entire rent roll.

Check 8 (cap structure verification) catches the cumulative vs. non-cumulative distinction that most controllers apply from memory instead of from the lease clause. The lease controls. Memory drifts.

Check 12 (statement delivery deadline verification) is the only one that can forfeit the landlord's right to collect. Miss the contractual billing window and in some leases the entire true-up balance is permanently uncollectable.

A reconciliation that passes all 12 checks is still auditable. Tenants with audit rights will exercise them. But the auditor who arrives after a clean reconciliation finds a complete documentation package rather than gaps that inflate the apparent error rate.

Run the checklist. Every time.
