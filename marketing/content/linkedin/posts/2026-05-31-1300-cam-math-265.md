The contrarian view on reconciliation timelines: "90 to 120 days after year-end" is not a planning window. It's a deadline. Planning backwards from April 30 with three-week steps leaves no buffer for anything.

Here's what the buffer is for: the December invoice that posts in late January. The GL close that slips by five business days. The calculation error found on the fourth of five properties you're reviewing simultaneously. The tenant who sends a question three days before the deadline.

Working backward from April 30 with two weeks of deliberate buffer:
April 1: statements delivered. Delivery date, not drafting date.
March 15: statements drafted and reviewed, ready to send.
February 15: pool calculations complete and QC-checked.
January 15: GL export complete and GL review done.

The December 31 GL export is available by January 5 in most organizations. Starting January 15 is achievable. Starting mid-February because "reconciliation season" begins in Q1 is not a plan. it's a default.

The lease's reconciliation clause determines the deadline. The industry convention that statements go out in March or April doesn't extend a lease that says 90 days. And the "deemed correct" provisions in retail leases don't protect late statements. they protect statements delivered within the window.
