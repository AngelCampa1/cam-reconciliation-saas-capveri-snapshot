"CRE FinOps" is a term we use deliberately at CapVeri. Here's what it means in practice.

FinOps, in its original context, is about making financial operations visible, accurate, and accountable at the line-item level. In cloud infrastructure, that means knowing exactly what you're spending and why.

In commercial real estate, the equivalent problem is operating expense recovery. Landlords have contractual rights to recover specific costs from tenants. But the gap between what's owed and what's actually collected runs up to 3.5% by property type, based on industry estimates from tenant audit firm published findings and operating-expense benchmarks.

That gap isn't from fraud. It's from the same problems FinOps was built to address: configuration drift, lack of independent verification, and a system that runs silently even when the inputs are wrong.

CRE FinOps is the discipline of making lease-based financial recoveries as visible and auditable as a line-item invoice. Every charge should be traceable to the lease clause and the GL entry that supports it. Any deviation from the lease should surface before statements go out, not after a tenant audit.

That's what we're building. Not a better spreadsheet. A verification layer for the financial operations side of commercial real estate.
