SLIDE 1:
AI or deterministic calculation for CAM reconciliation?
The answer changes when a tenant hires an auditor.

SLIDE 2:
The court test
When a tenant's attorney requests documentation for a $47,000 CAM charge, the question is not just "is the math right?"
It's "can you prove it, step by step, three years from now?"
Probabilistic systems cannot pass that test.

SLIDE 3:
What "deterministic" means
Same inputs. Same output. Every time.
Not approximately the same. Identical.
A CPA takes your inputs three years later and arrives at the same number.
That is the standard financial documentation requires.

SLIDE 4:
The audit trail problem with AI
AI language models generate output by sampling token probabilities.
Two calls with identical inputs can produce different results.
There is no step-by-step ledger showing how each dollar was allocated.
Just a plausible-sounding answer.

SLIDE 5:
The five dimensions
Accuracy: Deterministic = exact. AI = approximate.
Reproducibility: Deterministic = identical re-runs guaranteed. AI = output may vary.
Audit trail: Deterministic = full step-by-step ledger. AI = black-box reasoning.
Court defensibility: Deterministic = yes. AI = high risk.
Edge case handling: Deterministic = explicit business rules. AI = may hallucinate precedent.

SLIDE 6:
Where AI belongs in this workflow
Document extraction: reading a 200-page lease, identifying CAM clauses, classifying GL codes.
AI cuts this from hours to minutes.
That is where AI earns its place.
Not in the calculation.

SLIDE 7:
The right division of labor
Extract: AI reads the lease and identifies provisions.
Verify: a human confirms the extracted values.
Calculate: deterministic engine runs the math, identically every time.
Audit: full calculation ledger stored and retrievable.

SLIDE 8:
For CAM math, "probably right" is not good enough.
The same inputs must always produce the same output.
