Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"Based on practitioner reporting from property accounting teams, a standard CAM reconciliation takes 8 to 15 analyst hours per building. Most property accounting teams have 200 available hours per analyst per reconciliation season. Do the math before February."

Talking points:
- Based on practitioner reporting from property accounting teams, the 8-15 hour range per building depends on complexity: number of leases, number of expense categories, whether the gross-up requires detailed occupancy documentation,. Whether any leases have unusual provisions. Simple triple-net industrial with one tenant is closer to 8 hours. Complex multi-tenant office with layered cap structures is closer to 15. - A 200-hour available season per analyst is a realistic estimate after accounting for regular operations, tenant questions, year-end close tasks, and the actual business days between January and April. Some operations get less than this. - At 10 hours average per building, a single analyst can handle roughly 20 properties per season. Many property accounting teams are under-resourced relative to their portfolio size. The backlog shows up in March when statements are due and the queue is long. - Technology as a capacity multiplier: the computational steps in a CAM reconciliation. gross-up calculation, pro-rata share, cap application. can be automated. What cannot be automated is reading the lease, making judgment calls on contested categories, and signing off on the final statement. Separating the computational work from the judgment work allows an analyst to handle more properties in the same time. - The staffing math should be done in January, not March. Knowing that 40 properties need to be reconciled, that each takes an average of 11 hours, and that the team has 600 combined hours available means the plan is to start X date, prioritize the most complex properties first, and use specific tools or resources to handle the rest. - The cost of missing the reconciliation deadline is not just operational. Most commercial leases void the landlord's right to collect a true-up if the statement is not delivered within the required window. typically 90-120 days after year-end.

Closing line:
"Reconciliation season capacity planning is not optional. Start the math in January, before the work hits the queue."
