A framework for auditing your own CAM reconciliation before statements go out.

Most portfolios have at least one of these five problems active right now. This is the checklist we'd run if we were reviewing your GL today.

1. Gross-up factor check
Compare target occupancy to actual occupancy for the reconciliation period. If actual >= target, the gross-up factor should be exactly 1.0. Any factor above 1.0 in that scenario is billing past 100% occupancy, which is not a valid calculation.

2. Fixed-cost exclusion audit
Pull every line item in the CAM pool. Identify which ones are fixed costs: property taxes, insurance, fixed-contract security, capital reserves. Fixed costs should not be in the gross-up calculation. If they are, gross-up is inflating items that don't scale with occupancy.

3. Cap type verification
Each lease specifies a cap type: cumulative, non-cumulative, or a specific percentage. Pull the cap type for every tenant. Compare to what the ERP has configured. A cumulative cap misread as non-cumulative will undercharge or overcharge year after year.

4. Base-year anomaly flag
If the lease's base year was 2020 or 2021 (occupancy disruption years), the base-year expenses may be artificially low. A low base year means every subsequent year looks like an increase, even when actual costs normalized. This is a common source of tenant disputes.

5. Pro-rata denominator consistency
Check whether the denominator used in the pro-rata calculation matches the current lease exhibit. Remeasurements, new leases on the floor, and BOMA methodology changes all affect the denominator. A denominator that's two lease years stale will allocate the wrong share to every tenant on the floor.

These five checks don't require a new system. They require someone to actually look at the numbers against the lease language.

What usually stops teams from running them: time, and the fact that the ERP doesn't flag mismatches, it just runs whatever it was configured with.
