Industry estimates derived from tenant audit firm published findings and operating-expense benchmarks put CAM recovery leakage at up to 3.5% of the total operating expense recovery pool, varying by property type.

That's a number worth sitting with.

On a portfolio with $1 million in annual CAM billings, 3-5% is $30,000 to $50,000 in recoveries that were contractually owed but not collected. On a $5 million portfolio, it's $150,000 to $250,000. These aren't hypothetical numbers. They represent real contractual rights that expired uncollected.

The source of the leakage, according to industry research and the dispute patterns we've seen, is not bad math. It's the gap between what the lease requires and what the ERP is configured to calculate. Here's what that looks like at the line-item level:

Gross-up errors are the most common category. The factor is applied when occupancy already exceeded the threshold, adding charges that shouldn't exist. Or the factor is applied to fixed costs (insurance, property taxes) that should be excluded from the gross-up pool entirely.

Cap errors are the second most common. A cumulative cap is configured as non-cumulative, or the cap base is set from an anomaly year. The difference between cumulative and non-cumulative compounding is substantial over a five-year lease term.

Exclusion drift is the third category. A lease exhibit specifies that certain costs are excluded from the CAM pool: management fees above a threshold, capital expenditures, specific maintenance contracts. The ERP includes everything unless explicitly configured to exclude. Exclusions set up in Year 1 don't always survive system migrations, accounting staff turnover, or portfolio acquisitions.

Pro-rata denominator errors are the least visible but often the largest per-tenant. If the denominator used in the allocation calculation doesn't match the current lease exhibit (remeasurement, BOMA methodology change, new tenants on the floor), every tenant on that floor is receiving an incorrect allocation.

The common thread across all four categories: the ERP ran correctly. The configuration was wrong. And the only way to catch the configuration being wrong is to compare it to the lease before the statement goes out.
