Illustrative scenario: a 12-property BOMA 2024 transition.

The asset management team had planned the transition for eighteen months. The rollout covered a 12-property office portfolio in two markets, with dual measurements, lease review, tenant notices, and reconciliation model updates.

They started with four properties in Q1 2025. The plan was to measure under both BOMA 2017 and BOMA 2024, then have legal identify which leases referenced current BOMA standards and which locked the denominator to a specific measurement standard.

The dual measurement phase ran two months longer than planned because the measurement firm's BOMA 2024 treatment of outdoor amenity areas had to be verified.

The lease review produced a predictable split: some leases permitted current standards, some locked the prior standard, and a meaningful group was ambiguous.

For ambiguous leases, the landlord kept existing denominators rather than invite a dispute, then flagged those leases for renewal cleanup.

The first property using BOMA 2024 denominators showed a 4.2% denominator increase after rooftop terrace and courtyard areas qualified as rentable SF. The expense pool also increased because some amenity costs moved into recoverable CAM.

The operational lesson: tenant notification and per-tenant impact calculations take the most time. Start lease review and dual measurement together, not sequentially.
