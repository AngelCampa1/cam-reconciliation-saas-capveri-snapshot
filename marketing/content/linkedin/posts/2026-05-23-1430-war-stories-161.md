The significant growth in tenant audit firms since 2020 is not a story about adversarial tenants.

Audit firms are growing because their business model works. Contingency fees typically in the 25-40% range of recoveries, a majority finding rate across engagements, and technology that reduced the cost of doing an audit. these are the conditions that sustain a professional services market. The tenants using them are not unusual. They are responding rationally to an information asymmetry that has always existed.

The asymmetry: the landlord controls the GL, the reconciliation calculation, and the documentation. The tenant receives a summary statement and a request for payment. An audit is how the tenant closes that information gap.

The common framing of tenant audits as adversarial misses what actually drives them. Most audit engagements are not initiated because a tenant is suspicious of their landlord. They are initiated because ASC 842 made CPAs look at CAM statements, because a lease is expiring and the tenant is doing a full cost review, or because another tenant in the building mentioned they had found errors.

What the growth of audit firms actually signals is that the gap between what property managers know about their CAM calculations and what tenants can verify has become a market. Audit firms close that gap on contingency.

The property manager who closes that gap first. by running the same checks internally. removes the financial incentive for the audit before it starts.
