"Our reconciliation process is slow. We need to hire another person."

This assumes the problem is capacity. It is usually not.

For a typical 15-property portfolio, 65-80% of reconciliation time goes to three categories that have nothing to do with headcount: waiting for GL close and data gathering, manual ERP-versus-lease verification, and rework from errors discovered late in the process.

A single gross-up error found after drafting ten of fifteen tenant statements requires reworking every tenant in that pool. The time cost of a validation pass before drafting: 30-60 minutes. The time cost of discovering the error after drafting: 3-5 hours of rework. Another controller does not change this arithmetic.

Industry benchmark is 90-120 days post year-end. Teams that miss it face more than delayed cash: expenses older than 24-36 months become permanently uncollectable in most states. California SB 1103 sets an 18-month hard limit from the date of the tenant's written request. A statement sent 30 months after year-end may be legally valid under the lease. Economically unenforceable for the oldest expenses it contains.

A $100,000 true-up becomes worth substantially less if it arrives 28 months after year-end and the tenant's attorney can point to the statutory window.

The solution is not more hours in the current process. It is removing the waiting, the manual re-verification, and the late-discovered rework that make the current process slow. Those three changes compress the timeline without adding headcount.
