A tenant vacates on September 30. You have two options for handling their CAM reconciliation, and the wrong choice creates a billing dispute before the ink is dry on the termination agreement.

Here's the framework for handling mid-year move-outs cleanly.

Option A: Immediate reconciliation at move-out.
Calculate the tenant's CAM share through the vacate date, compare to estimates paid, and settle at move-out. The advantage: clean financial separation, no year-end true-up required for this tenant. The disadvantage: you're calculating from incomplete data. Year-end accruals, December invoices, and fourth-quarter property taxes haven't been posted yet. An immediate reconciliation uses estimated or annualized expenses, not final actuals.

Option B: Year-end reconciliation with partial-year proration.
Include the departed tenant in the year-end reconciliation. Calculate their full-year proportional share using their days in occupancy (January 1 through September 30 = 273 days / 365 = 74.8%), apply that fraction to the final annual pool, subtract estimates paid, and settle. The advantage: you're using final actual expenses. The disadvantage: the tenant may be gone for three months before they receive the statement.

The lease determines which option applies. Many leases specify immediate reconciliation at move-out. Others default to year-end. Read the termination provision, not the general reconciliation clause.

The complication that catches most property managers: gross-up activation. When the tenant vacated on September 30, the building's occupancy dropped. If the fourth quarter occupancy falls below the gross-up threshold because of the departure, variable expenses in Q4 must be grossed up. The departed tenant doesn't owe that gross-up amount for Q4 (they were gone). The remaining tenants absorb it. Track the gross-up trigger date and which tenants were present.

The security deposit settlement is a separate calculation. Do not net the CAM true-up against the security deposit return unless the lease explicitly permits it. Two separate settlements, two separate ledgers, applied in the sequence the lease specifies.

Document the vacancy period by suite. The occupancy records for the building during the partial-year period are what a tenant auditor will request if the gross-up calculation is disputed.
