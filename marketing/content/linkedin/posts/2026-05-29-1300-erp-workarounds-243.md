MRI's GL export for CAM reconciliation requires four specific reports to capture the full picture. Running the wrong report, or the right report with the wrong parameters. produces data that looks complete and isn't.

The four: General Ledger Detail (transaction-level expense data), Recovery Billing Report (what MRI billed to tenants based on its configuration), Rent Roll (current occupancy and lease data), and the Trial Balance (period summary for cross-referencing).

Five failure modes that produce bad source data:

Entity filter: MRI organizes data by entity. Running the GL export for the wrong entity, or forgetting to include a sub-entity that holds specific expense categories, creates a gap in the expense pool that looks like clean data.

Period 13 adjustments: MRI allows a 13th accounting period for year-end adjustments. These entries don't appear in the standard 12-period export unless explicitly included. Year-end accruals, corrections, and allocations booked to period 13 are missing from the reconciliation base.

Intercompany allocations: management fees and shared service costs charged from a parent entity to individual properties may not appear in the property-level GL export without an additional step.

Accrual basis vs. cash basis mismatch: if some entries are on accrual and some on cash, the export captures different date logic for each. The total can be correct on an annual basis but wrong on a period basis.

Prior-year adjustments posted to the current period: corrections to prior-year expenses that affect current-period recovery pool calculations won't be flagged as such in a standard export.

The MRI export procedure is well-documented. The failure modes are not.
