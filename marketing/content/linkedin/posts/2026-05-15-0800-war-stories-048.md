A tenant dispute letter arrives. The tenant believes they were overbilled. They want six documents within 30 days. What happens next depends almost entirely on how the property manager responds in the first 30 days.

Here is the step-by-step pattern that determines whether a dispute resolves in weeks or drags for months.

Step 1: Acknowledge within 72 hours. Not a resolution. an acknowledgment. Confirm receipt, identify who on the property management side owns the response, and give a realistic timeline. Tenants who receive no response in the first week escalate. Escalation brings in attorneys. Attorneys change the economics of the dispute.

Step 2: Pull the six documents before responding substantively. The standard dispute package is: the reconciliation statement, the GL detail for the CAM pool, the lease's operating expense definition, the gross-up calculation, the pro-rata denominator documentation, and the cap history if a cap applies. Collect all six before engaging on the merits. Partial responses invite more requests.

Step 3: Run the four calculations independently. Before responding to any specific allegation, run the gross-up, the pro-rata share, the cap application, and the management fee calculation from scratch. Not from the statement. from the raw inputs. If internal review finds an error before the tenant formalizes a claim, a proactive credit is far cheaper than a contested refund.

Step 4: Categorize the tenant's claims. Sort what the tenant is alleging into three groups: clearly incorrect (the tenant misread the lease), contested (both interpretations are reasonable), and confirmed (the charge was wrong). Respond to each group differently. Do not fight confirmed errors. Fighting them extends the dispute and damages the relationship with the tenant.

Step 5: Provide a written response to each allegation. Cite the lease clause. Attach the supporting calculation. If a charge is contested, explain the interpretation and offer to discuss. Generic responses that say "our charges are correct" without documentation prolong disputes by weeks.

Step 6: Resolve confirmed errors quickly. Issue a credit or refund on confirmed errors within 30 days of identification. Tenants who receive prompt resolution on acknowledged errors are far less likely to pursue remaining contested items aggressively.

The disputes that become expensive are the ones where step one was missed and step three was skipped.
