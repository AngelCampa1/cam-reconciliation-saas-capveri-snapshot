IREM operating expense benchmarks give property managers a reference point that most of them underuse.

The ranges by property type: Class A urban office, $12-18 per square foot. Class B suburban office, $7-12 per square foot. Industrial, $0.50-$1.25 per square foot.

These are not targets. They are the ranges within which comparable properties operate. A building outside these ranges. above or below. is an outlier. Outliers need an explanation.

Above-range expenses: either the building has genuinely higher costs due to location, age, or services, or expenses are being included in the CAM pool that shouldn't be. The benchmark doesn't tell you which. The lease does.

Below-range expenses: either the building is unusually efficient, or recoverable expenses are being excluded from the pool. The latter is CAM leakage. expenses the lease allows to be billed that aren't being billed.

The practical use of IREM benchmarks in reconciliation is a two-step process. First, compare total CAM per square foot to the benchmark for the building's class and type. Second, for any line item that looks anomalous. property taxes, insurance, maintenance, compare that category specifically to known ranges for the market.

Line items that look fine in the total can be outliers at the category level. A building within the IREM range overall can still have a property tax line that's 40% above comparable buildings, which will generate a tenant question during reconciliation season.

The benchmark is a diagnostic tool, not a defense. Use it before the statement is delivered.
