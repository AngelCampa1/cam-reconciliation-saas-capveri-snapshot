The building's asset manager had been pushing to adopt BOMA 2024 for two years. A 14,000 SF rooftop terrace on a Class A office building downtown. fully improved, maintained, and accessible to all tenants. was sitting completely outside the building's certified RSF under the 2017 standard. Every tenant's pro-rata share was being calculated against a 210,000 SF denominator that ignored a space tenants genuinely used.

When the re-measurement came back under BOMA 2024, the certified RSF was 223,800 SF. The terrace, plus a ground-level courtyard that also qualified, added 13,800 SF to the denominator.

The billing implications were more complex than expected.

For tenants with renewal options that referenced "BOMA standards as amended," the new denominator applied on renewal. Their pro-rata shares dropped. 10,000 SF in a 223,800 SF building is 4.47% versus 4.76% in the old denominator. On a $2.1M expense pool, the per-tenant annual difference ranged from $2,400 to $6,100 depending on suite size.

For tenants with leases that said "measured in accordance with BOMA standards as of the lease commencement date," the old denominator still applied. These leases required a formal amendment to switch.

The building ended up with three measurement cohorts: original-standard tenants (still on 210,000 SF), renewal tenants who had signed under BOMA 2024 language (using 223,800 SF), and two new tenants who had never been on the old standard.

Running three denominators in the same building is manageable, but it requires explicit documentation in every tenant's reconciliation statement showing which denominator was applied and why. The alternative. applying a single building-wide denominator, would have violated at least half the leases.

What made this case instructive: the maintenance cost for the terrace and courtyard also had to be decided. These spaces were now in the rentable area. Their upkeep costs. cleaning, landscaping, furniture maintenance, lighting, were legitimate operating expenses. Adding them to the pool partially offset the per-tenant reduction from the larger denominator.

The net effect on any single tenant depended on whether their suite had been remeasured under BOMA 2024 (which some had, for the terrace-adjacent floors), how large their suite was, and whether they held BOMA-2017-locked or BOMA-as-amended language.

BOMA 2024's outdoor measurement framework is not a checkbox. It requires lease-by-lease analysis, explicit denominator documentation per tenant, and a clear policy on when the new standard applies. Buildings that apply it inconsistently across a mixed-vintage lease book create exactly the kind of structural mismatch that generates multi-year audit exposure.
