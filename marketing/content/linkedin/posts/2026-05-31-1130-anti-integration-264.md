SLIDE 1:
Late CAM reconciliation has a known cost: delayed cash collection.
It has several hidden costs most property teams don't account for.
Here are the four.

SLIDE 2:
Hidden cost 1: Extended tenant audit window
Commercial leases typically give tenants 12-36 months from statement delivery to request an audit.
A statement delivered in October instead of April gives tenant auditors 6 additional months to find errors.
Late delivery does not reduce exposure. it extends it.

SLIDE 3:
Hidden cost 2: Statutory forfeiture risk
California SB 1103 creates an 18-month hard deadline for delivering reconciliation statements to qualified commercial tenants.
Failure to deliver forfeits the right to collect that year's CAM reconciliation.
Other states have similar provisions in standard lease forms.
The right to collect can be lost entirely. not delayed, lost.

SLIDE 4:
Hidden cost 3: Estimate-becomes-actual provisions
Many leases include a provision: if the reconciliation statement is not delivered within a specified period (commonly 180 days or 12 months), the prior year's estimate becomes the final amount.
The landlord cannot bill more than the estimate, regardless of actual expenses.
If actual expenses exceeded estimates. which they typically do. the shortfall is permanently unrecoverable.

SLIDE 5:
Hidden cost 4: The uncollectable threshold
Holland & Knight has documented that tenant audit claims older than 24-36 months become significantly harder to collect even when the landlord prevails.
Tenants dispute the amount, request additional documentation, and delay payment.
After 36 months, the practical collectability of any disputed reconciliation amount drops materially.
Late delivery extends the gap between expense incurrence and cash collection. And Increases the probability that disputed amounts become uncollectable.

SLIDE 6:
What drives late reconciliation
Most CAM reconciliation delays trace to one of four causes: incomplete GL data at year-end close, manual verification bottlenecks, spreadsheet errors discovered mid-process requiring rework, or sequential review processes that could run in parallel.
The delay is not usually a staffing problem. It is a process design problem.

SLIDE 7:
The compounding effect
Late delivery + extended audit window + estimate-becomes-actual provisions + uncollectable risk = a financial exposure profile that is larger than the face value of the reconciliation.
Each delay increases the probability that a portion of legitimate recovery is permanently lost.

SLIDE 8:
The hidden costs of late reconciliation exceed the visible cost.
The visible cost is delayed collection.
The hidden costs include forfeiture, uncollectable disputes, and extended audit exposure.
