SLIDE 1:
Excel doesn't charge per reconciliation.
That makes it feel free.
Here is what it actually costs.

SLIDE 2:
The labor cost that doesn't appear on any budget
A property controller running reconciliation for 10 NNN properties spends 250 hours per year on the process.
At $65/hour fully loaded, that is $16,250 per year for 10 properties.
At 50 properties, hours per property increase because cross-portfolio consistency checks and cap tracking add overhead: 1,750 hours, $113,750 per year.
These hours fall in Q1, competing with every other year-end deadline.

SLIDE 3:
The error cost that arrives without warning
Spreadsheets have no validation layer.
Wrong cell reference in pro-rata calculation: $2,000-$15,000 per tenant.
Gross-up applied to fixed expenses: $10,000-$50,000 per property.
Cap escalator not carried forward: $1,000-$5,000 per tenant per year.
None of these announce themselves. Each produces the wrong answer in the format of the right answer.

SLIDE 4:
The audit cost that multiplies the error
Tenant audit firms find material discrepancies in the majority of reconciliation statements they review.
They work on contingency and charge contingency fees in the 20-40% range of recovered amounts.
A $15,000 error discovered by a tenant auditor generates a $3,000-$6,000 contingency payment, plus the credit or refund, plus the dispute management time.
The same error found by the controller before the statement goes out costs an hour to correct.

SLIDE 5:
The total cost comparison for 10 properties
Excel: $16,250 labor + $3,250 QA + $8,000-$30,000 in error costs + $4,000-$8,000 per audit event = $31,500-$72,250 per year estimated total.
CAM software: $5,000-$25,000 software + $5,200 labor + $1,300 QA + $1,000-$5,000 in error costs = $12,500-$36,300 per year estimated total.
Software pays for itself when it eliminates 170+ hours of labor and prevents one or two material errors. For most portfolios above 5 properties, that happens in year one.

SLIDE 6:
When to stay with Excel
1-3 properties with simple NNN leases.
No gross-up clauses, no cumulative cap provisions in any lease.
The controller who built the spreadsheet is not planning to leave.
A solid peer review process for every calculation.
Below this threshold, the risk is real but manageable.

SLIDE 7:
When to move
4+ properties, or any gross-up or cumulative cap provision in any lease.
Any tenant audit finding in the last three years.
Reconciliation consuming more than 100 hours per year.
Key-person turnover during reconciliation season.
The gray zone is 3-5 properties. that's where the decision is closest.

SLIDE 8:
The question isn't whether Excel is free.
The question is whether its hidden costs exceed the cost of purpose-built software.
Above 5 properties, they almost always do.
