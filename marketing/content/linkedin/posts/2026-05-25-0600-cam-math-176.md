The contrarian take on mid-year move-out reconciliations: the dispute risk doesn't come from the pro-rata calculation. It comes from the timing.

Most landlords issue the departing tenant's final reconciliation at year-end. By then, the tenant has been gone for six months. Their records are filed. Their key contacts have moved on. And if their security deposit was applied at vacancy, the landlord's collection position is weaker.

The pro-rata math is simple: full-year actual CAM obligation multiplied by days in occupancy divided by 365. For a March 31 vacate, that's 90 days of exposure.

The complication is what the departure does to the rest of your pool. If your denominator is occupancy-based, it shrinks when the tenant leaves. Every remaining tenant's pro-rata share increases. If your leases include a gross-up provision, the vacancy can trigger a gross-up calculation for the post-vacancy period.

The landlord who issues the departing tenant's statement at year-end often discovers that the gross-up triggered by the vacancy increased the remaining tenants' bills. And Those remaining tenants now have questions about a 4% mid-year increase that nobody communicated.

Best practice: check each lease for the final reconciliation deadline. If the lease requires a statement within 90 days of vacate. which many do. issue it while records are fresh. Close the relationship cleanly before the year-end cycle.
