SLIDE 1:
The property controller's career is changing.
From back-office function to CRE FinOps.

SLIDE 2:
What's driving the shift
Operating expenses are a larger share of NOI. The controllable portion of NOI increasingly depends on recovery optimization.
Technology is automating the transactional work. Analytical judgment is what's left.
Institutional owners demand financial operations data beyond standard statements: recovery ratios, dispute rates, reconciliation cycle times.

SLIDE 3:
The career ladder
Based on the IREM Real Estate Management Salary Survey and the Robert Half Real Estate & Property salary guides as reference points, approximate U.S. market ranges by level:
Property Accountant (0-3 yrs): transaction processing, GL coding, basic reconciliation support. $55K-$75K
Senior Property Accountant (3-5 yrs): independent reconciliation, variance analysis, audit response. $70K-$90K
Property Controller (5-8 yrs): portfolio reconciliation management, QA review, process improvement. $85K-$120K

SLIDE 4:
Director to VP track
Senior Controller / Director of Financial Operations (8-12 yrs): portfolio-wide strategy, ownership reporting, acquisition support. $110K-$160K
VP Property Financial Operations / CFO (12+ yrs): enterprise oversight, investor reporting, strategic planning. $150K-$250K+

SLIDE 5:
The skill gaps that separate accountants from strategic controllers
Gap 1: Lease financial analysis. Understanding how a 5% cumulative cap compounds over 10 years, not just running the formula.
Gap 2: NOI translation. "$45,000 in recovered expenses" = "$750,000 in property value at a 6% cap rate." Speaking ownership's language.
Gap 3: Process design. Individual skill doesn't scale. 25 buildings requires workflows, not just competence.
Gap 4: Audit defense. Evaluating findings letters, preparing responses, negotiating settlements.

SLIDE 6:
What to build
Learn the math deeply, not just the formula.
Study lease drafting. Understand why provisions are structured the way they are.
Build reporting dashboards: recovery ratios, cycle times, dispute rates. Present quarterly.
Get involved in acquisitions: review CAM data during due diligence, quantify recovery gaps.

SLIDE 7:
The market opportunity
$20.7 trillion in U.S. commercial real estate assets.
Every property needs someone who can calculate, validate, and optimize expense recovery.
The supply of controllers with deep CAM expertise is small.
Genuine expertise in reconciliation and recovery optimization is a seller's market.

SLIDE 8:
The controller who shows the asset manager that fixing gross-up errors added $180K to NOI isn't doing accounting.
They're doing financial operations. The career ceiling is different.
