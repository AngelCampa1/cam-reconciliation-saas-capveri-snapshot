Using conservative portfolio modeling assumptions, the estimated annual ROI from an independent CAM verification layer: $573,250.

That number breaks down as: $475,000 in additional legitimate recovery from correcting systematic billing gaps, $75,600 in avoided tenant auditor payouts, and $22,650 in controller time reallocation (350 hours at $64.75/hour fully loaded).

The 20-50 building range is the hardest segment to serve with existing tools. Below 20 buildings, spreadsheets are manageable with disciplined process controls. Above 50, enterprise platforms are cost-justified. In the middle, the portfolio is too large for manual reconciliation at acceptable error rates but too small to absorb a $500K+ ERP replacement or custom integration build.

The five failure points specific to this segment: too many buildings for one controller to manage but not enough budget for a dedicated reconciliation team, lease diversity that exceeds what a single spreadsheet template can handle, Q1 crunch that competes with annual close and budget preparation simultaneously, tenant auditor claims that arrive faster than the team can respond to them, and key-person dependency on whoever maintains the master spreadsheet.

The 90-day path to reducing that exposure: 30 days to configure one property end-to-end (upload GL export, enter lease terms, complete initial comparison), 30 days to identify and resolve configuration discrepancies across properties 2-5, 30 days to generate verified statements for all properties in the portfolio.

No ERP replacement. No API integration. No IT project. The source data is the GL export your controllers already produce.
