40% of commercial leases have material CAM billing errors. That statistic comes from tenant audit firms, which have a financial incentive to find errors, so discount it if you want. The 28% figure from tenants who find errors on their own, without hiring an auditor, is harder to dismiss.

What does a tenant auditor actually do when they take an engagement?

They abstract the lease. That means reading the operating expense definition, the gross-up clause, the cap structure, the exclusion list, and the pro-rata formula. On average, manual lease abstraction takes 4 hours per lease. Auditors do this before they look at a single GL entry.

They request the GL detail for the CAM pool. Not the summary. The full account-level detail, usually for the last 3 audit years the lease permits.

They run their own calculation: not a review of the landlord's math, but an independent calculation from the raw inputs. The delta between the landlord's number and the auditor's number is the preliminary finding.

Then they check the seven standard items: CapEx coded as OpEx, gross-up on fixed expenses, management fee base, expenses outside the lease definition, pro-rata denominator, cap violations, and base year anomalies.

The majority finding rate in tenant audits is not because property managers are systematically dishonest. It is because most CAM reconciliation processes were built to produce statements, not to verify them. The auditor's process is designed to find what the statement-production process misses.

Running the same seven checks internally is what changes that finding rate.
