"Build vs. buy" in CAM reconciliation is usually framed as: should we build a better spreadsheet or buy software?

That's the wrong frame.

The real comparison is: what does the total cost of ownership look like for each option, including the costs most teams never track?

For a 10-property portfolio, spreadsheet reconciliation costs roughly $16,250 per year in labor (250 hours at $65/hour). That's before errors. Raymond Panko's spreadsheet error research (published via EuSpRIG) found errors in at least 86% of audited corporate spreadsheets, with formula cells as the primary failure point. In CAM reconciliation, a wrong cell reference in a pro-rata calculation costs $2,000-$15,000 per tenant. A gross-up applied to fixed expenses costs $10,000-$50,000 per property. A cap escalator not carried forward costs $1,000-$5,000 per tenant per year.

Purpose-built CAM software for the same portfolio costs $5,000-$25,000 per year in licensing, with 80 hours of labor instead of 250. Error-related costs drop to $1,000-$5,000 per year versus $8,000-$30,000 for spreadsheets.

Total estimated annual cost for 10 properties: spreadsheets $31,500-$72,250 versus software $12,500-$36,300.

The software pays for itself when it eliminates 170-plus hours of labor and prevents one or two material errors per year. For most portfolios above five properties, that happens in year one.

The breakeven point for staying with Excel: three properties or fewer, with simple NNN leases, no gross-up clauses, no cumulative cap provisions, and a stable team that won't lose the person who maintains the workbook. That's a narrow set of conditions.

Above five properties, the cost of "free" exceeds the cost of software.
