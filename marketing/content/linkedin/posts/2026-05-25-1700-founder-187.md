From Angel Campa, CapVeri founder:

"Building CapVeri as a solo, bootstrapped operation was a deliberate choice, not a constraint.

The /about page explains this directly. CapVeri is not a private-equity rollup or a venture-backed startup with millions to burn. That's stated because it's relevant to how we operate and what we prioritize.

Here's the practical implication for customers.

When a property accountant files a bug report, I read it. When a customer has a question about whether a specific gross-up configuration is being handled correctly, I look at it directly. When a feature request comes in, I evaluate whether it's genuinely valuable or just feature bloat.

This isn't a virtue signal. It's a description of how the product development cycle actually works. There's no ticket queue where customer feedback waits 90 days for prioritization. There's no enterprise sales team incentivized to promise features that the product team then has to build under pressure.

What there is: a single focus on whether the reconciliation calculation is correct and whether the output is useful for property accountants and landlords who need to do their job accurately.

The tradeoff is obvious. A solo operation has bandwidth limits. We're not going to win on the breadth of integrations or the depth of the enterprise feature set. We're going to win on accuracy, on the quality of the reconciliation output, and on the speed of response when something is wrong.

For a financial tool that's being used to support CAM billing decisions with legal and contractual implications, that tradeoff seems like the right one.

If you're evaluating CapVeri and the bootstrapped structure is a concern, that's fair. Reputation matters in CRE. The /about page says so explicitly. But if the structure means anything for you as a customer, it means direct access to the person responsible for the product."
