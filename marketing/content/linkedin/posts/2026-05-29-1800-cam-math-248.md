SLIDE 1:
The CAM true-up statement
10 components. Three generate most disputes.

SLIDE 2:
The formula
True-up amount = actual CAM obligation minus total estimated payments made.
Positive means the tenant owes more. Negative means the landlord owes a credit.

SLIDE 3:
The 10-component statement
1. Total recoverable expense pool
2. Excluded items and basis
3. Gross-up calculation, if applied
4. Adjusted pool after gross-up
5. Tenant denominator and pro-rata share
6. Tenant annual obligation
7. Estimates paid
8. Cap calculation, if applicable
9. Prior credits or adjustments
10. Final balance due or credit owed

SLIDE 4:
Error 1: wrong denominator
A correct pool with the wrong denominator still produces the wrong pro-rata share. If the denominator is 5% too high, the tenant is underbilled.

SLIDE 5:
Error 2: gross-up applied to fixed expenses
Property taxes and insurance do not vary with occupancy. Grossing them up creates phantom expense recovery.

SLIDE 6:
Error 3: cap not applied
A capped tenant's statement should show that the cap was tested. If not, tenants will ask whether the cap was waived or missed.

SLIDE 7:
Delivery window
Many leases require the true-up within 90-180 days after year-end. Missing the deadline can affect collection rights.

SLIDE 8:
Documentation package
Statement, GL backup, cap worksheet, gross-up worksheet, and denominator support.
