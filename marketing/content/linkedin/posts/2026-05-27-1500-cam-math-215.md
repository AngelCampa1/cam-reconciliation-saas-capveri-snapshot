SLIDE 1:
The anchor exclusion
What it does to smaller tenants, and why controllers need to track it carefully.

SLIDE 2:
How it works
When an anchor tenant's lease removes its square footage from the CAM denominator, the denominator shrinks for every inline tenant. The anchor pays a fixed CAM contribution. Inline tenants carry the remaining pool based on the smaller denominator.

SLIDE 3:
The math
100,000 SF shopping center.
40,000 SF anchor excluded.
60,000 SF inline denominator.
Inline tenant A has 6,000 SF.
Without exclusion: 6,000 / 100,000 = 6.0% share.
With exclusion: 6,000 / 60,000 = 10.0% share.

SLIDE 4:
The anchor shortfall
If the anchor's uncapped share would be $200,000 but the lease caps its flat contribution at $120,000, the $80,000 shortfall has to be handled exactly as the leases allow.

SLIDE 5:
Three tracking errors
1. Failing to apply the exclusion.
2. Applying different denominators across tenants.
3. Leaving dark-anchor space excluded after the anchor vacates.

SLIDE 6:
Before each reconciliation
Pull the denominator language from every inline tenant lease. Confirm whether the lease uses total GLA, leasable area excluding anchors, or a fixed denominator.

SLIDE 7:
Documentation requirement
Every statement should show the denominator used and the basis for it. If the denominator changed, the statement should explain why.
