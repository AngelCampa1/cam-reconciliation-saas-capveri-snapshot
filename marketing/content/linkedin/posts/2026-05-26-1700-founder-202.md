SLIDE 1:
What "deterministic accuracy" actually means in a CAM reconciliation tool
(And why it matters that we don't use AI for the math)

SLIDE 2:
Deterministic means: same inputs, same output, every time
No probabilistic scoring. No model-generated estimates.
The calculation follows a defined rule set. You can trace every number back to the rule that produced it.

SLIDE 3:
Why financial tools need this
A CAM reconciliation is a legal document.
If a tenant auditor asks why a charge was calculated a certain way, the answer must be traceable.
"The model suggested this" is not a defensible answer.

SLIDE 4:
What we use AI for (one specific thing)
Extracting lease terms from PDF documents.
Gross-up thresholds. Cap structures. Base years. Exclusion lists.
AI assists the extraction. A human reviews it before it affects any calculation.

SLIDE 5:
What we don't use AI for
The reconciliation calculation itself.
Gross-up math. Cap application. Pro-rata allocation. BOMA denominators.
These use hard-coded logic with published standards.

SLIDE 6:
The audit trail requirement
Every finding in a CapVeri output includes the specific lease clause, the GL line item, the input values, and the formula applied.
If you need to defend a number, you have a complete trace.

SLIDE 7:
The practical consequence
When actual occupancy is 97% and the lease threshold is 95%, the gross-up factor is exactly 1.0.
Not approximately 1.0. Exactly 1.0.
No rounding behavior from a language model that doesn't understand lease law.

SLIDE 8:
Every dollar is traceable.
That's the standard we hold the calculation engine to.
