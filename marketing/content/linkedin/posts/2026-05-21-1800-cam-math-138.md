The call came in mid-March: a national retailer's audit team requesting backup on five properties where the pro-rata denominator had been miscalculated since 2021 - a $300,000 cumulative exposure.

The property controller had run the same reconciliation process for eleven years. Yardi reports, Excel cap math, statements out by April 15. No disputes in the prior three years. The retail tenant was just doing their annual sweep.

Three weeks later, the findings landed.

The denominator had been wrong at all five properties since 2021. A BOMA remeasurement had reduced each building's certified GLA, but the update never made it into the system's lease flags. The old denominator was still calculating every tenant's pro-rata share. For three years.

Each property was overbilling its tenants by roughly 3.5% on a recoverable pool ranging from $400,000 to $650,000. Across five properties and three years, the exposure was just over $300,000 in overcharged CAM.

The refunds were the smaller problem. The audit process took fourteen weeks. The controller's time, outside counsel review, lease-by-lease documentation, tenant negotiations, amended statements. Total resolution cost exceeded the refund amount.

What went wrong: the denominator update was treated as an administrative task, not an accounting task. The facility manager updated the certificate. No one updated the lease abstracts. No one updated the ERP. The reconciliation ran correctly against the wrong inputs.

CAM reconciliation accuracy depends on the inputs as much as the math. A perfectly executed gross-up formula applied to a wrong denominator produces a wrong answer that looks right.

The specific failure point in this case. using the total building SF instead of the lease-defined leasable RSF. is the most common single source of billing disputes across commercial portfolios. The fix is mechanical: before each reconciliation, pull the current denominator from the signed lease abstract and compare it to the denominator in the system. Any difference stops the process.

The five-year rule applies here. Denominator drift accumulates silently and compounds every year. By year three, the total exposure often exceeds what any individual tenant would find worth disputing. until an auditor runs a portfolio sweep and finds all five properties at once.
