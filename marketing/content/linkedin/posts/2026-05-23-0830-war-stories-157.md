SLIDE 1:
When a CAM demand letter arrives
What it says, what it doesn't, and what to do in the first 72 hours

SLIDE 2:
What the letter usually contains
A claim that the tenant was overbilled by a specific amount.
A request for 6-8 categories of documentation.
A response deadline (typically 30 days).
Sometimes: a lease reference. Sometimes: a calculation. Often: neither.

SLIDE 3:
Check the procedural requirements first
Florida: 30-day written notice is a condition precedent to any legal remedy for a billing dispute.
California: SB 1103 requires a specific demand sequence before treble damages attach for qualifying small-business tenants.
If the letter doesn't satisfy the procedural requirement for the state, that is a threshold defense. before anyone reviews whether the billing was correct.

SLIDE 4:
Do not confuse procedural defense with billing defense
A letter that doesn't satisfy Florida's notice requirement can be resubmitted with the notice.
A letter that triggers a procedural defense today can restart the process correctly tomorrow.
Winning on procedure does not mean the billing was right. If there is an error, it persists.

SLIDE 5:
The 72-hour response
Acknowledge receipt in writing.
Identify the responsible party on the property management side.
Provide a realistic timeline for substantive response.
This step prevents escalation to attorneys. which changes the economics of the dispute significantly.

SLIDE 6:
Pull the six documents before responding on the merits
1. Reconciliation statement
2. GL detail for the CAM pool
3. Lease operating expense definition and exclusion list
4. Gross-up calculation with occupancy documentation
5. Pro-rata denominator history
6. Cap bank history (if applicable)
Do not respond substantively until all six are in hand.

SLIDE 7:
Run an internal review before responding
Before disputing the tenant's allegation, run the same calculation independently.
If the internal review finds an error, acknowledge it proactively.
A proactive credit before formal dispute is far less expensive than a contested refund.

SLIDE 8:
CAM dispute settlements typically come in well below the initial claim.
How far below is determined almost entirely by what happens in the first 30 days.
Quick, organized, complete responses land at the low end.
Slow, incomplete, or defensive responses land at the high end.
