The contrarian view on base year leases: the lease year that seems most convenient to use as a base year is often the worst one to use.

A base year set during 2020 or 2021. when buildings ran at 60-70% occupancy and operating costs were artificially suppressed. produces a floor that doesn't reflect what it costs to run the building normally. Every year after, as occupancy returns to 90-95%, expenses look like they're growing dramatically. They're not growing. They're normalizing. But the tenant reads a true-up statement that says they owe $6.00/SF more than the base year. The base year was wrong from the start.

The standard remedy is base year normalization: gross up the actual base year expenses to what they would have been at stabilized occupancy (typically 95%). The formula: Normalized Base Year = Actual Base Year Expenses × (95% / Base Year Occupancy%).

Without normalization on a 70% occupancy base year, the floor understates stabilized costs by roughly 35%. Every future reconciliation charges the tenant for 35% of normalization that should have been baked into the base.

Tenants with pandemic-era base years that were never normalized have been paying for this since 2022. Their auditors will find it. The documentation of whether normalization was applied, or deliberately not applied because the tenant negotiated the raw base. should be in writing at lease execution.
