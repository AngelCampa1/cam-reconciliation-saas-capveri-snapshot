The true-up is not the reconciliation. Conflating the two is the single most common source of missed billing deadlines.

The reconciliation is the process: pull the GL, verify the expense pool, calculate pro-rata shares, apply gross-up and caps, cross-check against lease terms. It takes weeks. It requires a controller, signed lease abstracts, and a working GL export.

The true-up is the settlement: actual CAM minus estimates paid equals the balance due or credit owed. It's one number per tenant. It emerges from the reconciliation when the reconciliation is finished.

The two are separated by weeks. The reconciliation closes the books. The true-up invoices the result.

Why this matters: on a 30-tenant building at $3,800 average true-up per tenant, the reconciliation determines whether you collect $114,000 or hand out credits. Miss a gross-up calculation in the reconciliation and the true-up is just a perfectly calculated version of the wrong number.

The statement deadline in your lease is for the true-up. The time pressure comes from the reconciliation. Start the reconciliation 90 days before the deadline, not 30.
