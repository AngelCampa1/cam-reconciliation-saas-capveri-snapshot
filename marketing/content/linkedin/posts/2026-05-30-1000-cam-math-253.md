The following is an illustrative scenario based on common denominator drift patterns in multi-property portfolios:

The portfolio had 22 properties. The chief operating officer noticed that one building's CAM dispute rate was consistently higher than the others. three formal audit requests in four years from tenants who were otherwise paying reliably.

When the controller pulled the billing history, the denominator hadn't changed in five years. The system had 175,000 SF configured. The anchor tenant had vacated in 2021. The space had been redivided and re-leased, changing the lease pool configuration. A BOMA remeasurement in 2022 had added 3,800 SF to the certified area. Two minor suite reconfigurations in 2023 had adjusted the rentable area of two suites.

The system still said 175,000 SF. The actual correct denominator, tracking each change, was approximately 178,300 SF.

The 3.3% drift seemed small. But on a $1.5M CAM pool, a 3.3% denominator error shifts $49,500 in costs across the tenant mix. For a 10% pro-rata tenant, that's $4,950 per year. Over four years, $19,800 in overpayment exposure. well within the audit lookback window.

Each of the three audit requests had been triggered by a different tenant noticing that their per-SF CAM rate was out of line with what they expected based on their lease. None of them had modeled the denominator drift specifically. Their auditors had.

The fix required rebuilding the denominator history from the BOMA remeasurement forward, reconciling each event against the certified measurement certificate and the lease amendments, and producing amended statements for the three years within the audit window. The three dispute settlements totaled $44,200.

The annual denominator reconciliation, comparing the system's denominator against the current certified RSF and any mid-year changes in the lease pool. would have caught the drift at the first annual increment. The 3,300 SF discrepancy from the BOMA remeasurement alone would have been found in 2022.

A pool-specific denominator tracking log that records the date of each change, its source, and the resulting denominator is the only way to detect drift before it accumulates into a multi-year audit finding. A system denominator that nobody checks against the certified measurement certificate is a denominator that will drift.

Across a 22-property portfolio, checking the denominator once per year before reconciliation takes less time than resolving one dispute.
