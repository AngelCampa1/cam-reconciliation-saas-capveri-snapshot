RealPage's CAM pool wizard walks you through setup step by step. That is exactly why it is easy to misconfigure.

The wizard makes it easy to accept defaults that do not match your leases. The errors that result do not show up immediately. they show up when reconciliation statements go out and tenants start asking why their numbers do not match what the lease says.

Four configuration points where the defaults are most likely to be wrong:

Cap rules: the wizard defaults to non-cumulative. Many office leases from 2015 onward specify cumulative caps, where unused headroom carries forward year over year. The two methodologies produce identical results in Year 1 and diverge significantly over time. Setting non-cumulative when the lease says cumulative systematically overcharges tenants every year the actual increase is below the cap.

Allocation basis: RealPage uses the denominator you define in the pool setup, not automatically from all active leases. When space is added or removed from the building, update the pool denominator manually. It will not update itself.

GL account assignment: what goes wrong is rarely the obvious categories. It is the capital expenditures that look like repairs, the residential amenity costs in a mixed-use property that should not appear in commercial CAM pools, and the new GL accounts created mid-year that were never mapped to the recovery pool.

Gross-up on fixed expenses: the default variable/fixed classification for new accounts is often variable. Property taxes, insurance premiums, and contract-based services that do not change with occupancy will be grossed up until someone audits the classification line by line.

None of these errors produce error messages. RealPage calculates correctly based on its configuration. The only way to catch them is to compare the output to the lease.
