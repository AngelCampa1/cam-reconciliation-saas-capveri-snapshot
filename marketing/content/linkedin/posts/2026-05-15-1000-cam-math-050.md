A one-property portfolio should finish reconciliation in 2-3 weeks. If you're managing five buildings and it's taking eight weeks, the process is the problem, not the volume.

Here are the benchmarks by portfolio size and building complexity.

For 1-5 properties: 2-3 weeks is accurate, 4-6 weeks is typical, 8+ weeks signals a process problem.

Per-building time by complexity: a simple NNN building with uniform leases runs 4-6 hours. A standard multi-tenant building with mixed structures runs 8-12 hours. A complex building with anchor exclusions, multiple denominators, and cumulative caps runs 12-18 hours.

What makes a building complex? The number of lease variations, not the number of tenants. A 40-tenant building where every tenant has the same lease form runs faster than a 10-tenant building where every tenant negotiated different exclusions, different cap structures, and different gross-up thresholds.

The seven causes of delays beyond complexity: GL export problems (accounts not mapped, December invoices not posted), late vendor invoices from Q4, incomplete lease abstracts, serial property processing instead of parallel, manual spreadsheet formulas with circular references, missing prior-year carryforward data, and insufficient staffing during Q1 season.

Every delay past the 90-day deadline is a liability. Not just operationally. In some leases, late delivery waives the landlord's right to collect the true-up balance. That's a fixed deadline, not an aspirational target.
