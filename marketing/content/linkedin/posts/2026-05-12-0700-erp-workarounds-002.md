Your Yardi GL export doesn't balance. Before you touch a single CAM formula, stop.

The instinct is to start investigating tenant-level math. That's almost always the wrong place to start. The problem is usually in the export configuration itself, not the reconciliation logic downstream.

Here's the diagnostic sequence that finds the root cause in the shortest time.

1. Check the date basis first. Yardi can filter exports in ways that look identical on the surface but exclude late-posted accruals. If the export uses posting-date logic, the statements reflect a different basis and the totals will be close but won't tie. Rerun the export to match the basis your finance team uses for final statements. If the gap collapses, stop there.

2. Check account scope before anything else. Saved export filters don't update automatically when new GL accounts are added. If expenses were reclassified mid-year or new vendor categories were created, those accounts may have been excluded from the filter silently. Pull the chart of accounts for the same entity, identify every account with period activity, compare it to your export set. Missing accounts are one of the highest-frequency causes of "the math is wrong" when the math is actually fine.

3. Confirm the export was pulled after final close.
This is the easiest mistake to make in Q1 crunch. If the export was pulled before final journal entries posted, it's stale. Check the export timestamp against the close completion date. A stale export looks valid. it's technically correct as of the pull date. Don't debug it as if it's a live data issue.

4. Check accounting basis alignment.
Even when dates are right, accrual vs. cash can create a gap that looks like a reconciliation error. Year-end payables will show up in one basis and not the other.

5. Consider intercompany structure last.
If the statements are consolidated and the export is entity-level, the gap is structural. Intercompany eliminations won't show in an entity export. Document them explicitly and compare on the same structural basis.

The pattern matters: a large, abrupt gap points to account scope or wrong entity. A small, persistent gap points to date basis or late close adjustments. A gap that only appears at the consolidated level points to intercompany treatment.

None of this requires opening a support ticket with Yardi. It requires the right QA sequence before the reconciliation starts.

Once the GL export ties cleanly, then move to recovery pool configuration, gross-up logic, and pro-rata denominators.
