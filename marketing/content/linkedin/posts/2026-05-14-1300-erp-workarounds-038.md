MRI's recovery billing module gives you more control over gross-up than most ERPs. Which means there are more ways to configure it wrong.

Five mistakes show up repeatedly.

Building vs. floor Share Type. In a 10-story office building where floors 1-5 are fully leased and floors 6-10 are 50% vacant, building-level occupancy is 75% while floor-level occupancy for floors 1-5 is 100%. Tenants on those floors get grossed up under a building Share Type even though their floor is full. The lease controls which methodology applies. not convention.

Variable/Fixed classification not configured. New expense categories in MRI often default to Variable. That means capital expenditures get added to the variable pool and grossed up until someone explicitly changes the flag. If $100,000 in fixed expenses are incorrectly flagged as Variable in a building at 80% occupancy with a 95% gross-up threshold, the gross-up multiplier overstates expenses by $18,750. charged to tenants who have no obligation to pay it.

Gross-up threshold doesn't match lease terms. MRI allows a single threshold per recovery pool, but leases may specify different thresholds per tenant. Large anchor tenants often negotiate 90% thresholds while standard tenants have 95%. Some tenants have no gross-up clause at all. Without tenant-level overrides, the pool-level setting applies to everyone incorrectly.

Occupancy period mismatch. Year-end snapshot versus full-year average produces very different results when a building had mid-year vacancies. A tenant who left in October means December 31 shows lower occupancy than the building maintained for most of the year.

Recovery pool hierarchy in multi-building portfolios. Shared expenses need to be allocated across buildings correctly. A retail tenant's lease specifying "Building 1 only" while the recovery pool includes shared parking from both buildings is a billing error.

MRI will not self-report any of these. It calculates what you configured.
