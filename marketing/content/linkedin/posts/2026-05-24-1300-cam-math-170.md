The cumulative cap carry-forward is not a landlord favor. It's a contractual right that most landlords never use because they lost the schedule.

Here's the math: 5% cumulative cap, $8.00/SF base year. In year three, actual expenses come in at $8.45/SF and the ceiling is $8.82/SF. The tenant pays $8.45. The landlord banks $0.37 in unused ceiling.

Year four: expenses spike to $9.85/SF. The ceiling is $9.72/SF. And The landlord has $0.37 banked. Without a carry-forward schedule, the landlord caps at $9.72 and absorbs $0.13. With the schedule, the math is: prior year actual × 1.05 = new ceiling. The ceiling compounds from the base year, not from actual collections.

The contrarian point: non-cumulative caps and cumulative caps look identical in the system if the carry-forward isn't tracked. Both produce a ceiling. Only cumulative caps produce a ceiling that accounts for prior-year restraint.

Landlords who apply non-cumulative math to cumulative leases lose recovery every time actual expenses spike. The error compounds silently across every reconciliation after the first year.

Keep a per-tenant cap ledger. Document the base year, the cap rate, the cap type, and the ceiling calculation for every year. It takes an afternoon to build and prevents the most recoverable landlord error in CAM.
