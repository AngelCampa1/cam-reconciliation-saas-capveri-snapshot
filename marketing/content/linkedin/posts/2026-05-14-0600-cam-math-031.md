A building that goes from 200,000 SF to 212,000 SF under BOMA 2024 didn't add a single square foot of new construction. The measurement changed.

That 12,000 SF increase comes from outdoor amenity spaces. rooftop terraces, courtyards, tenant patios. that prior BOMA standards excluded from rentable area. When these spaces meet the criteria for inclusion, they flow into the denominator.

Here's what that does to a tenant's pro-rata share: a tenant at 5% of the old 200,000 SF denominator becomes 4.72% of the new 212,000 SF denominator. On a $2 million CAM pool, that tenant's annual charge drops by $5,600. Multiply across 15 inline tenants and the landlord's recovery position shrinks by $84,000.

That recovery shortfall does not disappear. For properties where outdoor spaces are included in the denominator but not yet covered by existing leases, the landlord absorbs the gap until those leases roll and new leases are written to the updated measurement.

A 12,000 SF rooftop terrace that costs $30,000 to $60,000 annually to maintain is now in the CAM pool but diluting rather than contributing to recovery. The remeasurement math works in the landlord's favor only when new leases are signed against the larger RSF base.

Know the denominator your leases reference before you update the system.
