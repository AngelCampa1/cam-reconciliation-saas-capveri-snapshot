The conversation about CAM reconciliation problems almost always ends up in the same place: "We need to replace Yardi."

It is the wrong conclusion. And reaching it is expensive.

Replacing Yardi means 12-18 months of implementation, data migration, parallel operation, and retraining. Cost for a mid-market portfolio: $500,000 to $2M. The new ERP will have the same CAM configuration problem. just with different screens.

The actual problem is not the ERP. The actual problem is that your ERP has no knowledge of your lease terms. It has knowledge of what someone entered into its recovery pool configuration, often during initial setup, sometimes years ago, sometimes by someone who no longer works there.

When a lease is amended and the gross-up threshold changes from 95% to 90%, Yardi continues using 95% until someone manually updates the configuration. Yardi does not know the lease changed. That is not a flaw in Yardi. That is how every ERP works.

What teams actually need is an independent verification layer. Not a replacement.

The workflow: export GL data from Yardi as CSV (the same export your property accountant already produces for year-end close). Upload to independent reconciliation software. The software recalculates from the raw GL data. without inheriting the ERP's recovery pool configuration. Where the calculation diverges from the lease-term calculation, it flags the specific discrepancy with the specific lease clause that governs it.

What stays the same: your Yardi instance, your GL account structure, your existing workflow. No IT involvement, no API credentials, no implementation project.

What changes: you have an independent check before statements go out, not after tenants dispute. For a 30-building portfolio, the shift is from 6-8 weeks of Q1 reconciliation work to 1-2 weeks of focused review.

Tenant auditors. Tango Analytics, Visual Lease, LeaseQuery. are hired on contingency to find exactly these discrepancies. They typically charge 20-40% of the amount they recover for the tenant. On a $15,000 error, that is $3,000-$6,000 to the tenant's auditor, plus the credit or refund, plus the damaged relationship.

The independent verification approach using CSV exports from your existing ERP costs a fraction of what one disputed reconciliation costs in staff time, legal review, and tenant relations.

The simplest starting point: pick your most complex building and run its GL export through independent reconciliation software. Use the discrepancies found (or not found) to decide whether the workflow is worth extending to the full portfolio.

Fix the verification layer. Keep the ERP.
