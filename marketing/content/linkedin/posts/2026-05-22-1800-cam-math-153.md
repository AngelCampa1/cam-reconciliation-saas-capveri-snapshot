SLIDE 1:
Management fees in CAM
(They're usually recoverable. The "usually" is where the disputes live.)

SLIDE 2:
The baseline rule:
Most commercial leases permit management fees as a recoverable CAM expense.
The underlying rationale: managing the property. overseeing vendors, processing invoices, responding to tenant issues. is a legitimate operating cost that benefits all tenants.
Always verify. Some leases, especially those negotiated by experienced tenant-rep brokers, explicitly exclude them.

SLIDE 3:
Two fee structures:
Percentage of gross revenues. most common. Example: $2,000,000 annual gross rents × 4% = $80,000 in CAM.
Fixed administrative fee. less common for institutional properties. Flat annual dollar amount regardless of revenues.
The structure matters for gross-up and cap calculations. Get the classification wrong and both calculations are wrong.

SLIDE 4:
Common lease limits on management fee recovery:
Percentage caps. "management fees shall not exceed 4% of gross revenues" regardless of actual charged rate.
Market rate limits. fee must be at or below market for comparable properties (most often invoked for affiliated management companies).
Explicit exclusions, some retail and healthcare leases exclude management fees from CAM entirely.

SLIDE 5:
Variable vs. fixed classification:
Percentage-based fees are variable. they scale with occupancy and revenue. These should NOT be included in gross-up calculations. Grossing up a percentage fee creates a circular calculation.
Fixed admin fees are fixed. include them in gross-up calculations.
Misclassifying management fees propagates errors through every gross-up calculation for the year.

SLIDE 6:
The double-billing error:
A property management company charges a 4% fee on gross revenues. The same company also bills its direct staff costs separately through the CAM pool (property manager salary, assistant salary). The lease covers both, or appears to.
The audit finding: both the percentage fee and the direct staff costs are in the pool. One covers the other. Any lease that says "management fee" typically intends one or the other, not both.

SLIDE 7:
The cap structure question:
Are management fees inside or outside the controllable expense cap?
Some leases exclude management fees from cap calculations entirely. Others include them. A few have a separate cap just for management fees.
If your lease is silent, the default interpretation varies by jurisdiction. Get this wrong and you've either applied the cap too broadly or not broadly enough.

SLIDE 8:
Before each reconciliation:
Step 1: Pull the management fee amount from the GL.
Step 2: Compare it against the lease's percentage cap.
Step 3: Verify whether it's classified as fixed or variable in your system.
Step 4: Check whether it's inside or outside the controllable expense cap.
Fifteen minutes. Prevents the most common management fee dispute.
