Most property accounting teams still rely on Excel as their primary CAM reconciliation tool.

Three things changed that made that number dangerous:

Tenant audit firms became a standard part of the lease cycle. Companies like Tango Analytics and Visual Lease review reconciliation statements on contingency, looking for errors. They find material discrepancies in the majority of statements they audit. Excel has no self-validation layer. it cannot tell the controller that a formula no longer matches the lease.

Portfolio scale increased. A 3-building Excel process managed by one experienced controller has recoverable risk. A 20-building process distributed across a team of four, with multiple spreadsheet versions in circulation during Q1, has a systemic version control problem that Excel cannot solve.

ASC 842 and IFRS 16 raised the stakes for lease data accuracy. Tenants and their advisors now scrutinize lease financials more carefully than they did five years ago.

The workflow hasn't changed: export from ERP, paste into spreadsheet, apply formulas, verify caps manually, generate statements. The risk profile has changed around it.

A gross-up error applied to the wrong expense category on a 200,000 SF building produces a discrepancy that compounds annually. The error looks correct in the spreadsheet. The tenant's auditor finds it two years later and charges 20-40% of the recovered amount on contingency.

The problem is not that Excel is old. The problem is that Excel has no awareness of the leases it's supposed to calculate against.
