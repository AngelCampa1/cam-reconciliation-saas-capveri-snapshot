Property management companies automate accounts payable, lease abstraction, maintenance requests, and tenant communication. CAM reconciliation, the process with the highest financial impact per error. still runs in Excel at most firms.

Here's why that's backwards.

Every other back-office process creates linear value. Automating AP saves the cost of the AP clerk's time. The ROI is the cost saved.

CAM reconciliation has a multiplier: the capitalization rate.

A $50,000 gross-up error doesn't cost you $50,000. At a 6% cap rate, it costs you $833,333 in property value. because NOI is capitalized. A $25,000 error destroys $416,667 in value. A $100,000 error at a 5% cap rate destroys $2,000,000.

The same math works in reverse. Finding $50,000 in previously uncaptured CAM revenue creates $833,333 in value from a process change, not a capital investment.

Where does the time actually go?

In a typical reconciliation cycle, the rough time split looks something like: ~40% data gathering, ~25% lease interpretation, ~15% actual calculation, ~10% quality review, ~10% statement generation and delivery (illustrative).

The calculation step is only 15% of the work. But it's where errors are most expensive. A formula error affects every tenant in the building, every year it goes undetected.

What automation actually solves is not speed. It eliminates entire categories of errors.

Gross-up misconfiguration, a system that knows which expenses are variable won't gross up property taxes. Cap tracking. cumulative cap carry-forward maintained automatically, not manually tracked in a spreadsheet. Denominator accuracy, pro-rata shares calculated from the current rent roll, not a static number typed in last year. CapEx detection. GL entries screened against classification rules before they enter the CAM pool.

The build vs. buy math for a 20-building portfolio:

Manual Excel process: 300 hours times $75 per hour fully loaded equals $22,500 per year in labor, plus 1-5% material error rate across the portfolio. Purpose-built software: $5,000-$15,000 per year with near-zero errors on automated checks.

The software pays for itself if it catches one $15,000 error per year. Tenant audit firms report finding overcharges in a significant portion of reconciliation statements they review. Tenant audit recovery amounts vary significantly by property type and error category.

CAM reconciliation is consistently the highest-ROI process to automate in property management for exactly this reason: the error cost is high, the fix is tractable, and the savings land directly on the NOI line.
