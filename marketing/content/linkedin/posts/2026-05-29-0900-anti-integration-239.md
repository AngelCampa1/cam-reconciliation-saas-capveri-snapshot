A $50,000 CAM reconciliation error doesn't cost $50,000.

At a 6% cap rate, it costs $833,000 in asset value.

Property owners and lenders use NOI to value commercial real estate. A systematic underbilling of $50,000 per year reduces NOI by $50,000, which reduces the property's implied value by $833,000 at a 6% cap rate. The error is not a billing mistake. it is a valuation problem.

Two data points that frame the exposure:

Raymond Panko's spreadsheet error research (published via EuSpRIG) found errors in at least 86% of audited corporate spreadsheets, meaning the majority of professionally produced models contain at least one error. CAM reconciliation spreadsheets are not immune.

Tenant audit firms find material discrepancies in the majority of reconciliation statements they review. They work on contingency with contingency fees in the 20-40% range of recovered amounts. Their incentive is to find errors. Their success rate suggests they usually do.

The combination is predictable: a process with a high inherent error rate, reviewed by external auditors who find those errors in the majority of cases, with each error multiplied by the cap rate into an asset value impact that dwarfs the calculation cost.

CAM reconciliation is not the most complex process in property accounting. It is the one where errors are most expensive relative to what it costs to prevent them. The barrier to independent verification is a CSV export. the same export your team already produces for monthly close review.
