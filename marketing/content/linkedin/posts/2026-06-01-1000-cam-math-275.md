The most expensive confusion in CAM billing: treating reconciliation and true-up as synonyms.

The reconciliation is the process. It takes weeks. It involves pulling GL data, verifying the expense pool, applying gross-up, applying caps, calculating each tenant's share. This is accounting work.

The true-up is the result. It's a number: the difference between what the tenant owed and what they paid. This is a settlement number. It converts into an invoice or a credit.

Landlords who say "we sent the reconciliation" when they mean "we sent the true-up invoice" are describing two different stages of a two-stage process as one event. The practical problem: when a tenant disputes the invoice, they dispute the reconciliation process. the methodology, the inputs, the math. Responding to a true-up dispute requires producing the reconciliation documentation. If the reconciliation was never documented separately from the invoice, the dispute response requires rebuilding it.

Build the reconciliation as a standalone document with all calculation steps visible and documented. Produce the true-up invoice from it. Keep both. The true-up invoice tells the tenant what they owe. The reconciliation documentation is what you produce when they ask why.

These are not the same document. Treating them as the same document creates the audit exposure that a 12-month tenant audit window is designed to exploit.
