The 2026 reconciliation season has three layers of complexity that previous seasons didn't have simultaneously.

BOMA 2024 is now operative. The updated measurement standard changes how rentable area is calculated for buildings that have been remeasured, which affects the pro-rata denominator for every affected tenant. Reconciliations for remeasured buildings need to reflect the new denominator and explain the change to tenants who will notice the difference.

SB 1103 is in its second year. California property managers are past the initial adjustment. Into the phase where documentation practices and audit engagement patterns are settling. The treble damages provision has changed how tenant audit firms approach California portfolios. The standard of documentation that was acceptable before January 2025 is not the standard anymore.

Property tax volatility is elevated heading into the 2026 cycle. Harris Central Appraisal District is issuing 2026 notices; Cook County's south and west suburbs are under triennial reassessment for 2026-2028; Maricopa County continues to see appreciation-driven assessment activity. Tax is typically 25-40% of the CAM pool, so a meaningful assessment increase on a building moves total tenant charges by several percentage points before any other expense category changes. That movement needs a cover letter explanation, not just a line item on the statement.

The 15-point checklist for the 2026 season includes: BOMA 2024 denominator verification, SB 1103 documentation requirements for California properties, and a property tax cover letter for markets with above-average assessment increases. Properties with none of these three conditions still need the standard seven checks. Properties with any of them need additional preparation before the statement is delivered.

The practical window for completing 2026 reconciliations is March through June for calendar-year leases. For operations that haven't started the preparation work, the window is narrowing.
