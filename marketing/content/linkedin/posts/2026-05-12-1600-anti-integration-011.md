Excel doesn't have a bug. It has a design philosophy: trust the user.

In CAM reconciliation, "trust the user" means trusting someone to never mistype a cell reference, never forget to update a formula when a lease renews, never accidentally reference last year's occupancy instead of this year's. At three buildings, that trust is manageable. At twenty, it's a liability.

Here's what actually goes wrong, and what to do instead.

The silent formula error is the most expensive problem. Excel calculates whatever formula you give it. It doesn't know that cell B47 should reference the gross-up threshold from the lease, not the hard-coded value someone typed three years ago. When the threshold changed from 95% to 90% at renewal and the spreadsheet wasn't updated, Excel continues at 95% producing a wrong number that looks right. The spreadsheet balances. The format looks professional. The statement goes out.

Version control fails at scale. At Q1 crunch, three controllers are working on thirty buildings simultaneously. One has "Reconciliation_2025_v4_FINAL.xlsx". Another has "v5_FINAL_corrected.xlsx". Someone will generate statements from the wrong version. This is not a theoretical risk at portfolios over fifteen buildings.

The audit trail problem shows up when it's too late. When a tenant disputes a reconciliation, Excel cannot tell you when the formula was set, whether it changed during the process, or whether the version that produced the statement is the same version you're showing them now.

And when the controller who built the master spreadsheet in 2019 leaves. And They eventually leave. the knowledge leaves with them.

What to use instead:

The simplest viable upgrade is ERP-native reconciliation (Yardi's recovery billing module, MRI's recovery analysis). It maintains an audit trail and handles most standard calculations. The limitation: it doesn't validate whether its own configuration matches your lease terms.

The more defensible approach is independent verification alongside your ERP. Keep Yardi or MRI for GL management and billing. Add a layer that reads your ERP's CSV export and validates every calculation against lease terms. This catches the configuration errors that ERP-native reconciliation can't catch. because it's calculating the same thing using a different source.

What this replaces: the Excel spreadsheets. What it doesn't touch: your ERP, your GL structure, your existing workflow.

For a thirty-building portfolio, teams that have made this transition report it compresses reconciliation from eight weeks to three. with the same staff, catching errors before statements go out instead of after.

The spreadsheet isn't dying because it's bad at math. It's dying because the cost of its failure modes now exceeds the cost of the alternative.
