MRI's recovery billing module is more granular than most property management systems. That precision helps. It also creates more ways to configure gross-up incorrectly.

Five mistakes account for many MRI gross-up errors.

1. Floor-level versus building-level share type mismatch.
In a partially vacant building, floor-level occupancy and building-level occupancy can produce very different gross-up factors. The lease decides which one applies.

2. Variable and fixed expense designation not audited after new GL accounts are added.
If a new capital improvement account defaults into the variable pool, MRI may gross it up even though the lease excludes it.

3. One pool-level threshold applied to leases with different thresholds.
One tenant may have 90%, another 95%, and another no gross-up clause at all. The setup has to match tenant-level terms.

4. Occupancy period set to year-end snapshot instead of annual average.
A December vacancy can distort the year if the lease calls for weighted-average occupancy.

5. Recovery pool hierarchy errors in multi-building portfolios.
Shared expenses must be allocated to the correct buildings before tenant-level recovery is calculated.

MRI will not self-report these issues. If gross-up is set to 90% when leases say 95%, MRI produces the 90% result without warning.

The audit: export the expense type configuration for every GL account in the pool, review fixed versus variable classification, pull every lease with a gross-up clause, and compare threshold, occupancy method, and recovery hierarchy to the system setup.

That is an annual step before statements go out.
