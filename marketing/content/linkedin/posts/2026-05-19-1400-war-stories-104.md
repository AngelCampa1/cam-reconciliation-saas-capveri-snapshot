Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"CAM leakage is not a billing dispute. It's money the lease allows you to recover that you're not recovering. And On a 200,000 square foot building, it runs $5,900 to $35,300 per year."

Talking points:
- CAM leakage is the gap between what the lease permits a landlord to bill. What is actually billed. Unlike overbilling, leakage harms the landlord, not the tenant. And unlike overbilling, it doesn't generate a demand letter. it just quietly reduces NOI. - There are five types of leakage. Gross-up leakage: the gross-up factor was calculated wrong or not applied to the full variable pool. Cap escalator leakage: the CAM cap was tracked incorrectly, suppressing recoveries in years when above-cap charges were actually permitted. Pro-rata share leakage: the tenant's share was calculated on a denominator that was too large. Expense classification leakage: recoverable expenses were coded to non-recoverable GL accounts. Base year drift: the base year was set abnormally low and was never corrected. - Dollar impact by type on a 200,000 SF building: gross-up leakage runs $2,500-$15,000 per year. Cap escalator leakage runs $1,500-$8,000. Pro-rata share leakage runs $1,000-$5,000. Expense classification leakage runs $500-$4,000. Base year drift runs $400-$3,300. - The leakage compounds. A building that has been underrecovering for three years is not just losing money in year three. it has lost the cumulative total. On a 200,000 SF building at the high end, that is over $100,000 in suppressed recovery. - Leakage is almost never discovered through normal operations. It shows up when someone compares the reconciliation output to what the lease actually permits. That comparison requires looking at the lease, which most reconciliation processes don't do systematically. - The fix is an annual check against the lease terms: verify the gross-up calculation, the cap bank, the pro-rata denominator, and the expense account mapping. Four checks. Caught early, leakage is corrected in the next reconciliation cycle.

Closing line:
"Your lease allows you to recover these costs. Make sure you actually are."
