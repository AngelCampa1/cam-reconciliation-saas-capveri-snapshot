The lease says "3% of Operating Expenses." The management agreement says "3% of Effective Gross Revenue." Your building has $1.2M in operating expenses and $4M in gross revenue.

These produce a $36,000 management fee and a $120,000 management fee. Both are 3%. One is recoverable. One is not.

The lease controls what you can bill tenants. The management agreement controls what you actually pay. When the bases don't match. And They often don't. the difference is a landlord cost. It can't be recovered through CAM. Every dollar over the lease-permitted amount that goes into the pool is a refund waiting to happen.

The contrarian point: most management fee disputes aren't about whether the fee is recoverable at all. They're about which calculation base was used. A correct percentage applied to the wrong base produces a wrong number that looks precise.

Check the specific clause in each tenant's lease. If it says "of Operating Expenses," use operating expenses. If it says "of Gross Revenues," use gross revenues. If the management agreement charges more than the lease permits, absorb the difference. The tenant's auditor will find the mismatch.
