Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"FASB ASC 842 didn't just change lease accounting. It turned your CAM statements into balance sheet items. And Your tenants' auditors are now reading them."

Talking points:
- ASC 842 requires tenants to capitalize operating leases on their balance sheets. Variable CAM charges are excluded from the capitalized value. But That means tenants. Their CPAs now scrutinize every CAM reconciliation to correctly separate fixed from variable components. - Before ASC 842, CAM overbilling was an income statement issue for the tenant. After ASC 842, a miscalculated fixed versus variable split can distort the tenant's balance sheet. That changes how seriously their finance team treats a disputed statement. - Industry observers and tenant audit firms report that CAM audit request volume rose after ASC 842 adoption. Tenants with public reporting obligations or private equity ownership are under pressure to validate CAM classifications. Their outside accountants are generating the requests, not just their real estate teams. - Estimate accuracy became a compliance issue. Under ASC 842, tenants need reliable CAM estimates for their monthly accruals. A landlord whose estimates are consistently off by 15-20% creates problems for the tenant's financial close process. - The documentation standard is higher. Pre-842, a one-page reconciliation statement was often enough. Post-842, tenants and their auditors want GL backup, expense categorization schedules, and the gross-up calculation detail. This is not optional for tenants with audit obligations. - The practical effect for property managers: the audience for your reconciliation statement expanded. It used to go to the tenant's real estate contact. Now it goes to their controller and their CPA firm. Both of them know what to look for.

Closing line:
"Your reconciliation statement is now part of a financial reporting chain that didn't exist before 2019. Build it like someone who knows CPAs will read it."
