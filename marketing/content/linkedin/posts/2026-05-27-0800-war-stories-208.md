SLIDE 1:
Why spreadsheets fail as CAM audit trails
(And what a real audit trail requires)

SLIDE 2:
What a spreadsheet shows you
The current state of the file.
The numbers as they exist right now.
Nothing about who changed what, when, or why.

SLIDE 3:
What a spreadsheet does not show you
Who changed cell B47 from $142,000 to $0 in November.
What the cell contained before the change.
Whether the change was an error, a correction, or an adjustment. And What supported it.

SLIDE 4:
Why this matters
Tenant audit firms consistently report material discrepancies in the majority of reconciliation statements they review.
Many of those errors are not original mistakes. They are corrections that were never documented.
When a tenant auditor asks why a number changed between the draft and the final statement, the answer is frequently "I don't remember."

SLIDE 5:
The version control problem
Files get copied, renamed, emailed, and saved to multiple locations.
Which version is authoritative: the one emailed in February or the one in the shared folder updated in March?
Without version control, the answer is contested. and "contested" is the start of a dispute.

SLIDE 6:
The retention problem
CAM audit windows run 12-48 months after the statement is issued, depending on the lease.
Most spreadsheet operations cannot produce a complete, verified reconciliation package from three years ago.
The lease allows the tenant to audit it. If you can't reconstruct it, you can't defend it.

SLIDE 7:
What a real audit trail requires
Immutable records: once a calculation is run, the inputs and outputs are locked.
Version history: every change is logged with a timestamp, user, and reason.
Retention: full reconciliation packages maintained for the lease's audit window plus a buffer.
Reproducibility: the calculation can be rerun from stored inputs to produce the same output.

SLIDE 8:
A spreadsheet is not an audit trail.
It is a calculation tool with no memory.
The audit trail is the documentation that exists around the calculation. And Most of it lives nowhere when the process runs in spreadsheets.
