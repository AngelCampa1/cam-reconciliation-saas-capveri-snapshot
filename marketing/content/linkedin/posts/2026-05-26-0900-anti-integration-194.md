SLIDE 1:
A tenant's attorney asked for the 2022 gross-up methodology in 2025. The platform couldn't reproduce the same output twice. That's the court test for CAM reconciliation software.

SLIDE 2:
Why this question matters
A tenant files a dispute. Their auditor requests the calculation methodology.
You run the same inputs through your "AI-powered" reconciliation platform.
The output is different from the original.
"The AI said so" is not a defensible accounting methodology in arbitration.

SLIDE 3:
What deterministic means
Same inputs. Same output. Every single time.
Not approximately the same. Identical.
A CPA takes your inputs three years from now and arrives at the same number.
That is the standard GAAP requires. That is the standard courts require.

SLIDE 4:
Why AI cannot do this
Language models generate output by sampling token probabilities.
Two runs with identical inputs can produce different results.
Models drift between versions.
There is no step-by-step ledger showing how each dollar was allocated.
This is not a limitation to be improved. it is how the technology works.

SLIDE 5:
What a proper CAM audit trail requires
The gross-up formula applied, including the specific occupancy percentage used.
Each expense category determination: included, excluded, or capped.
The pro-rata share calculation with denominator and numerator.
The cap calculation showing base year, cumulative increases, and floor adjustments.
The final settlement amount and how it was derived.
A deterministic engine produces all of this. An LLM produces a number and a plausible explanation.

SLIDE 6:
Where AI belongs in the workflow
Document extraction: reads a 200-page lease, identifies CAM clauses, classifies GL codes.
AI cuts this from hours to minutes.
This is where AI earns its place.
Not in the calculation.

SLIDE 7:
The vendor evaluation question
When a CAM software vendor says "AI-powered reconciliation," ask one question:
"Can you run the same inputs twice and get the identical result, and can you produce a step-by-step calculation ledger showing every input, formula, and intermediate result?"
If the answer is no, or unclear. the audit trail is broken.

SLIDE 8:
For CAM math, "probably right" is not good enough.
Three years from now, a tenant's attorney will ask for the calculation.
The answer cannot be "the model produced that output."
