"Excel is free" is a true statement that leads property teams to the wrong conclusion.

The visible cost of Excel CAM reconciliation is near zero. The actual cost is buried in three places: labor hours nobody tracks, errors nobody catches, and audit exposure nobody prices until it arrives.

For a 10-property portfolio, fully loaded labor cost alone runs $16,250 per year at $65/hour for 250 hours. That's before errors. Purpose-built CAM software for the same portfolio typically costs $5,000-$25,000 per year based on publicly listed pricing from major CAM reconciliation software vendors.

The math is closer than most teams realize. And it flips decisively once you add error costs.

Raymond Panko's spreadsheet error research (published via EuSpRIG) found errors in at least 86% of audited corporate spreadsheets, with formula cells as the primary failure point and a per-cell error rate that compounds with sheet size. In CAM reconciliation, the relevant errors. wrong cell reference in a pro-rata calculation, gross-up applied to fixed expenses, cap escalator not carried forward. carry price tags of $2,000 to $50,000 per occurrence. A single gross-up error on a 200,000 SF building can exceed the annual cost of CAM software for the entire portfolio.

The other cost most teams ignore: key-person risk. When the controller who built the master spreadsheet in 2019 leaves, the replacement inherits a workbook with no documentation, custom formulas that reference non-obvious cells, and conditional logic that took years to build. The typical cost of a departure during reconciliation season: two to four weeks of delayed statements plus consultant fees.

The decision framework is simple. Under three properties with straightforward NNN leases and no gross-up clauses: spreadsheets are manageable. Above five properties with any lease complexity: the total cost of ownership tilts decisively toward software.

The spreadsheet isn't free. It's deferred payment on a risk you haven't priced yet.
