The contrarian take on property managers and CAM reconciliation: the job is not to understand the math. It's to own the inputs that make or break the math.

Property managers don't run the reconciliation. Controllers do. But every critical input comes from the PM: the lease abstract, occupancy records, move-in and move-out dates, amendments, mid-year event log.

A controller who runs perfect gross-up and cap math on a superseded lease abstract produces a precisely wrong number. The abstract error doesn't show in the calculation. It shows when the tenant's auditor reads the signed lease and finds a 4,200 SF suite that expanded to 6,800 SF in April. two years before the reconciliation was run.

The five things a property manager needs to deliver to accounting before the reconciliation runs: current lease abstract index, occupancy log with move-in and move-out dates, amendment index with execution dates, mid-year events log (expansions, contractions, construction, service changes), and the tenant delivery roster for statement distribution.

These are not accounting tasks. They are PM deliverables. Accounting cannot produce an accurate reconciliation without them.

The one deadline that creates permanent exposure: statement delivery. When the PM owns the delivery calendar but starts pulling data 10 days before the deadline, there's no buffer for a verification problem or a correction. Start 60 days before.
