A 60-tenant building. Each estimate letter needs the tenant's name, property address, suite number, new monthly CAM estimate, effective date, and the line-item breakdown of the new estimate. Manual process: 15 minutes per tenant. Total: 900 minutes, or 22 hours of work at the beginning of Q1, right when every other reconciliation deadline is also due.

Here's the framework that gets it to 2.5 hours.

Step 1: Build the data source correctly.
The estimate letter data comes from two places: the reconciliation output (what the new annual CAM estimate should be, calculated from the prior year's actual expenses plus a forward-looking adjustment) and the tenant roster (name, suite, address, effective date). These need to be in a single structured spreadsheet before any letters are generated.

Step 2: Build the template once, parameterize everything.
The estimate letter has one format. The data varies by tenant. Build a template where every variable field is a placeholder: tenant name, suite, monthly amount, annual total, effective date. Those placeholders pull from the data source. This is a data architecture decision, not a mail merge trick.

Step 3: Batch generate.
With a clean data source and a parameterized template, the 60 letters generate in one run. The output is a letter per tenant with the correct figures for each one. Review takes 30-45 minutes for 60 letters, not 15 minutes per letter.

Step 4: Batch deliver.
If the tenant portal supports bulk upload, upload all 60 PDFs in one operation. If delivery is by email, the batch generation step should produce the addressed emails, not just the attachments.

Step 5: Log delivery timestamps.
The effective date of the estimate change is legally triggered by delivery. For estimate letters with a January 1 effective date, delivery in the first week of December gives tenants reasonable notice and gives you a clean record of when the new estimate obligation began.

The 9x difference between manual and systematic comes from building the process once, correctly, so it runs the same way every year without consuming 22 hours of Q1 capacity that should go toward reconciliation work.
