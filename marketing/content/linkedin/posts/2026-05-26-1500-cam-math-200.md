The contrarian take on reconciliation season: "We always finish by May" is not a benchmark. It's an excuse.

For calendar-year commercial leases with 90-day deadlines, March 31 is the line. A team finishing in May is delivering statements 30 to 45 days past the contractual window. In some lease structures, that's not a courtesy issue. it's a collection issue.

What drives May delivery: GL closes late. Data isn't pulled until February. Errors found late in the process require rework. Complex properties that should have started first started last.

The 2026 version of this problem has additional layers: elevated office vacancy rates make gross-up calculations more consequential. Insurance and utility cost increases produced larger year-over-year variances than tenants expected. More tenants. specifically in California. now have SB 1103 rights requiring 30-day landlord responses. More tenants generally have retained outside auditors after finding errors in 2023 and 2024.

The practical change: statements issued in March with a clear variance explanation get processed as routine invoices. Statements issued in May with no variance context arrive at the same time tenants are reviewing their Q1 occupancy costs. The combination produces more disputes, not fewer.

The process fix is front-loading: GL pull in the first week of January, complex properties started by January 15, QC before drafting, not after.

The result is statements in March. Which is when they belong.
