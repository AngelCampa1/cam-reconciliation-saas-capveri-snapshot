A 100,000 SF shopping center. 40,000 SF anchor excluded from the CAM denominator. An inline tenant leasing 6,000 SF doesn't hold 6% of the building. They hold 10% of the effective denominator.

That 67% increase in effective share comes directly from the anchor exclusion. The anchor negotiated a fixed-dollar CAM contribution instead of a percentage share. The remaining 60,000 SF of inline tenants absorbs 100% of the recoverable CAM expenses minus the anchor's fixed payment.

The math is straightforward: 6,000 / 60,000 = 10%. On a $500,000 CAM pool after the anchor's contribution, that inline tenant pays $50,000 instead of $30,000 they'd pay at a 6% share.

The anchor exclusion is disclosed in the lease. Tenants who signed knew the denominator. The problem isn't the disclosed exclusion. It's the undisclosed expansion.

Three tracking errors that turn disclosed exclusions into disputes: the anchor's fixed contribution shrinks over time (capped at a 2003 base year while actual CAM grew 40%), the anchor renegotiates to self-maintain certain services and their exclusion expands, or the anchor vacates and the landlord doesn't have clear lease language on what happens to the denominator.

On a 10-tenant inline strip center with a 40,000 SF anchor shortfall: the landlord absorbs $80,000 per year in unrecovered CAM from the anchor vacancy alone. The remaining tenants don't owe that. The denominator isn't leased area, it's leasable area in the inline section.

Document the anchor's contribution method, the exclusion boundaries, and the vacancy fallback rules before the reconciliation runs.
