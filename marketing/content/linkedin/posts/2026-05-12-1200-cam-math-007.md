Seven CAM reconciliation errors cause most of the $15,000-$30,000 per-property recovery gap modeled across typical commercial portfolios. Here is where to look before statements go out.

1. Gross-up applied when actual occupancy already meets the threshold.
When actual occupancy equals or exceeds the target, the gross-up factor must be 1.0. Any factor above 1.0 inflates the pool past 100% occupancy.

2. Cap rate entered as a whole number instead of a decimal.
A 5% cap entered as 5.0 instead of 0.05 is effectively no cap. The statement may still look normal, so this survives review unless someone checks the setup.

3. Zero or a prior-period anomaly used as the base year.
A pandemic-low or construction-era base can distort every later year. Once the base is wrong, every capped year built on it is wrong too.

4. Cumulative cap bank not tracked.
A cumulative cap lets unused headroom carry forward. If the bank disappears during a spreadsheet handoff or system migration, the landlord usually under-recovers.

5. Overlapping lease dates after expansion or move-out.
When old and new square footage both remain active, the pro-rata share can be wrong for the whole period.

6. Admin fee calculated before the CAM cap.
If the lease says the cap applies before the admin fee, the sequence matters. Running the fee first changes the billed amount.

7. Base year set during a vacancy-heavy period.
A depressed base year can make normal recovery look like an excessive increase later.

Yardi and MRI will not warn you about these. They calculate what is configured. The fix is a specific pre-send check for each one.
