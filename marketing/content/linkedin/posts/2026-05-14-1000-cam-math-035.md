A tenant renews for five years. The renewal option says "base year resets to the year of renewal." The base year in your system still says 2020.

This is the most expensive oversight in lease renewal administration. A tenant whose 2020 base year was $180,000. Whose 2025 actual is $215,000 owes $35,000 in excess CAM. A tenant whose base year correctly reset to 2025 at $215,000 owes zero. Same lease, same building, same expenses, $30,000 different outcome in a single year.

The reset provision lives in the renewal option clause, not in the base year definition section of the lease. Most abstracts don't surface it. Property accountants running the reconciliation don't see it because they're reading the original lease terms.

The fix requires two steps. First: at every lease renewal, read the renewal option clause specifically for base year reset language. Second: when a reset is found, update the base year figure in Yardi, MRI, or RealPage before the first reconciliation cycle of the new term runs.

Neither Yardi nor MRI will flag a stale base year. The system calculates the excess using whatever base year is configured, correctly and silently, in the wrong direction.

On a portfolio of 50 tenants where 10% renew per year with missed resets, the cumulative overcharge compounds. Five years in, you have five tenants with stale base years each accumulating $20,000 to $35,000 per year in overbilling. That's the kind of finding that triggers a forensic audit of the entire portfolio.
