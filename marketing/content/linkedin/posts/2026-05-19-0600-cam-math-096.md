A $72,000 CAM shift on a 250,000 SF building. That's what a 3% change in total rentable SF produces on a $2.4 million expense pool. And that's just the measurement change. before you update any denominators or notify any tenants.

BOMA 2024 adoption is a 6 to 12-month process, not a system update. The mistake most portfolios make is treating remeasurement as a configuration task and skipping the steps that determine whether the measurement change is actually recoverable.

Three decisions before the system update: review lease language (does each tenant's lease say "BOMA standards as amended" or does it reference a specific version?), run dual measurements (you need both BOMA 2017 and BOMA 2024 numbers to show tenants the before-and-after), and model the recovery impact per tenant before you notify anyone.

For a 200,000 SF office building, the before-and-after might show that Tenant A's CAM charge decreases by $3,058 because their pro-rata share dropped from 6.045% to 5.906% as the denominator grew. That information needs to be in a letter to Tenant A at least 90 days before the new measurement takes effect, not in the reconciliation statement after the fact.

The cost of getting this right: $13,500 to $39,500 per property (remeasurement, lease review, tenant notification, system updates, parallel testing). The cost of getting it wrong: tenant disputes, lease audit challenges, and potential restatement of prior-year reconciliations that used the wrong measurement standard.

Do not update the system denominator before you know which leases permit the update.
