The Yardi Voyager export is not the hard part of CAM reconciliation.

It is, however, the part where most teams introduce errors that don't surface until Q1 crunch.

Three exports do most of the work for CAM verification: the Recovery Analysis Report, the General Ledger Analytics Export, and the Rent Roll with Lease Charges. Each one has a specific purpose. Using the wrong one, or using the right one with the wrong parameters, produces data that looks complete and isn't.

The Recovery Analysis Report shows what Yardi calculated for each recovery pool. It is the output of Yardi's configuration. correct if the configuration is correct, wrong if it isn't.

The General Ledger Analytics Export shows what actually hit the GL. This is the raw expense data before recovery pool processing. The gap between the GL and the Recovery Analysis Report is where configuration errors live.

The five places exports break down: date range mismatch (accrual period doesn't match cash entries), entity filter too narrow (intercompany allocations don't appear), account scope incomplete (GL accounts added mid-year weren't included), format changes after a Yardi update (column order shifted, column names changed), and pre-close timing (running the export before month-end close captures incomplete data).

Exporting from Yardi is straightforward. Exporting from Yardi in a way that captures the right data, with the right parameters, for the right period, from the right entities. that requires knowing exactly which failure modes apply to your installation.

The export is the starting point for independent verification. If the starting point is wrong, the verification is wrong.
