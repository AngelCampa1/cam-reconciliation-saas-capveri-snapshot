"We need better CAM reconciliation software" gets a polite nod from the CFO and no budget. It sounds like a quality-of-life improvement for the accounting team.

The request that gets approved looks different. It quantifies the financial impact in three numbers.

Using modeled assumptions for a 20-property portfolio:

Additional recovery. For a 20-property office portfolio with $38M in recoverable expenses and an 87% recovery ratio against an industry benchmark of 92%, the gap is $1.9M per year. Software conservatively captures 25-40% of that gap $475,000 to $760,000 in annual recovery improvement.

Reduced audit exposure. At eight tenant audit requests per year, with 65% finding errors averaging $35,000, and settlements at 60% of findings, the current annual audit cost is $109,200. If pre-statement error detection drops the finding rate to 20%, audit cost falls to $33,600. That's $75,600 in annual savings, plus internal labor.

Controller time savings. Manual reconciliation runs 20-40 hours per property per year. With software it's 8-15 hours. For 20 properties, that's 240-500 hours redirected to lease analysis, acquisition support, and ownership reporting. not copying formulas in Excel.

The combined conservative estimate: $568,000 in annual benefit. Against a validation-only tool that costs $4,000-$10,000 per year.

ROI of 57:1 to 221:1.

The CFO's three follow-up questions always come down to: how confident are you in the recovery estimate, what happens if you don't do this, and can we try it before committing?

That last one is the easiest: run one property through independent verification. If the findings are worth more than the annual subscription, the business case proves itself.
