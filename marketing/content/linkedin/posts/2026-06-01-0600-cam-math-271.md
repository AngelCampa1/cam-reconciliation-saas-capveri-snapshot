Tenant audit firms routinely find material discrepancies in the reconciliation statements they audit. A significant share of landlords are billing incorrectly right now.

The five errors that account for most of the dollar damage: non-recoverable expenses in the pool, gross-up applied to fixed costs, CAM caps not enforced, wrong pro-rata denominator, and partial-year proration errors.

None of these are exotic. None require complex lease language to detect. They're all identifiable in a pre-send review that most teams don't run.

In modeled scenarios across typical portfolio sizes, the per-building recovery gap often runs $15,000-$30,000 per year. the difference between what a building should collect and what it actually collects, after netting over-billings and under-billings, represents money that flows neither to the landlord nor into a dispute. It quietly disappears into billing errors that neither side catches.

For a 20-building portfolio, that's $300,000-$600,000 per year. Capitalized at a 6% cap rate, it's $5-10 million in understated portfolio value.

The math doesn't require a complex investment thesis. It requires asking whether anyone has checked the reconciliation methodology against the current lease terms in the last 12 months.
