SLIDE 1:
The 7 things tenant auditors check first
(And how to find every one of them yourself)

SLIDE 2:
Finding 1: CapEx coded as OpEx
Roof replacements. HVAC installs. Parking lot resurfacing.
Auditors sort your GL by dollar amount and look for keywords: "replace," "new," "install."
Dollar impact: $15,000-$100,000+ per occurrence.

SLIDE 3:
Finding 2: Gross-up on fixed expenses
Property taxes and insurance do not vary with occupancy.
Grossing them up overbills tenants in every below-threshold year.
This is the most commonly cited finding in tenant audit letters.

SLIDE 4:
Finding 3: Management fee overstatement
Fee calculated on the wrong base. 3% of gross vs. 3% of net.
On a $1M expense pool, that difference is $12,000 per year.
Or an admin fee charged on top of a management fee when the lease allows only one.

SLIDE 5:
Finding 4: Expenses outside the lease definition
Legal fees. Leasing commissions. Tenant-specific work billed to the common pool.
Auditors map every GL account in your CAM pool to the lease's operating expense definition.
Anything that doesn't map is a finding.

SLIDE 6:
Finding 5: Pro-rata share errors
Wrong tenant SF. Wrong building denominator. Partial-year not prorated.
A 1% pro-rata error on a $1.5M expense pool is $15,000 per tenant per year.
On a 10-year lease: $150,000+.

SLIDE 7:
Findings 6 and 7: Cap violations and base year anomalies
Cap: year-over-year increases exceeding the lease limit.
Base year: set during a construction period or vacancy year, permanently suppressing the tenant's threshold.
Both errors compound. Neither gets caught until an auditor looks.

SLIDE 8:
None of these are math errors.
The formulas are usually right. The inputs are wrong.
Run the same seven checks before your statements go out.
