A 180,000 SF Class A office building. Fourteen tenants. One wrong cell reference in a SUM formula.

The range should have been =SUM(A12:A52). Someone typed =SUM(A12:A43,A45:A52). Cell A44, a $142,000 annual insurance premium. fell through the gap.

Here is an illustrative scenario based on common reconciliation error patterns: the error goes unnoticed for three years. The recovery ratio drops from 91% to 84%. The controller assumes it is vacancy-driven and compensates by grossing up insurance more aggressively to "make up" the shortfall. Which is wrong. insurance is a fixed expense that should never be grossed up.

In this modeled scenario, the gross-up overcharge runs $82,000 per year. Over three years: $247,000 in overbilled CAM.

A tenant auditor finds it in 30 minutes. It is one of the five standard checks on every audit engagement.

Refunds total $247,000. Five other tenants hire the same firm after word gets around. The largest tenant. 23% of the building. does not renew.

Two errors, one root cause: nobody verified the formulas inherited from the previous controller.

Recovery ratio variance is a signal. A 7-point drop without a corresponding lease or vacancy change means something is wrong with the reconciliation, not the market. Investigate before you compensate.
