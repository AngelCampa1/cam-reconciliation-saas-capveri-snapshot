The contrarian point on denominator changes: most landlords document them only when a tenant disputes them. That's exactly backwards.

A denominator change. from a BOMA remeasurement, an anchor departure, or a common area reclassification. shifts every tenant's pro-rata share simultaneously. Every tenant receives a different bill from the prior year, for reasons that appear nowhere in the statement.

A 3.3% pro-rata share shift on a $1.5M expense pool is a $45,000 annual reallocation across tenants. On an individual 10% tenant, that's $4,500 per year in unexpected billing change. The tenant's immediate interpretation: billing error.

There are five denominator change triggers that require documentation: BOMA remeasurement, anchor departure or space reclassification, common area expansion, tenant expansion that changes the denominator pool, and BOMA standard update. Any one of these should produce a denominator change memo that goes to every affected tenant before the reconciliation statement, not after the dispute letter.

The memo doesn't need to be long. It needs to answer three questions: what changed, why it changed, and how it affects each tenant's pro-rata share specifically.

Proactive documentation turns a billing surprise into a line-item explanation. Reactive documentation turns a billing surprise into a formal audit request.
