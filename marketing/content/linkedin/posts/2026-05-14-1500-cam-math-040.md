SLIDE 1:
How to calculate CAM for a tenant who moved in mid-year
(The four-step method that survives audit)

SLIDE 2:
The first mistake: using the lease execution date instead of the commencement date.
The commencement date is when the tenant's financial obligations begin. The execution date is when they signed. These can be weeks apart. Use commencement.

SLIDE 3:
The second mistake: using months instead of days.
A tenant who commenced June 15 is in occupancy for 200 days (June 15 through December 31), not 7 months. Use actual days for precision. $12,000 annual CAM × (200/365) = $6,575.34. Using 7/12 gives you $7,000. a $424 overbilling.

SLIDE 4:
Step 1: Confirm the commencement date from the signed lease, not the system.
System entry dates drift. The lease commencement date is the legal trigger.

SLIDE 5:
Step 2: Count actual days in occupancy.
Days from commencement through year-end. Use a date calculator. Don't estimate by month.

SLIDE 6:
Step 3: Calculate the partial-year fraction.
Days in occupancy / 365 = the fraction. Apply that fraction to the annual CAM charge after all pro-rata share and gross-up calculations are complete.

SLIDE 7:
Step 4: Document the commencement date and day count in the reconciliation workpapers.
Tenant auditors check this. They ask for the lease. If your numbers don't match the lease commencement date, you're explaining an error, not a methodology.

SLIDE 8:
The rule: calculate annual first, prorate second.
Run the full-year CAM pool calculation, apply pro-rata share, then apply the days fraction.
Prorating before applying the annual pool creates a different. And Wrong. result.
