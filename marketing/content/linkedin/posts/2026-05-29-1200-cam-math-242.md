In an illustrative cost analysis for a 22-property portfolio running manual reconciliation for over a decade:

The property management company had been running CAM reconciliation manually for eleven years. Twenty-two properties. One controller, two analysts. Reconciliation season ran January through May. Every year.

The new CFO asked a question the team had never answered: what does this cost us?

The direct cost calculation was straightforward. Controller time: 40 hours per year on reconciliation management and review. Analyst time: 12 hours per property × 22 properties = 264 hours. At fully-loaded cost of $55/hour: $16,720 in direct labor.

The indirect cost was harder to quantify but more significant. In three of the prior five years, the company had resolved formal tenant disputes. Two disputes went through outside counsel review. One dispute required amended reconciliation statements and a $31,000 settlement. Total dispute-related costs across five years: approximately $87,000, or $17,400 per year averaged.

Combined: $34,120 per year to run reconciliation on 22 properties. That's $1,551 per property per year in direct and dispute-related costs.

The CFO also found, during the dispute review, that the company had been systematically underbilling one property for three years. The gross-up formula had been applied to the variable pool only. correct. But The variable pool had been configured to exclude management fees, which for this particular property's lease were variable and should have been included. Annual underbilling: approximately $14,800. Three years: $44,400 in unrecovered income.

The reconciliation was running on time, within budget, and under the team's quality targets. No one had noticed the underbilling because it wasn't a line item that generated a tenant complaint. The tenants were paying correct amounts. The building was leaving money on the table.

The total cost picture: $34,120 per year in operating costs, plus $14,800 per year in unrecovered income from a single configuration error, plus the unquantified risk premium from running a process with no independent verification.

The business case for independent verification software doesn't require a compelling ROI pitch. It requires answering the CFO's question: what does the manual process actually cost?
