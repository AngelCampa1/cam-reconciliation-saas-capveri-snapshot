A perfectly accurate CAM reconciliation statement delivered 30 days late can cost a landlord the right to collect $50,000 in true-up billings. The math doesn't matter if the timing fails.

Most commercial leases require statements within 90 to 120 days of year-end. For December 31 properties, that's March 31 to April 30. The lease's reconciliation clause determines which applies. not industry convention, not what you did last year.

Three lease-type variations worth knowing:

Retail leases, especially national tenant leases, often specify 90 days with a "deemed correct" provision: if the tenant doesn't exercise audit rights within the window, the statement is final and binding regardless of errors. These are enforceable. Miss your delivery deadline and the tenant may refuse the true-up demand entirely.

Office leases generally allow 120 to 180 days. Class A office leases increasingly include a notice provision that the landlord must send 60 days before the audit window closes, reminding the tenant their rights are expiring.

Industrial leases typically track to 90 to 120 days but have lower dispute rates. When industrial tenants do dispute, it's almost always a capital item (dock leveler replacement, major roof repair) in the operating CAM pool.

The audit window starts when the statement is "delivered" per the lease's notice provisions, not when you sent it or when the tenant acknowledges it. Certified mail or email with read receipt, logged with a delivery confirmation, is the minimum documentation for proving the clock has started.

Working backward from your April 30 deadline: January 15 GL export complete. February 15 pool calculations done. March 15 statements drafted and reviewed. April 1 delivery with two weeks of buffer.

Build the buffer. The last ten days before a deadline are when errors happen.
