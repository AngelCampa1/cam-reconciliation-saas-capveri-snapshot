HOOK (0:00-0:08):
CAM expense caps don't work the same way in every lease. Here's the difference between the three types. And Why using the wrong one costs money on both sides.

TALKING POINT 1 (0:09-0:28):
Type one: the non-cumulative cap. This limits the year-over-year increase in controllable expenses. Prior year actual × (1 + cap rate) = this year's ceiling. If actual expenses hit the ceiling, the landlord absorbs the rest. If actual comes in below, the tenant pays actual. And No capacity carries forward. The ceiling resets every year based on what was billed last year.

TALKING POINT 2 (0:29-0:50):
Type two: the cumulative cap. This limits total growth from the original base year. The ceiling is Base × (1 + rate)^N, compounding annually. The difference from non-cumulative: unused ceiling capacity carries forward. If the landlord undercharges one year, the ceiling continues rising. In a future year with a spike, the landlord can draw on the carryforward bank. This is more landlord-favorable and requires a running ledger.

TALKING POINT 3 (0:51-1:10):
Type three: the base year cap. The tenant pays only the amount by which controllable expenses exceed the base year amount. The base year is fixed at the beginning of the lease. Future years are compared against that floor. there's no compounding ceiling, just the base. This type is most sensitive to anomaly years: a 2020 or 2021 base year with pandemic-level occupancy creates an artificially low floor.

TALKING POINT 4 (1:11-1:30):
Applying the wrong cap type produces under-recovery for the landlord or over-billing for the tenant, depending on the direction of the error. Most ERPs have a configuration field for cap type. Verify it against the signed lease before each reconciliation. not against what was configured when the lease was originally entered.

CLOSING (1:31-1:45):
Three cap types. Three different math paths. One check before the reconciliation runs: pull the cap clause from the lease and confirm the system configuration matches.

ON-SCREEN TEXT:
Non-cumulative: prior year actual × (1 + rate) = ceiling (resets annually)
Cumulative: base year × (1 + rate)^N = ceiling (carries forward)
Base year: tenant pays excess above base (no compounding)
Cap type drives the math. verify it against the lease every year
