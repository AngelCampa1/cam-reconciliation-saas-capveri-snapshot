The contrarian take on mid-year move-in CAM calculations: the formula is simple, but three of the four inputs are routinely wrong.

The formula: tenant's annual obligation = full-year obligation × (days in occupancy / 365). Straightforward. But "days in occupancy" depends on the commencement date. which is not always the rent commencement date, not always the lease execution date, and often not what's in the ERP.

Most leases use one of three triggers: possession date, rent commencement date, or lease commencement date. A tenant with a free-rent period may receive keys in month one but not start paying base rent until month four. In most leases, CAM starts at possession. not at rent commencement. Every month of free rent where CAM wasn't billed is a gap.

The second input problem: the denominator. When a tenant moves in mid-year, the leasable denominator for other tenants should change from that date forward. Most ERP systems handle this automatically only if the lease setup is correct. When it's wrong, every other tenant's pro-rata share is understated for the remainder of the year.

The correct verification step: after any mid-year move-in, pull the commencement date from the signed lease. not the system. And Verify it matches the ERP. Then confirm the denominator update is reflected in all subsequent estimate calculations.

It takes fifteen minutes and prevents a class of errors that are nearly invisible until year-end.
