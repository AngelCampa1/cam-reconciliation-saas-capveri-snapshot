The following is an illustrative scenario based on a common GL categorization error pattern:

The tenant auditor had been at the building for three years without finding anything significant. Standard lease, straightforward CAM pool, no anchor exclusions. Annual reconciliation statements delivered by March 15 every year, backed by GL exports organized by vendor.

Year four: the auditor flagged a $78,000 HVAC line item. Not because the amount was unusual. HVAC maintenance had been in the $60,000 to $80,000 range for three years. Because the transaction description said "roof-mount unit replacement, Building B."

Building B was not the property the tenant was leasing. It was a different property in the same management company's portfolio.

The invoice had been cross-coded to the wrong property GL in 2023. The property controller had done a GL review before the reconciliation but had not checked vendor invoices against the property address. The amount was plausible. it matched the HVAC range. The vendor was one of the regular contractors. Nothing flagged.

The auditor requested the underlying invoice as part of a standard documentation request. The property address on the invoice was the first line.

The tenant's CAM share was 14%. Their exposure on the $78,000 item: $10,920. Two prior years had been outside the audit window. For the year in question, they received an $11,200 credit after accounting for interest provisions.

The GL review step that would have caught this: after pulling large transactions, verify the vendor invoice against the property. A $78,000 HVAC invoice for a building that isn't yours is immediately visible when you open the invoice. Most GL reviews check the account, the vendor, and the amount. Very few check the property on the invoice.

The counterintuitive finding from cross-property coding errors: they don't always appear as unusual amounts. They look exactly like normal expenses because they are normal expenses. just for a different building. The only detection method is checking the source document.

A documentation-request-ready GL file, where vendor invoices are filed by date and accessible by account. makes the verification step trivial. The 60 seconds to open an invoice and confirm the property address is the entire check.
