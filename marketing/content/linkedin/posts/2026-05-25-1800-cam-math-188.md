The biggest risk in batch CAM reconciliation isn't getting one property wrong. It's getting one property's data into another property's calculation.

An analyst working on five properties simultaneously, pulling GL exports that all look similar because they come from the same Yardi instance. Property A's insurance allocation lands in Property B's pool. The account codes match. The amounts look reasonable. The statement goes out.

Context switching errors in batch processing are harder to catch than calculation errors because nothing looks wrong. The numbers reconcile. The math is correct. The wrong data just went into the correct formula.

The contrarian point: the standard advice for multi-property reconciliation is "start early and prioritize by deadline." That's correct but incomplete.

What actually prevents batch errors: property-specific workspaces. Separate folders. Separate calculation files. A checklist that requires confirming property-specific data, property name, address, GL account list, denominator. at the start of each property, not inherited from the prior one.

Start complex properties first, not simple ones. Counter-intuitive, but important: complex properties need more review cycles. Starting them early gives review time. Starting them last makes them the final bottleneck. And The ones most likely to produce statement delivery deadline pressure.
