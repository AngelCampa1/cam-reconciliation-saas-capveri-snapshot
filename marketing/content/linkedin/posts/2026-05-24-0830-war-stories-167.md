The conventional wisdom in CAM audit defense is to fight every claim.

The data on CAM audit outcomes suggests a different approach works better.

Disputes that resolve quickly and cheaply share a pattern: the property manager acknowledges confirmed errors immediately, organizes documentation before the auditor asks for it, and responds to contested items with lease citations rather than assertions.

Disputes that become expensive share the opposite pattern: the property manager disputes everything including the errors that are clearly correct, provides documentation slowly or incompletely, and treats the auditor as an adversary rather than a verification process.

CAM audit settlements typically land well below the initial claim. The difference is almost entirely about how the dispute was handled procedurally, not about the merits of the underlying billing.

Landlords who separate the question "is this charge correct?" from the question "how do we respond to this request?" consistently do better. Correct charges get defended with documentation. Incorrect charges get credited immediately. Contested charges get a written explanation with the lease clause.

That three-bucket approach. correct, incorrect, contested, is not soft. It is a negotiation strategy grounded in the fact that audit firms get paid on contingency. Every confirmed error that gets credited removes that dollar amount from their fee base. Fast, organized responses reduce the time the auditor spends on the engagement,. Limits the scope of what they find.

Fighting confirmed errors extends the dispute. The auditor has more time, the relationship deteriorates, and other tenants notice.
