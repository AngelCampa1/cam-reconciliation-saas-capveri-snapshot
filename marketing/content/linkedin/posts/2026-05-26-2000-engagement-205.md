The 3-5% CAM recovery leakage figure gets cited often. It's worth being specific about what it actually means.

The figure comes from industry estimates derived from tenant audit firm published findings and operating-expense benchmarks, which put typical leakage at up to 3.5% by property type. It represents the gap between operating expense recoveries that are contractually owed to landlords under executed leases and the recoveries that are actually billed and collected.

It's not a figure about tenant underpayment. Tenants typically pay what they're billed. The leakage is on the billing side: recoveries that were owed under the lease but weren't captured in the reconciliation.

Here's what that looks like in practice.

Scenario 1: The gross-up was not applied when it should have been.
The lease permits normalization to 95% occupancy. The property ran at 93% during the reconciliation period. The gross-up factor was set to 1.0 in the ERP because the prior year's actual occupancy was already at 95%. The factor wasn't updated for the current year. The landlord forfeited the normalization recovery they were entitled to.

Scenario 2: An expense exclusion is too broad.
The lease excludes "capital expenditures." The property accountant, reading "capital expenditures" conservatively, also excludes a major HVAC repair that qualified as an operating expense under the lease. The repair cost isn't recovered. The tenant benefits from an exclusion the lease didn't grant.

Scenario 3: The pro-rata denominator is understated. A new tenant was added to the floor with a higher measured square footage than the prior tenant. The denominator was updated for the new lease. Not retroactively applied to the reconciliation for the period before the new lease commenced. The other tenants on the floor received a larger share of the operating costs than they were responsible for.

Each of these errors runs in the landlord's disfavor. None of them involve overcharging tenants. All of them are the result of configuration that didn't match current lease terms.

The 3-5% is not evenly distributed between over- and under-recovery. The errors that produce tenant disputes are the ones that get tracked. The errors that run against the landlord are often invisible until someone runs the independent calculation.
