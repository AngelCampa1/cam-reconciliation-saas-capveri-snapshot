A number from our work building CapVeri: the pro-rata denominator is the source of error in more portfolios than the gross-up calculation.

Gross-up gets more attention because it's more visible. The factor is a discrete number, easy to audit, easy to point to when it's wrong.

The denominator issue is subtler. The total rentable area used to calculate each tenant's pro-rata share is set when the lease is executed, based on the measurement at the time. Remeasurements happen. New leases on the floor add square footage. BOMA methodology changes between lease generations.

If the denominator for an existing tenant was set under an older measurement standard and a new tenant on the same floor was measured under BOMA 2024, the pro-rata math is inconsistent. Both tenants are being allocated based on denominators that don't refer to the same thing.

The dollar impact depends on the floor size and the number of tenants. On a 50,000 square foot floor with six tenants, a denominator that's off by 1,200 square feet shifts approximately 2.4% of the total CAM pool allocation. Across a $300,000 CAM pool, that's $7,200 per year, distributed incorrectly across all tenants on the floor.

This is the kind of error that doesn't produce a tenant dispute immediately. It produces a tenant dispute in Year 5 when an auditor does the measurement comparison.
