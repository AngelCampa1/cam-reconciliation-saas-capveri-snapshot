The contrarian view on CAM billing KPIs: most portfolios measure the wrong thing.

Recovery ratio. actual CAM collected divided by the total recoverable pool. is reported as a performance metric. A 92% recovery ratio sounds good. What it doesn't capture: whether the 8% shortfall is from uncollectable tenants or from systematic underbilling errors that the team doesn't know about.

Dispute rate below 5% is considered excellent. But dispute rate measures tenant-initiated disputes, not errors. A portfolio where tenants don't audit but still have 40% of reconciliations containing material errors has a 0% dispute rate and a significant liability.

The metric most portfolios don't track: recovery gap. the difference between what the reconciliation should have produced, calculated from a correct methodology, and what it actually billed. This requires an independent calculation to measure. Without it, you're tracking compliance with your own math, not accuracy against the lease.

Cycle time under 75 days is accurate. But cycle time starting from GL close to last statement delivery can be under 75 days and still have 30% of statements with calculation errors. because fast isn't the same as accurate.

The four KPIs worth tracking: recovery ratio, dispute rate, cycle time from GL close, and error rate on a sample basis. The error rate is the only one that measures actual reconciliation quality. The others measure velocity and surface.
