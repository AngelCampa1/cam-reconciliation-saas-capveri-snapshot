When a tenant audit firm sends its opening documentation request, the 72-hour response window matters more than what happens in the following weeks.

The pattern in CAM audit outcomes is consistent: landlords who respond within 72 hours with organized, complete documentation consistently resolve disputes at the lower end of the settlement range. Landlords who take two or three weeks to respond, or who provide documents piecemeal, give the audit firm time to find additional issues. And Signal that the property management operation may have other documentation problems worth examining.

The 72-hour response does not have to be a complete resolution. It needs to be: acknowledgment of receipt, identification of the responsible party, and a realistic response date. That structure alone changes the tenor of the engagement.

What the complete response should include: the GL detail for every year under audit, the reconciliation calculation with all inputs shown, the lease's operating expense definition, the gross-up calculation with occupancy documentation, the pro-rata denominator history, and the cap bank history if a cap applies.

Disputes that become expensive almost always share the same early-stage characteristic: the first response was slow, incomplete, or defensive. Slow and incomplete invite follow-up requests. Defensive signals that the landlord does not believe the calculation is correct, which encourages the auditor to look harder.

CAM audit settlements typically come in well below the initial claim -- the difference often determined by how the first 30 days of the dispute are managed.
