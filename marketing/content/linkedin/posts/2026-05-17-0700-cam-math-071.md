The gross-up formula is three lines of math. The five ways it gets applied incorrectly are not about the math: they're about classification and sequencing.

Here's the correct framework, followed by what breaks it.

The formula:
Grossed-Up Variable Expenses = Variable Expenses / Actual Occupancy % × Target Occupancy %

On a 200,000 SF building at 78% occupancy with a 95% gross-up target and $1,200,000 in variable CAM expenses:
Grossed-Up Amount = $1,200,000 / 0.78 × 0.95 = $1,461,538

The gross-up adds $261,538 to the recoverable pool. Without it, tenants in a half-empty building pay far less than they'd owe at normal occupancy, and the landlord absorbs the gap.

Step 1: Classify every expense as variable or fixed before the formula runs. Variable expenses scale with occupancy: janitorial, utilities, security, and landscaping. Fixed expenses don't: property taxes, insurance, management fees (at a fixed dollar amount). Applying the gross-up to the entire expense pool inflates fixed costs that have nothing to do with occupancy. That's both mathematically wrong and a lease violation.

Step 2: Use the correct occupancy metric.
The lease specifies which occupancy number to use. Most leases call for economic occupancy (rent-paying tenants as a percentage of total leasable area). Physical occupancy counts suites that are occupied but may be in free-rent periods. Using physical when the lease requires economic changes the gross-up factor, sometimes materially.

Step 3: Apply gross-up to the building expense pool, not to individual tenant shares.
The gross-up adjusts the total recoverable pool. Individual tenant pro-rata shares are applied after the pool is grossed up. Grossing up each tenant's share independently produces a different (incorrect) result.

Step 4: Check the threshold, not just the formula.
If actual occupancy already meets or exceeds the gross-up target, the factor is 1.0. No adjustment required. A gross-up factor above 1.0 at full occupancy inflates the pool beyond 100% of normalized expenses, which isn't possible.

Step 5: Confirm the base year gross-up for base-year leases.
When the lease uses a base year structure, the base year expenses should also be grossed up to the target occupancy before they become the tenant's floor. A base year that wasn't normalized understates the floor, inflating the tenant's excess charge in every subsequent year.

Gross-up applied to fixed costs is the most common and most expensive error in CAM tenant audits. On a $2 million expense pool, that's a six-figure dispute waiting for a tenant auditor to find it.
