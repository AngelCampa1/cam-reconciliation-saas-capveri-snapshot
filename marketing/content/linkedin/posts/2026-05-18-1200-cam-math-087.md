The admin fee clause in a commercial lease has three different calculation methods, and they produce three different numbers on the same $500,000 expense pool. Using the wrong one creates either a tenant dispute or uncollected revenue.

Here's the framework for each method.

Method 1: Net method.
Admin fee = Rate × Pool
At 15%: $500,000 × 0.15 = $75,000
The fee is straightforward. The base is the expense pool before the admin fee is added.

Method 2: Gross method.
The gross method solves for a fee that represents the specified percentage of the total (pool plus fee). The formula is circular: the fee is a percentage of a number that includes the fee, so you solve algebraically:
Fee = Rate × Base / (1 - Rate)
At 15%: $500,000 × 0.15 / (1 - 0.15) = $88,235
That's $13,235 more than the net method on the same expense pool.

Most billing systems calculate $75,000 and stop. The gross method is what the lease requires when the clause says "15% of total CAM including the fee." Without solving the circularity, the landlord leaves $13,235 per year uncollected on every lease with this language.

Method 3: Capped method.
The fee is calculated by either method but cannot exceed a specified dollar ceiling (for example, $60,000). If actual expenses surge 20%, the fee calculation might produce $90,000 but the cap limits collection to $60,000.

The sequencing problem: when does the admin fee get added relative to the cap?

If the lease applies the controllable expense cap before adding the admin fee, the fee is calculated on the capped amount. If the fee is added first and then the cap applied, the cap may be partially offset by the fee. The lease specifies the sequence. Getting the sequence wrong changes the tenant's total obligation.

The double-counting trap: both a management fee and an admin fee appearing in the same CAM pool when the lease permits only one. The management fee compensates the property management company for running the building. The admin fee is a separate administrative markup the landlord charges on top of operating expenses. Some leases explicitly permit both. Most commercial leases permit one or the other. Both appearing is an immediate tenant audit finding.

In Texas, undisclosed fee methods can void the entire assessment under Property Code §93.012. The fee must be disclosed and calculated correctly or the landlord risks losing the entire recovery for the period.
