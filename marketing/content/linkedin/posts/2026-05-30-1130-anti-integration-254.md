In our experience reviewing portfolio reconciliation workflows, the majority of CAM reconciliation cycle time is not calculation time. It is waiting time, verification time, and rework time.

The bottlenecks: waiting for AP to confirm invoice allocations, waiting for confirmation that a GL account is correctly classified, manually verifying that the gross-up formula matches the lease after someone updated the spreadsheet, regenerating statements after a discrepancy is found post-draft.

The calculation itself. applying gross-up, computing pro-rata shares, checking caps. is a small fraction of the total cycle. The work around the calculation is where the 8-week Q1 reconciliation schedule comes from.

Five tactics that address the actual bottlenecks:

Pre-close preparation: finalize GL account classifications before year-end close, not during reconciliation. Every account that needs a classification decision during reconciliation adds a day of waiting.

Parallel processing: run initial reconciliation calculations as soon as the GL closes, while statement formatting and tenant communication are being prepared simultaneously.

Validate before drafting: run the full independent verification comparison before generating any draft statements. Finding a gross-up error after 30 statements are drafted means 30 statements to regenerate.

Standardized outputs: build reconciliation statement templates before Q1. The time lost to formatting decisions and formatting consistency across a portfolio is underestimated.

Batch tenant communications: one Q1 schedule notification to all tenants, one statement batch, one response window. Drip communications extend the cycle and fragment the team's attention.

The calculation is not the constraint. The process around the calculation is.
