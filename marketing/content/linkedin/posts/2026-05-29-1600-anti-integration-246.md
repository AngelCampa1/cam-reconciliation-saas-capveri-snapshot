Modeled scenario: a 20-property portfolio uses an independent lease-vs-ERP comparison and identifies $475,000 in recoverable income that the ERP configuration was not delivering.

The business case for CAM software is not only labor savings. The real case is recovery protection.

For a 20-property portfolio with $2M in annual operating expenses across properties, systematic underbilling or overbilling compounds every year. Each year a configuration error goes undetected, it produces the same wrong recovery outcome again.

Additional recovery from error correction:
Independent verification can reveal excluded accounts that should not be excluded, denominators that do not match leases, gross-up methodology applied to the wrong expense types, or cap settings that do not match signed terms.

That is not aggressive billing. It is billing what the leases allow.

Audit defense savings:
Tenant auditors often work on contingency. A clean audit trail can resolve many disputes before they become formal audits. If an audit proceeds, complete documentation narrows the dispute faster.

Controller time reallocation:
Spreadsheet-based reconciliation can consume hundreds of controller hours per year across a portfolio. Software does not eliminate that work. It shifts hours from manual reconciliation to review, exception handling, budget variance analysis, and lease negotiation support.

Illustrative first-year impact:
Conservative case: $475,000 additional recovery, $75,600 audit savings, and 240 hours of controller time at $65/hour. Total: $566,200.

Against a $12,000-$40,000 annual software cost, the modeled return can be material.

The hard part is not the math. It is admitting current statements may be wrong and creating a defensible way to find out.
