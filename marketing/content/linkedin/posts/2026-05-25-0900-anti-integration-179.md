SLIDE 1:
CAM reconciliation: build vs. buy.
The spreadsheet is not free. Here is the full cost comparison.

SLIDE 2:
The visible cost of Excel
Software license: effectively $0 (bundled with Office).
Purpose-built CAM software: $5,000-$25,000 per year for a 10-property portfolio.
This comparison is why "free" wins the initial budget conversation.
But it is the wrong comparison.

SLIDE 3:
The hidden cost of Excel: labor
A 10-property portfolio: 250 hours per year at $65 fully loaded = $16,250 in labor.
Purpose-built software for the same portfolio: 80 hours = $5,200.
Labor savings alone: $11,050 per year.
And this is before errors.

SLIDE 4:
The hidden cost of Excel: errors
Studies find error rates of 1-5% in cells containing formulas.
Wrong cell reference in pro-rata calculation: $2,000-$15,000 per tenant.
Gross-up applied to fixed expenses: $10,000-$50,000 per property.
Cap escalator not carried forward: $1,000-$5,000 per tenant per year.
A single gross-up error can exceed the annual cost of software for the entire portfolio.

SLIDE 5:
The hidden cost of Excel: key-person risk
The workbook was built by a controller who left two years ago.
The current team inherited it. Nobody fully understands the custom formulas.
When that institutional knowledge leaves, the typical cost: 2-4 weeks of delayed statements plus $5,000-$15,000 in consultant fees to reconstruct the process.

SLIDE 6:
The total cost comparison (10 properties)
Excel: $31,500-$72,250 per year.
Purpose-built software: $12,500-$36,300 per year.
The difference is labor + errors + audit defense + key-person risk.
None of these appear in the budget line that says "Excel = free."

SLIDE 7:
When to stay with Excel
Three properties or fewer, with simple NNN leases, no gross-up clauses, no cumulative cap provisions, and a stable team that will not lose the person who maintains the workbook.
That is a narrow set of conditions.

SLIDE 8:
Above five properties, the cost of "free" exceeds the cost of software.
The software pays for itself when it eliminates 170-plus hours of labor and prevents one or two material errors per year.
For most portfolios, that happens in year one.
