A $1.1M expense pool losing $11,000 for every percentage point of recovery shortfall - and most controllers can't tell you their recovery ratio without running a new report.

These four KPIs measure whether the reconciliation process is working, or just producing statements.

KPI 1: Recovery ratio.
Formula: Total CAM billed / Total recoverable operating expenses.
Benchmarks: NNN office 85-95%, retail 80-90%, industrial 90-98%. Below these floors is a red flag.

Every percentage point of recovery on a $1.1 million expense pool is $11,000 per year. At a 6% cap rate, one point of recovery is worth $183,333 in property value. The five most common causes of low recovery: GL accounts not mapped to recovery pools, CapEx over-exclusion, gross-up not applied at low occupancy, new expense categories never added to billing, and anchor exclusions reducing the billable base.

KPI 2: Dispute rate.
Formula: Number of tenant disputes / Number of reconciliation statements sent × 100.
Acceptable range: 5-15%. Below 5% is excellent. Above 25% indicates billing errors, not just difficult tenants.

Each dispute costs 4-8 hours of controller time to research and resolve. A formal tenant audit consumes 20-40 hours. At $75/hour fully loaded, a 20% dispute rate on a 50-tenant building (10 disputes) costs $3,000 to $6,000 in labor before any financial adjustments.

KPI 3: Cycle time.
Formula: Days from fiscal year-end to final reconciliation statement delivery.
accurate: under 75 days. Good: 75-90. Acceptable: 90-120. Concerning: 120-180. Critical: over 180.

A 20-building portfolio with a 150-day average cycle time has more than $300,000 in delayed receivables (assuming $15,000 average true-up per building). Late delivery also extends tenant audit exposure indefinitely in leases where the audit clock doesn't start until a compliant statement is received.

KPI 4: Error rate.
Formula: Number of amended reconciliation statements / Total statements sent × 100.
Excellent: under 2%. Acceptable: 2-5%. Concerning: 5-10%. A tenant who receives a corrected statement will audit every future statement. The auditor knows you've had errors.

Track all four at the property level, quarterly. Year-over-year trends matter more than absolute numbers. A recovery ratio declining from 93% to 88% over two years signals a growing problem even though 88% is still within range.

One building in a portfolio with low recovery, above-average disputes, slow cycle time, and elevated errors is the first building to self-audit.
