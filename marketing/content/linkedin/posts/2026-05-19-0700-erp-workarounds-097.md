Yardi is not miscalculating your CAM. It is calculating exactly what you told it to calculate.

That distinction matters. When a reconciliation does not match the lease, the instinct is to blame the software. But Yardi only knows the recovery codes, pools, dates, caps, and thresholds entered into its records.

Six configuration errors create most of the variance:

1. Recovery pool GL account mismatch.
A new GL account is added mid-year, but no one adds it to the recovery pool. The expense stops flowing into reconciliation.

2. Charge code to recovery code mismatch.
The tenant receives a CAM charge, but it calculates from the wrong expense base. The statement looks normal. The amount is wrong.

3. Occupancy snapshot instead of weighted average.
A December 31 snapshot can miss a mid-year vacancy or expansion. On a large pool, that denominator shift can move tens of thousands of dollars.

4. Gross-up disabled or misconfigured.
At 72% occupancy with $380,000 in variable expenses, gross-up can materially change recovery. If the setting is wrong, the landlord absorbs the difference or bills too much.

5. Cap escalation base and method.
Cumulative and non-cumulative caps can match in Year 1 and diverge every year after.

6. Proration for mid-year lease events.
A November 1 move-in billed for a full year is overbilled. A February move-out billed for zero creates leakage.

The validation workflow: pick three tenants, including one large tenant, one small tenant, and one mid-year event. Export Yardi recovery detail. Rebuild the math manually from the same GL data and the signed lease.

This takes two to three hours per property. It catches errors that otherwise persist for years.
