A single gross-up misconfiguration found after 10 of 15 tenant statements are drafted means 3-5 hours of rework across the affected batch. The teams that finish reconciliation season fastest don't start earlier - they catch errors before drafting begins.

Here are the three categories where time actually disappears.

For a typical 15-property portfolio, the time distribution looks like this: waiting for GL close and data gathering (20-25%), manual ERP-vs-lease verification (25-30%), rework from errors discovered late (20-25%), statement drafting and formatting (15-20%), review and approval (10-15%). The first three categories account for 65-80% of total time. They are the ones worth attacking.

The highest-value single change: validate before drafting, not after. The most expensive time waste in reconciliation is discovering a pool-level error after drafting ten of fifteen tenant statements. A single gross-up error in the recovery pool requires reworking every tenant in that pool. The time cost of a validation pass before drafting is 30-60 minutes. The time cost of discovering a pool error after drafting is 3-5 hours of rework. These are not close.

The second change is pre-close preparation in November and December. The fastest possible reconciliation start date is January 1. But Only if everything except the GL data is ready. Most teams start gathering lease abstracts and ERP configuration data in January, adding 1-2 weeks before any calculation work begins. Lease terms, denominator updates, cap base year confirmations, and ERP configuration audits can all be completed before GL close. When January 1 arrives, data gathering is done.

The third change is parallel processing. Sequential completion, finish Property 1 before starting Property 2. is the default but rarely the fastest approach. Start the simplest properties simultaneously in Week 1 (single-tenant NNN, no gross-up, no cap provisions). Start mid-complexity properties in Week 2. Reserve Week 3-5 for complex properties with multi-pool structures and anchor tenant exclusions. The key constraint is staff capacity, not sequencing logic.

Three shortcuts that consistently create more problems than they save: skipping ERP configuration review before starting, accepting prior-year calculations without re-verifying lease terms, and sending statements as each property is completed rather than in coordinated batches. Configuration errors discovered mid-process require rework across all affected tenants. Leases renew and cap base years change. Staggered delivery means early tenants are already disputing while you are still drafting for the last properties.

The goal is not moving faster through the same process. It is eliminating the waiting, manual re-verification, and late-discovered rework that make the current process slow.
