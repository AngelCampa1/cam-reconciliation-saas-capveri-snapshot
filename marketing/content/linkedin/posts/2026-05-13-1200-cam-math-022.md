A mixed-use building with retail on the ground floor and office above cannot run a single CAM pool without creating a billing dispute on day one.

Here's the framework for running separate pools the right way.

Step 1: Define the pools by use type.
Retail CAM covers the retail tenants and the common areas they use: lobbies, restrooms, loading areas, and parking that serves the retail component. Office CAM covers the office tenants and their common areas. These pools are distinct even if the building shares a mechanical system.

Step 2: Categorize each expense as retail-only, office-only, or shared.
Property taxes and building insurance are shared expenses. Ground floor retail lobby maintenance is retail-only. Upper-floor corridor janitorial is office-only. HVAC maintenance may be shared if the system serves both use types, or separately tracked if the building has independent systems.

Step 3: Allocate shared expenses by SF ratio.
For expenses that genuinely serve both use types, allocate by the proportion of gross leasable area each component represents. If retail is 30% of GLA and office is 70%, shared expenses split 30/70 before going into each pool.

Step 4: Apply separate denominators for each pool.
The retail pool divides among retail tenants using the retail GLA as the denominator. The office pool divides among office tenants using the office GLA. Using the total building GLA as the denominator for both pools understates every tenant's share and under-recovers total expenses.

Step 5: Apply pool-specific gross-up and caps.
Gross-up calculations use occupancy data for the relevant use type, not the whole building. If retail occupancy was 80% and office was 92%, the retail pool gets grossed up to 95% for variable expenses. The office pool may not need gross-up at all. Caps are tenant-specific regardless of which pool the tenant draws from.

The most common error in mixed-use buildings: using the total building RSF as the denominator for both pools. A retail tenant on the ground floor should not be funding the office elevator lobby cleaning, and the office tenants should not be recovering costs from the ground-floor retail atrium.

If the GL doesn't separate expenses by use type, start there. The reconciliation can only be as accurate as the expense categorization underneath it.
