A tenant audit engagement notice arrived 18 months after a 20-year tenant signed a renewal. Not because they suspected fraud. Their CPA flagged the ASC 842 workpaper.

The assumption is wrong, and acting on it produces worse outcomes.

Tenant audit firms are hired for three reasons that have nothing to do with the landlord's character. First, the lease is expiring and the tenant is doing a full cost review. auditing CAM is part of confirming their total occupancy cost for the next location decision. Second, the tenant's CPA flagged the CAM statement as part of ASC 842 lease accounting work. Found something that needed explanation. Third, another tenant in the same building or portfolio used an audit firm and mentioned it.

None of these motivations require the tenant to believe the landlord was dishonest. They require the tenant to believe the billing might be wrong. which is sometimes the case.

The landlord who treats the audit engagement as a trust violation creates a defensive posture that makes documentation harder to produce and disputes more expensive to resolve. The landlord who treats it as a verification request. like any other accounting question. produces documentation quickly, acknowledges errors that exist, and resolves confirmed findings.

The majority-finding rate in tenant audits is not evidence that landlords are dishonest. It is evidence that CAM reconciliation processes are not designed with the assumption that someone will independently verify the output. That gap is what the audit closes.

Closing the gap yourself, before the auditor does, is the operational change that changes the outcome.
