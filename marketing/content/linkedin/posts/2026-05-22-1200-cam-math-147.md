A $42,000-per-year loan origination fee had been sitting in the recoverable CAM pool for five years. Excluded by every tenant's lease. Invisible to every reconciliation until the October audit letter arrived.

The national food-and-beverage tenant sent the audit letter in October. Year-end review, routine request for three years of GL backup. The property manager forwarded it without concern. the reconciliations had been clean every year.

The auditor's first finding arrived six weeks later. A line item appearing in the recoverable CAM pool on every reconciliation since 2021: $42,000 per year in amortized loan origination fees from the 2018 refinancing. The lease excluded financing costs. The exclusion language was clear. The item had been in the pool for five years.

Three years of audit exposure, seven tenants who each held the same exclusion, at a 12% average pro-rata share. Refund calculation: $42,000 x 3 years x 7 tenants x 12% average share = roughly $106,000. With interest provisions triggered under two of the leases, the total settlement landed at $124,000.

The controller who had set up the CAM pool in Yardi in 2019 had mapped a corporate accounting category that included both operating management costs and debt service allocations. The category had been correct for financial reporting. It was wrong for CAM. No one had checked the exclusion list against the account mappings since the original setup.

This is the most common origin story for CAM exclusion errors: correct accounting, wrong category for CAM. Operating expense accounts in an ERP are designed for financial reporting, not for lease-specific inclusion/exclusion compliance. They're built to capture every cost. CAM pools are supposed to filter based on each lease's specific language.

The two-step review that would have caught this: First, pull the list of accounts mapped to the CAM pool. Compare each account against the exclusion language in the lease. Any account that includes financing costs, depreciation, above-market management fees, or leasing-related costs needs to be split or excluded before the pool runs. Second, when an account contains mixed recoverable. Non-recoverable items, create a sub-account or manual adjustment to exclude the non-recoverable portion.

The tenant auditor found the issue by doing exactly this. taking the GL backup and reading each account description against the lease's exclusion list. An hour of work on the landlord's side before each reconciliation would have produced the same result five years earlier, for a fraction of the cost.

The exclusion list in the lease is the governing document. Not the ERP's account mapping. Not what prior owners did. Not industry convention. If the lease says financing costs are excluded, the GL account that contains financing costs does not belong in the pool. even partially.
