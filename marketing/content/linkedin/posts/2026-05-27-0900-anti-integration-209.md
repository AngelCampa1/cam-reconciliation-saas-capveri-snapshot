For a 30-building portfolio, the before-and-after of adding an independent CAM verification layer:

Before: 6-8 weeks of Q1 reconciliation work across 3-4 property controllers, errors discovered by tenant auditors after statements are sent.

After: controller exports GL from the ERP (same export already produced for month-end close), uploads to independent reconciliation software, reviews flagged discrepancies. Total time: 1-2 weeks of focused review instead of 6-8 weeks of building from scratch.

The savings come from eliminating the manual calculation work, not from replacing the ERP.

The ERP calculates based on its configuration. Independent software recalculates from the raw GL data and lease terms, without inheriting the ERP's recovery pool settings, gross-up thresholds, or cap configurations. Where the two calculations diverge, the discrepancy appears with the specific tenant, the specific calculation element, and the lease clause that governs it.

What stays the same: the ERP, the GL account structure, the existing workflow, and IT setup. No API credentials, no VPN access, no firewall rules, no integration maintenance.

What changes: discrepancies are caught before statements go out rather than after tenant auditors find them.

Tenant audit firms typically work on contingency, with contingency fee structures that typically run 20-40% of recovered amounts, per published tenant audit firm rate schedules. On a $15,000 error, that is $3,000-$6,000 to the tenant's auditor, plus the credit or refund, plus the damaged relationship.

The verification layer using CSV exports from the existing ERP costs a fraction of what one disputed reconciliation costs.
