SLIDE 1:
How CPAs verify CAM statements
(The same 7-step process tenant auditors use. And You should too)

SLIDE 2:
Step 1: GL reconciliation
Tie the CAM pool total on the statement back to the GL.
If the numbers don't match, that variance is the first finding.
Run this before the statement leaves the building.

SLIDE 3:
Step 2: Expense categorization
Every GL account gets mapped against the lease's operating expense definition.
Accounts that look recoverable but are excluded by the lease get flagged.
A lease matrix. one row per account, one column per lease. catches this systematically.

SLIDE 4:
Step 3: Gross-up verification
Check whether actual occupancy cleared the threshold.
If yes, the multiplier is 1.0. Full stop.
Then verify that property taxes and insurance were excluded from the gross-up pool.

SLIDE 5:
Step 4: Denominator check
Tenant SF verified against lease exhibit.
Building denominator checked year over year.
A 1% error on a $1.5M CAM pool is $15,000 per tenant per year.

SLIDE 6:
Step 5: Cap application
Reconstruct the cap bank from the base year forward.
Over-cap charges compound. A $5,000 over-cap error in year two becomes a $15,000 problem by year four.
The cap history must be documented, not reconstructed from memory.

SLIDE 7:
Steps 6 and 7: Exclusion list and tie-out
Exclusion list: legal fees, leasing commissions, capital improvements, checked against the billed pool.
Tie-out: recalculate the final billed amount from verified inputs and compare to what was actually charged.
Both steps take 30 minutes. Both catch errors the billing system doesn't flag.
