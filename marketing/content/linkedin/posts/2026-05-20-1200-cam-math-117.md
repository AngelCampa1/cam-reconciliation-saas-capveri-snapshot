The pro-rata share formula has two inputs: the tenant's square footage and the building's total rentable square footage. Controllers spend significant time verifying the numerator. Almost nobody verifies the denominator with the same rigor. That imbalance is where revenue disappears.

Denominator drift is not a single event. It accumulates through multiple small changes over years.

Consider a 175,000 RSF office building over five years. Year 1: kiosk program adds 800 SF. Year 2: BOMA remeasurement adds 3,200 SF. Year 3: a tenant vacates and the space is reclassified, removing 2,500 SF. Year 4: common area converted to storage adds 1,800 SF. Year 5: anchor begins HVAC self-maintenance. the building denominator stays at 178,300 SF, but the HVAC pool denominator drops to 138,300 SF for that expense category.

A tenant with 12,000 RSF who signed at a 175,000 denominator:
- Lease share: 12,000 / 175,000 = 6.857%
- Year 5 system share: 12,000 / 178,300 = 6.730%
- Year 5 HVAC pool share: 12,000 / 138,300 = 8.677%

The tenant's overall share drifted 0.127 percentage points lower, costing the landlord $1,905 per year on this one tenant's slice of overall CAM. Their HVAC share jumped 1.82 percentage points higher, generating a $3,640 per year overbilling on the HVAC pool.

Net impact on this one tenant: $1,735 annual overbilling. The tenant has grounds for an audit finding on the HVAC allocation. The landlord is simultaneously under-recovering on the overall pool. Both are products of the same denominator drift, going in different directions.

The system vs. lease mismatch is structural. Property management systems maintain one denominator per building. Leases are written per tenant with potentially different denominator definitions. Some say "total building area of 175,000 RSF" (fixed). Others say "total rentable area as measured from time to time" (variable). Most systems apply one number to everyone. That's fine when leases agree. It's a problem when they diverge.

The fix requires three practices: maintain a denominator change log with date, cause, old value, new value, and affected tenants. Review denominator settings at every lease signing, renewal, and amendment. Audit denominators annually before reconciliation statements are generated. the 30-minute comparison of system denominators to lease denominators prevents the most common audit findings.

On a $1.5 million CAM pool, a 3% drift from a building remeasurement shifts $45,000 in annual recovery. That's not configuration noise. That's a material revenue error that compounds until someone checks.
