Most property accounting teams still rely on Excel for at least part of their reconciliation process. The workflow has worked for 25 years. The question is whether the risks have finally outgrown the convenience.

Three things changed.

Tenant audit firms got better. Five years ago, they reviewed statement totals. Now they request the workbook and check the formulas. They know the common Excel errors. broken SUM ranges, hardcoded overrides, stale references. And They look for them specifically.

Portfolio sizes grew. A controller managing five properties can maintain five spreadsheets. A controller managing twenty-five has twenty-five spreadsheets, each slightly different, each updated once a year. The probability of a material error in at least one workbook approaches certainty.

Expenses grew. A formula error that produced a $5,000 variance in 2015 produces a $12,000 variance now because the expense pool is larger. Same percentage error, bigger dollar finding.

The spreadsheet isn't dying because it's bad. It's dying because the cost of its failure modes now exceeds the cost of the alternatives. A purpose-built CAM tool costs $200-$500 per property per year. A single audit finding from a spreadsheet error costs $20,000-$100,000 or more in refunds, legal costs, and damaged tenant relationships.

The expected value calculation is straightforward once you run it.
