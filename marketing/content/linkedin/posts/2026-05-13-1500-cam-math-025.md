SLIDE 1:
5 CAM reconciliation best practices from BOMA
(The ones that actually prevent disputes)

SLIDE 2:
Best practice 1: Use economic occupancy for gross-up, not physical.
Physical occupancy counts occupied suites. Economic occupancy counts rent-paying tenants. A tenant in free-rent period is physically present but economically absent. BOMA standards specify economic occupancy for the gross-up calculation.

SLIDE 3:
Best practice 2: Verify the denominator before the reconciliation runs.
Not after. The denominator drives every tenant's share. A wrong denominator creates a systemic billing error across the entire rent roll. Check it against the most recent BOMA-certified measurement before calculations start.

SLIDE 4:
Best practice 3: Run an independent verification of the reconciliation output.
Yardi or MRI calculated what you configured. An independent calculation from the lease terms, not the system configuration, catches the gap between what the system was set up to do and what the lease requires.

SLIDE 5:
Best practice 4: Classify variable and fixed expenses before applying gross-up.
Gross-up is legally permitted only for variable expenses. Applying it to fixed costs. property taxes, insurance, fixed-contract maintenance, is a lease violation. BOMA's standard expense definitions provide the classification framework.

SLIDE 6:
Best practice 5: Deliver statements before the contractual deadline.
Not on time. Before. A statement delivered the day before the deadline gives the tenant no notice and gives you no buffer for delivery failures. Build in two weeks of margin. Late statements in some leases permanently waive the landlord's right to collect.

SLIDE 7:
The common thread across all five: the math is not the hard part.
The hard part is verifying that the inputs are correct before the math runs.
