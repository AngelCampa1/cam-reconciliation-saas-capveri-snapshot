In a 12-tenant office building, a $22,000 elevator modernization might be fully recoverable from one tenant, partially recoverable from another, and completely excluded from a third.

Tenant A's lease: "CapEx over $15,000 per item excluded." Not recoverable.
Tenant B's lease: "All CapEx as defined under GAAP excluded." Not recoverable.
Tenant C's lease: No CapEx exclusion. Recoverable.

Same expense. Three different answers. If you're handling this from memory during reconciliation season, one of those determinations is going to be wrong.

The contrarian point: most CAM exclusion errors are not errors of ignorance. Controllers generally know what the exclusions are. They're errors of scale. In a 12-tenant building with 20 common exclusion categories, that's 240 individual determinations per reconciliation. No one consistently gets 240 things right from memory under time pressure.

The fix is structural: an exclusion matrix. One axis is expense categories. The other is tenants. Each cell is yes or no. Update it when leases are signed, renewed, or amended. Reference it during reconciliation instead of re-reading the lease.

The matrix takes an afternoon to build for a full portfolio. It eliminates a category of error that generates more refund exposure than any other single factor in multi-tenant reconciliations.
