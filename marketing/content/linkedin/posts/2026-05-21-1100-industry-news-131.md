Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"When a property tax appeal succeeds and you receive a refund, the CAM reconciliation gets complicated in a way most property managers aren't prepared for."

Talking points:
- Property tax is typically 25-40% of the total CAM pool. When the assessed value is appealed and a refund is issued, that refund reduces the net tax expense. But When does it reduce it? The year the refund is received, or the year the original tax was charged? - Most leases are silent on this. When the lease doesn't specify, the accounting treatment becomes a judgment call. And A contested one. Tenants who were billed during the appeal year expect a credit when the refund arrives. Landlords who received the refund in year three may not have told tenants in years one and two. - The year-of-appeal versus year-of-refund treatment matters for tenants with CAM caps. If the original tax was charged in a capped year, the tenant may have paid up to the cap. The refund coming back in a different year may or may not flow through to the tenant, depending on how the cap clause is worded. - The notification obligation. Many leases require the landlord to notify tenants of a pending appeal. More require notification when a refund is received. Property managers who handle the appeal without notifying tenants create a documentation gap that surfaces in the next audit. - Practical steps: when filing an appeal, check the lease for notification requirements. When a refund is received, document the year-of-origin for the original charge, determine which year's pool should be credited, notify tenants per the lease requirements,. Issue a reconciliation adjustment. - The administrative burden of doing this correctly is real. But the cost of not doing it is a dispute that surfaces two years after the refund was received, when documentation of the original appeal and the refund allocation may not be easily accessible.

Closing line:
"A successful tax appeal creates an obligation. Document the treatment before the question comes in from a tenant or their auditor."
