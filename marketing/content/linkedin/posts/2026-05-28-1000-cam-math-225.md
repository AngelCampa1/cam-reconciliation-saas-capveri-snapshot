Three admin fee calculation methods. Three different numbers. Most leases don't specify which one applies.

On a $500,000 CAM expense pool with a 15% admin fee:

Gross method (fee calculated on pool including the fee): $88,235.
Net method (fee calculated on pool excluding the fee): $75,000.
Capped method (fixed dollar ceiling): depends on the cap, but often $60,000 or below.

The difference between gross and net: $13,235 per year. For a tenant with a 10% pro-rata share over a 10-year lease, that's $13,235 in total billing variance from a single clause interpretation.

The contrarian point: most property management systems default to the gross method because it's the standard configuration. The lease may or may not say "gross." Most leases say something like "3% of total operating expenses" without specifying whether "total operating expenses" includes or excludes the fee itself.

Tenant auditors challenge this in nearly every audit precisely because the language is ambiguous. The gross method produces the higher fee. When the lease is silent, both interpretations are legally defensible.

The practical step: find the admin fee clause in every lease. If it says "of total CAM expenses including management fees," the gross method is clear. If it says "of total CAM expenses" without qualification, document your interpretation. The documentation is what determines whether you're right or wrong when the audit arrives.
