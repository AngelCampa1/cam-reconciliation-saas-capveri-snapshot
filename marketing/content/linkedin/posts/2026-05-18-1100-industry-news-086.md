Sun Belt metros continue to absorb significant population growth, per U.S. Census Bureau Vintage 2024 population estimates that show Phoenix, Nashville, Austin, and Tampa among the fastest-growing large metros in the country.

That growth is showing up in CAM pools in ways that are not always visible until reconciliation season.

Insurance. Florida and other hurricane-exposed Sun Belt markets ran through a multi-year hard market for commercial property insurance, with double-digit rate increases routine through 2022-2023 per Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index and Lockton Market Update reporting. Even after rate moderation in 2024, the cumulative effect on CAM pools is material. For landlords, insurance is recoverable. For tenants, the line item that represents 10-15% of the total CAM pool is now meaningfully higher than the multi-year average. That generates questions.

Property taxes. Rapid appreciation in Sun Belt markets has pushed reassessment cycles forward in several jurisdictions. Phoenix and Dallas both saw above-average assessment activity. Tenants who did not see a corresponding explanation in their reconciliation documentation are more likely to dispute.

New construction stock. Sun Belt markets have absorbed a meaningful share of national industrial construction over the last five years, per CBRE quarterly Industrial Figures reporting. That new stock carries different CAM structures, different expense profiles, and different gross-up thresholds than the older buildings nearby. Property managers handling both need to be careful that reconciliation templates built for one don't get applied to the other.

The growth story in Sun Belt markets is real. What it creates operationally is a set of CAM pools with unusual year-over-year variance in two of the highest-scrutiny line items: insurance and property taxes. Both need documentation that explains the increase before tenants ask.
