Billing a tenant for an excluded expense doesn't just create a refund obligation. It invites a forensic audit of your entire GL.

That's the asymmetry worth understanding: the exposure isn't limited to the single line item the tenant caught. Once they have grounds to dispute, they have grounds to review everything.

The BOMA standard exclusions are a starting point: CapEx, leasing commissions, tenant improvement costs, landlord-only costs, and management fees above the lease cap. But the list in the standard is not the list in your lease. Every tenant may have a different exclusion clause.

The practical gap is between the lease and the system. Yardi or MRI will recover whatever accounts you've mapped to the recovery pool. Neither one reads each tenant's exclusion clause independently. Configuration drift, the system including an account the lease excludes. is invisible until someone checks.

Practitioners and tenant audit firms consistently rank CapEx misclassification among the largest single sources of CAM disputes. A capital project billed as an operating expense moves the dispute from a single line item to the entire statement. One roof replacement coded to the wrong account poisons the entire statement for every tenant who catches it.

The fix isn't complicated: maintain an exclusion matrix with one row per tenant and one column per expense category. Update it every time a lease is signed, amended, or renewed. Run the reconciliation through the matrix, not around it.
