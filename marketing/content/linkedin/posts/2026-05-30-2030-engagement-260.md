A definition that's worth being precise about: "CAM cap" means different things in different leases, and the difference is financially significant.

A non-cumulative cap limits the year-over-year increase in controllable CAM expenses to a fixed percentage, typically 5%. Each year, the prior year's actual becomes the new base. Increases reset annually.

A cumulative cap sets a maximum aggregate increase over the lease term rather than limiting each year individually. In years where costs increase less than the cap percentage, the unused allowance can carry forward. The landlord may be able to recover larger increases in later years if costs spike.

A fixed-amount cap sets an absolute ceiling on total CAM billings per square foot per year, regardless of actual costs.

Each of these structures has materially different implications for how much the landlord can recover over a multi-year lease term. A misconfiguration that applies a non-cumulative cap when the lease requires cumulative (or vice versa) produces errors that compound annually.

Property accountants who set these up at lease execution understand the distinction. The question is whether the ERP configuration was set correctly, and whether it's still correct after amendments and renewals have been applied.
