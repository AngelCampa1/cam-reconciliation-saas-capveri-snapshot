When a Yardi recovery pool produces the wrong CAM amount, seven root causes account for almost all of it. Not thirty. Seven.

The most consequential is the first one: Occupancy Type set to Physical instead of Economic.

Physical occupancy is a snapshot. typically December 31. If a major tenant vacated in November, the December 31 reading shows occupancy 15 percentage points lower than the weighted average for the full year. Physical at December 31: 75%. Economic for the full year: 86.25%. On $500,000 in variable expenses, the difference in grossed-up pool total is $82,500. which all flows to the remaining tenants. This is one of the most common Yardi configuration errors,. It produces numbers that look defensible until someone calculates the weighted average.

Root cause 2: fixed expenses flagged as variable. Property taxes, insurance premiums, and contract-based landscaping do not vary with occupancy. When they are classified as variable, they get grossed up as if they would. The overcharge flows to every tenant in the pool, every year, until someone checks the classification line by line.

Root cause 3: wrong gross-up threshold. Pool is set to 90%. Three leases say 95%. Two leases say 90%. One has no gross-up clause. A single pool-level setting cannot serve all three correctly. The fix is tenant-level overrides. But Those require someone to check each lease.

Root cause 4: unmapped GL accounts. New accounts created mid-year during chart restructuring never get added to the pool. The expense reduces NOI. Tenants are never billed. The gap stays invisible because the total CAM charge looks reasonable compared to the prior year.

Root cause 5: denominator not updated. The lease says total building RSF. Yardi is using occupied RSF because a tenant moved out mid-year and no one updated the setting. Every tenant's pro-rata share is wrong by the same percentage.

Root cause 6: cap configuration error. Non-cumulative applied when the lease says cumulative. Year 1 is identical. Year 2 and beyond diverge. By the time anyone notices, three or four years of statements need to be restated.

Root cause 7: wrong recovery code assignment. The tenant is assigned to the wrong pool entirely. The calculation runs, the statement looks normal, and the amount is based on expenses from the wrong pool.

The validation that catches all seven in two to three hours: pick three tenants. largest, smallest, most complex. Calculate their CAM manually using GL data and lease terms. Compare to Yardi's output. If they match within 1%, the pool is likely correct. If they diverge, trace the discrepancy to one of these seven items.

That two to three hours prevents errors that otherwise persist until a tenant's auditor finds them.
