The portfolio had 23 properties. The accounting team of four analysts ran the CAM reconciliation every year, starting in late January and wrapping the last statements in mid-May. Fourteen to sixteen weeks was normal. Nobody had ever suggested it might be slow.

Then a new controller joined and asked an uncomfortable question: what do the reconciliations cost in analyst time?

The calculation: 23 properties × 12 hours average analyst time = 276 hours. At fully-loaded cost of $55/hour, that was $15,180. Reasonable for a portfolio of that size.

What came out next was not reasonable. The rework calculation: in the prior year, 7 of the 23 properties had errors caught after statements were already drafted. Average rework time per property: 8 hours. That was 56 hours of rework, plus a 3-week delay on the affected properties. Total rework cost: $3,080. Total delay on 7 statements: 3 weeks each, in a portfolio where some leases had 90-day delivery deadlines from year-end.

Two properties in that 7 had been delivered after the lease's contractual deadline. In one case, the tenant's lease had a provision that late delivery extended the reconciliation window. But Did not waive the right to dispute. In the other case, the attorney reviewing the lease found language suggesting late delivery could affect collection rights. Potential exposure had to be assessed and documented.

The benchmark for a 21-50 property portfolio: 5-8 weeks from GL close to last statement for a well-organized team, 8-12 weeks for average, 14 weeks or more indicates a process problem. This portfolio was running at the slow end of average and paying for it in rework costs and deadline exposure.

The root cause wasn't analyst skill. It was where errors were being caught: after drafting, not before. A pre-reconciliation GL review. the 30-minute scan of large transactions, year-over-year variances, and management fee calculation. was not part of the process. Errors were found when statements were cross-checked, not when the GL was first pulled.

Adding a structured GL review at the front of each property's process added 30 minutes per property. 23 properties × 30 minutes = 11.5 hours. At $55/hour: $632.

It eliminated 56 hours of rework.

The math is not complicated. The process change is: run the GL review before the calculation, not after.
