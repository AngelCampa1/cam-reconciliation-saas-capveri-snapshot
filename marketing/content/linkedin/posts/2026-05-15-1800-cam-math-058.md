SLIDE 1:
How to document lease exclusions so they actually get applied
(The exclusion matrix method)

SLIDE 2:
The problem with exclusion lists: they live in 47 different lease abstracts.
85% of commercial leases exclude CapEx. But which CapEx, at which dollar threshold, with which exceptions, varies by tenant. A building-wide exclusion policy doesn't work. You need a per-tenant matrix.

SLIDE 3:
Build the matrix: one row per tenant, one column per expense category.
Columns: CapEx, management fee, admin fee, anchor exclusions, self-maintenance, tenant-specific carve-outs.
Values: Included, Excluded, Partially Excluded (with note on threshold or condition).

SLIDE 4:
Example: a $22,000 elevator modernization.
Tenants A and B: excluded by CapEx clause.
Tenant C: recoverable at $10,000 amortized over 20 years (= $500/year per lease provision).
Same expense, three different treatment rules.
Without the matrix, one of those three gets billed wrong.

SLIDE 5:
Update the matrix at every lease event.
Lease signing, lease renewal, lease amendment, anchor departure. each one potentially changes the exclusion profile for one or more tenants. Update immediately, not at reconciliation time.

SLIDE 6:
The $5,280 math.
One tenant overbilled $1,760/year for 3 years because an elevator exclusion wasn't applied.
That's $5,280 before the tenant found it and triggered a full GL audit.

SLIDE 7:
Run the reconciliation through the matrix, not around it.
The matrix is the source of truth for what goes into each tenant's calculation.
Not the system's default recovery pool settings.

SLIDE 8:
The exclusion matrix doesn't just prevent overbilling.
It's the documentation package you need when a tenant requests audit.
A clean, current matrix answers 80% of audit questions before they become disputes.
