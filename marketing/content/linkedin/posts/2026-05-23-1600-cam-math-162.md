The property controller ran the 2025 reconciliation the same way she had run every reconciliation for the previous eight years. GL export, account mapping, pro-rata shares off the rent roll, statements by April.

The statement for one office tenant. a regional law firm on floors 4 and 5. came back with a dispute letter three months later. The firm's auditor had found that the building had been re-measured under BOMA 2024 in 2023, increasing total certified RSF from 182,000 to 188,400. The increase was mostly from reclassifying outdoor terrace space on the seventh floor.

The billing system still had 182,000 SF as the denominator. The 2024 re-measurement had never been updated in the system.

The law firm's auditor ran a simple calculation: with the larger denominator, their pro-rata share should have been 12.3%, not 12.7%. On a $1.4 million pool, the difference was $5,600 per year. The dispute covered two billing cycles for $11,200.

What the auditor also found,. Surprised the controller: two other tenants in the building had leases executed after the BOMA 2024 re-measurement. Their leases referenced the updated 182,400 SF certified area. When the controller ran pro-rata shares for these tenants using the old 182,000 denominator, their shares were understated by the same 0.4%. The controller had been under-recovering from those two tenants by a smaller amount for the same two years.

The building was simultaneously overbilling some tenants and underbilling others. using the same wrong denominator. because the BOMA 2024 update had never been reconciled against what was in the billing system.

BOMA 2024 reclassified outdoor amenity spaces as rentable SF and eliminated load factors on balconies and rooftop terraces. The net effect across a typical building: 2 to 5% RSF increase. On a 182,000 SF building, that's 3,640 to 9,100 additional SF. Every tenant's pro-rata share shifts when the denominator changes, even if the CAM pool itself doesn't grow.

The standard verification that would have caught this: after any re-measurement, pull the certified RSF and compare it against the denominator in the billing system. If they differ, check which leases reference which measurement standard and update denominators accordingly. When mixed-vintage leases exist in the same building. some under BOMA 2017, some under BOMA 2024. document which denominator applies to which tenant and run the math separately.

This is not a rare situation. Any building re-measured in 2023 or 2024 and any portfolio with leases from multiple vintages has some version of this issue to resolve.
