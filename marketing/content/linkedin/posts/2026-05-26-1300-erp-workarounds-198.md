The Share Type field in MRI may be the single most consequential configuration choice in CAM setup. It determines the denominator in every pro-rata calculation for every tenant it governs.

Two options: building-level (denominator = total building RSF) and floor-level (denominator = floor RSF only). These are not close approximations of each other.

A 15,000 SF tenant in a 120,000 SF building with 30,000 SF on their floor:
Building-level pro-rata: 15,000 / 120,000 = 12.5%. If the total CAM pool is $600,000, the tenant pays $75,000.
Floor-level pro-rata: 15,000 / 30,000 = 50%. If floor operating costs are $80,000, the tenant pays $40,000.

Those two numbers represent a $35,000 gap for a single tenant. The correct answer depends on what the lease says, not which number is larger.

Building-level is standard for most commercial leases where expenses are building-wide and shared by all tenants. Floor-level is appropriate when the lease explicitly defines the denominator as floor square footage, when the building has separate operating pools by floor, or when floors have materially different costs.

The error that creates audit exposure: the setting was configured during initial property entry and never revisited. A lease renewal that changes the denominator definition, a lease amendment that restructures the expense pool, or a simple data entry error from original implementation. any of these can result in the wrong Share Type applied to every reconciliation for years.

The diagnostic check takes three minutes: compare the pro-rata percentage in MRI to what the lease actually specifies. If they do not match, the Share Type is wrong.
