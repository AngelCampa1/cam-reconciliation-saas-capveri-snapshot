HOOK (0:00-0:08):
The most common tenant audit finding is a capital expenditure in the operating CAM pool. Here's how to know when CapEx belongs there. And When it doesn't.

TALKING POINT 1 (0:09-0:30):
The baseline rule is simple: CapEx is not recoverable in CAM. A capital expenditure creates a new asset, extends useful life, or substantially upgrades something that already exists. These costs belong on the asset ledger. CAM recovers operating expenses. the ongoing cost of running the building.

TALKING POINT 2 (0:31-0:55):
Three exceptions. First: capital improvements that reduce operating expenses. A new HVAC that cuts utility bills by $15,000 a year. the annual amortized cost can flow through CAM if the lease allows it. Second: code compliance improvements required by a government authority. ADA upgrades, fire code retrofits. Third: specific lease allowances, which are more common in institutional-quality buildings.

TALKING POINT 3 (0:56-1:20):
When CapEx is recoverable, only the amortized annual portion goes in the pool. A $200,000 roof replacement with a 20-year useful life: $10,000 per year in the CAM pool. Never the lump sum. Never the full cost in year one.

TALKING POINT 4 (1:21-1:40):
The classification test that matters: Does this cost better the property, adapt it to a new use, or restore it to like-new condition? If yes, it's likely capital. If it restores existing functionality without upgrading it, it's more likely a repair. When it's borderline, document your reasoning. That documentation is what survives a tenant audit.

CLOSING (1:41-1:55):
Check your CAM pool for any single-year CapEx before statements go out. Any item above your capitalization threshold needs a classification memo or it becomes your highest-probability audit finding.
