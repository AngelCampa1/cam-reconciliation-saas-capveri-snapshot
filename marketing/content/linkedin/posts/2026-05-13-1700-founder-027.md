One of the things that surprised Angel Campa while building CapVeri: how often the problem isn't the calculation itself. It's the inputs.

Property accountants know the formulas. Gross-up, pro-rata, cap application. These aren't mysteries. The problem is that the inputs to those formulas, the occupancy number, the cap type, the expense pool, are pulled from the ERP configuration. And the ERP configuration is set up once, at lease execution, and rarely revisited.

Here's what that looks like in practice.

A tenant signs a lease in 2021 with a 5% cumulative cap and a base year of 2020. The property accountant at the time loads the cap type and base year into Yardi. Two years later, that accountant leaves. The new person doesn't know why 2020 was chosen as the base year. They don't question it. Why would they? The system ran clean last year.

But 2020 was a low-occupancy, low-expense year for most commercial properties. Using it as a base year means every subsequent reconciliation starts from an artificially low floor. The tenant is seeing increases calculated against a year that doesn't represent normal operating costs. That's not fraud. It's configuration drift from an unusual year that never got flagged.

The tenant's auditor, two years later, flags it immediately.

The landlord's position: we ran the calculations correctly. And that's true. The calculation was correct given the inputs. The inputs were the problem.

This is the gap that motivated CapVeri. The ERP doesn't self-audit. It runs what it was given. An independent layer that reads the actual lease language and compares it to the configured inputs is the only way to find this kind of drift before it becomes a dispute.

The fix for the base-year problem is simple once you know it's there: pull the base-year line from the lease, compare it to the ERP configuration, flag any year that falls in a recognized anomaly window (2020, 2021), and document the business reason for using it. Either the base year is justified or it needs to be renegotiated.

What we're building at CapVeri is the layer that runs that check automatically, on every reconciliation cycle, without a separate audit engagement.
