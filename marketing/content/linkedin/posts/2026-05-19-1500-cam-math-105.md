A skilled property controller earns $75,000 to $110,000 per year. At 2,080 annual work hours, that's $54 to $80 per hour fully loaded. A 20-building portfolio consumes 300 to 500 hours of controller time annually just for reconciliation. That's $16,000 to $40,000 in annual reconciliation labor before disputes, errors, or rework.

Then there's the error exposure. Roughly 40% of CAM reconciliations contain material errors, per PredictAP's 2023 industry analysis citing JLL data. Most are discovered only after tenants retain auditors. A single contested CAM dispute easily runs five to six figures in settlement costs and legal fees once discovery begins.

A portfolio with 100 tenants at a 5% annual dispute rate generates 5 disputes per year. At $35,000 average settlement: $175,000 in annual dispute exposure.

The math for purpose-built software on the same 20-building portfolio: $10,000 to $15,000 per year in software cost, 3 hours per building in controller time (review only, not full reconciliation), and systematic error detection before statements go out.

Conservative annual benefit: $60,000 in recovered missed charges + $175,000 in dispute avoidance + $18,000 in labor savings = $253,000. Against $12,500 in software cost, the payback period is approximately two to three weeks.

That's not an ROI model built for a sales pitch. It's the arithmetic of what manual reconciliation actually costs when you include errors, disputes, and the labor to manage them.
