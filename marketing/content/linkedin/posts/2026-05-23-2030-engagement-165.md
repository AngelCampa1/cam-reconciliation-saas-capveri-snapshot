A take worth examining: BOMA measurement standards are overkill for most commercial leases.

The argument goes: most tenants aren't going to audit the floor plan measurement, and remeasuring to BOMA 2024 creates more disputes than it resolves.

There's some practical truth to that in the short term. But the longer-term risk is in the denominator.

The pro-rata calculation multiplies your tenant's rentable square footage by the total rentable area of the building. If those measurements were done to older standards, and a new tenant's space was measured to BOMA 2024, the denominator is inconsistent. The allocation is wrong for everyone on the floor, not just the new tenant.

The inconsistency usually goes unnoticed until a sophisticated tenant or an auditor asks to see the measurement documentation for the denominator. At that point, the landlord is defending a denominator built from two different measurement standards applied at different times.

BOMA 2024 consistency is not about precision for its own sake. It's about having a denominator that can be defended.

Does your team have a policy on measurement standard consistency when new leases are added to an existing floor?
