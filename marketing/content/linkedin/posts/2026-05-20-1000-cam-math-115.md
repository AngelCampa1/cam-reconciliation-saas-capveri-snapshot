Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"If your tenant's CAM bill jumped 20% and nothing in the lease changed, look at the denominator."

Talking points:
- The pro-rata share formula is simple: tenant RSF divided by building RSF. When the denominator shifts, every tenant's share recalculates. even if their own space and the total expense pool stay exactly the same. - Five things move the denominator: BOMA remeasurement under a new standard, an anchor tenant vacating or arriving, a tenant starting or stopping self-maintenance exclusions, kiosk or storage space added to or removed from the rentable pool,. Common area reclassification. - The communication problem: tenants receive a bill with a different share percentage and no explanation. Without documentation, the conversation is instantly adversarial. - A 5,000 SF remeasurement on a 150,000 RSF building moves every tenant's share by roughly 3.3%. For a tenant paying $50,000 in annual CAM, that's a $1,650 increase with no change in actual expenses. The math is correct. The tenant needs to know why. - An anchor departure on a 200,000 RSF center can push a 10,000 SF inline tenant from 5.0% to 6.45% overnight. That's a 29% increase in their CAM bill from nothing except a vacancy event they didn't cause. - Document every denominator change: what changed, the old and new values, per-tenant impact, effective date, and the supporting measurement certificate or lease event.

Closing line:
"The denominator change is a communication problem, not a math problem. Document it before the statement goes out."
