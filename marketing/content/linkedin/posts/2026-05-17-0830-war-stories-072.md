A 5-percentage-point recovery ratio gap on a $3M expense base is $150,000 in annual underrecovery, which is $2.5M in suppressed asset value at a 6% cap rate. Institutional buyers see that before they look at almost anything else.

On a $50 million acquisition, a 5% gap is not a rounding error. It is a price negotiation.

Where the gaps come from:

Unmapped GL accounts. Expenses that belong in the CAM pool were coded to accounts not included in the reconciliation. Nobody mapped the account when it was created, and nobody checked whether it should be recoverable.

CapEx over-classification. Capital improvements were coded as operating expenses in the GL, the same error tenants find and dispute. In this direction, it inflates the apparent CAM pool without corresponding lease support.

Stale lease abstracts. The lease was amended two years ago. The amendment changed the gross-up threshold or added an exclusion. The reconciliation still runs on the original terms.

These three problems share a single characteristic: they are invisible until someone compares the reconciliation to the actual lease text. That comparison does not happen in normal operations. It happens at disposition, when the buyers' team does it and adjusts the price.

The recovery ratio gap that shows up at sale was almost certainly present for years before anyone noticed it.
