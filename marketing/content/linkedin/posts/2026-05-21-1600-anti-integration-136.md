"Automating CAM reconciliation isn't a priority for us right now."

The problem with this position: it assumes the current risk is known and acceptable. It usually is not.

Every other back-office process has a linear return. Automating AP saves the AP clerk's time. Automating maintenance tickets reduces response time. The ROI is the cost saved.

CAM reconciliation has a multiplier. the capitalization rate.

A $50,000 gross-up error does not cost $50,000. It costs $833,333 in property value because NOI is capitalized. Finding $50,000 in previously uncaptured CAM revenue creates the same $833,333 in value. from a process change, not a capital investment.

Tenant audit firms consistently report finding material discrepancies in the majority of reconciliation statements they review. Raymond Panko's spreadsheet error research (published via EuSpRIG) found errors in at least 86% of audited corporate spreadsheets. The combination of complex lease terms and manual spreadsheet work creates systematic error risk. not because controllers are careless, but because the process is too complex for manual execution at scale.

Controllers are the most financially skilled people in a property management company. Using their time to copy formulas in Excel is a misallocation of talent. Every hour in a reconciliation spreadsheet is an hour not spent analyzing lease renewal economics, reviewing tenant credit risk, or supporting acquisition due diligence.

The argument is not "automate because it is faster." It is "automate because the cost of the current error rate exceeds the cost of the software" and because finding $50,000 you were not billing creates $833,333 in property value.

No other back-office process has that multiplier.
