SLIDE 1:
The 10-point CAM self-audit
Run it before the statement goes out, not after the dispute arrives

SLIDE 2:
Check 1: GL tie-out
Does the total CAM pool on the statement match the GL?
Tie them out to the penny before anything else.
A variance here is finding number one for any auditor.

SLIDE 3:
Check 2 and 3: Gross-up threshold and fixed expense exclusion
Is actual occupancy at or above the gross-up threshold? If yes, the factor is 1.0.
Are property taxes excluded from the gross-up base?
Are insurance premiums excluded from the gross-up base?
All three questions. Every reconciliation.

SLIDE 4:
Check 4: Management fee base
Is the management fee calculated on the base the lease specifies?
3% of gross revenue and 3% of net operating expenses are different numbers.
On a $1M pool, the difference is $12,000 per year.

SLIDE 5:
Check 5: Capital expenditures
Are there any capital improvements in the operating expense pool?
Roof replacements, HVAC equipment, parking lot resurfacing.
Most leases exclude these. Most billing systems don't flag them.

SLIDE 6:
Check 6 and 7: Lease exclusions and denominator
Are there any GL accounts that the lease explicitly excludes?
Legal fees. Leasing commissions. Tenant-specific work.
Is the tenant's square footage correct per the lease exhibit?
Has the building denominator changed? If so, is there a lease amendment or remeasurement to support it?

SLIDE 7:
Check 8 and 9: Cap bank and base year
If the lease has a CAM cap, was the cap bank calculated correctly from the base year forward?
Was the base year set during a construction period or anomaly year that artificially suppressed the tenant's threshold?
Both errors compound. Neither gets noticed until an auditor reconstructs the history.

SLIDE 8:
Check 10: Complete documentation package
Can every number on the statement be traced to a supporting document?
If the answer is no for any line item, the documentation is not ready.
The statement is the last thing to prepare. The documentation comes first.
