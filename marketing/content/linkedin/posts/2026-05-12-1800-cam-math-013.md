A $200,000 roof replacement shows up in your GL. Whether it belongs in your CAM pool depends on a three-part test most property accountants have never seen written down.

The default rule is simple: capital expenditures are not recoverable CAM expenses. CapEx extends useful life, improves functionality, or replaces a major building system. CAM covers operating and maintenance expenses. These are different categories in the lease, in the accounting standards, and in IRS treatment.

The exceptions are narrow and must be explicitly authorized in the lease. The three situations where CapEx can be recovered through CAM: the improvement reduces operating costs that are themselves recoverable, the work is required for code compliance, or the lease has a specific provision allowing amortized CapEx recovery.

Even where recovery is permitted, it must be amortized over the improvement's useful life, not expensed in the year incurred. A $200,000 roof with a 20-year useful life means $10,000 per year in the CAM pool, not $200,000.

The BAR test (Betterment, Adaptation, Restoration) from IRS regulations draws the line between a repair and a capital improvement. A repair maintains existing functionality. A capital improvement does more. Roof patching is a repair. Roof replacement is capital. Tenant auditors use the BAR test first. Property accountants should use it before statements go out.
