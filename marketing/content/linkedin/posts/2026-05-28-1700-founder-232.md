From Angel Campa, CapVeri founder:

In an illustrative scenario for a portfolio of this size relying on third-party consultants every two years, up to $72,000 in leakage could accumulate between audit cycles. That is the exact type of configuration drift that point-in-time audits don't catch. Consultant audits are valuable. What they can't do is watch every statement.

Third-party CAM audit engagements are valuable. The findings are usually reliable. The auditors know what they're looking for.

The problem is the cadence. A consultant audit happens when someone decides to pay for one. That might be every few years, or after a tenant dispute triggers it, or as part of a portfolio acquisition.

Between audits, the portfolio runs unverified. Configuration drift accumulates. Lease amendments don't get reflected in the ERP. Personnel change. The next audit, two years later, finds errors that have been running since the last one.

The value of a systematic check isn't the sophistication of the check. The value is the frequency. Running a lease-vs-ERP comparison every reconciliation cycle, even a basic one, catches errors when they're one year old rather than four.

The consultant model treats reconciliation accuracy as an episodic event. The workflow model treats it as a standing operational requirement. Those are different positions with different cost profiles and different risk outcomes.

Neither is wrong in all cases. But for a portfolio where the reconciliation cycle is annual, an annual verification step is the right model.
