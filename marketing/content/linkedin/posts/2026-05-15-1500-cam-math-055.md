Hook line (say on camera, first 3 seconds):
"If you're reconciling 10 properties sequentially, you're doing it the expensive way."

Talking points:
- The context-switching tax is real. Every time you leave one property's reconciliation. Pick up another, you pay 15-30 minutes of ramp-up time reconstructing the GL structure, the lease exceptions, and where you left off. For 10 properties, that's 2-5 hours of dead time per reconciliation cycle. - Start with the complex properties. Simple NNN buildings with uniform leases get done in 4-6 hours each. Complex buildings with anchor exclusions, multiple cap structures, and cumulative carryforwards take 12-18 hours. If you start with the easy ones, you'll hit January 15 and have six complex properties still unstarted with 60 days to deadline. - Parallel processing works if you have the staff. Assign one controller per property type or property cluster. They own the GL export, the lease abstract review, and the draft statements for their cluster. No handoffs between those phases. - The three pitfalls of batch reconciliation: inconsistent methodology across properties (cap calculations handled differently on Building A vs. Building B), shared staff bottlenecks (both controllers waiting on the same accountant for GL confirmation), and deadline calendar collisions (three properties with the same 90-day statement deadline, one controller, one January). - The bottleneck is always the calculation phase, not the review phase. Every controller believes it's the other way around.

Closing line:
"Complex properties first, parallel processing where possible, and never let the bottleneck be the deadline calendar."
