A 60,000 SF office tenant, a 22% year-over-year CAM increase, and a documentation gap that turned a defensible single-year billing into a three-year gross-up dispute. The first 72-hour response determined most of the outcome.

A 60,000 square foot office tenant receives their reconciliation statement in late March. Their controller flags a 22% year-over-year increase in total CAM charges. significantly above what they expected based on their budgeted estimate. They retain a CPA to review the statement.

The CPA requests GL detail, the gross-up calculation, and the lease's operating expense definition. The property manager sends the summary statement again and notes that the charges are "per the lease." The CPA follows up. The property manager asks the controller to pull the detail. Two weeks pass.

The CPA, unable to reconcile the summary statement to any documentation, expands the review to include the prior two years.

What would a complete response in the first 72 hours have included?

The GL detail for the year under review, organized by the expense categories on the statement. The gross-up calculation showing the occupancy percentage used, the threshold in the lease,. Which expense accounts were included in the variable pool. The pro-rata denominator history showing the building's total rentable area and any changes. The management fee calculation showing the base on which the fee was computed.

With that documentation, the CPA's review would have been bounded to the current year. With incomplete documentation, the scope expanded to three years. And The CPA found that the gross-up factor had been applied to property taxes for two of those three years.

The refund claim covered all three years. The original billing question. the 22% increase in a single year. was actually defensible. The three-year scope expansion was not.

The first 72-hour response did not cause the billing error. It created the conditions in which the billing error was discovered.

Complete documentation sent quickly bounds the scope of every audit. Incomplete documentation invites scope expansion. The choice is usually made in the first week without anyone realizing its consequences.
