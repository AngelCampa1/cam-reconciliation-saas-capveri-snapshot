SLIDE 1:
A property controller who can translate a $45,000 gross-up correction into $750,000 in property value at a 6% cap rate is not just closing the books. That is asset management.

SLIDE 2:
What changed
Operating expenses are a larger share of NOI. The controllable portion of property value increasingly depends on recovery optimization, not only expense control.

SLIDE 3:
The career ladder
Property accountant: transaction processing, GL coding, basic reconciliation support.
Senior property accountant: reconciliations, accruals, variance explanations.
Controller: portfolio close, internal controls, lender reporting, owner reporting.
CRE FinOps controller: lease economics, recovery strategy, audit defense, NOI translation.

SLIDE 4:
Four skill gaps
Lease financial analysis: know how a cumulative cap compounds over a 10-year term.
NOI translation: convert recovery corrections into property value.
Data validation: compare lease terms against ERP configuration.
Audit defense: document the math before tenants challenge it.

SLIDE 5:
The language switch
Accounting speaks in dollars. Asset management speaks in cap rates and property value.

A controller who says, "fixing this gross-up error adds $180K to NOI, which is $3M in value at a 6% cap," changes the conversation.

SLIDE 6:
Building the skill set
Learn the math deeply. Read amendments, not only abstracts. Understand how ERP configuration turns lease language into billed dollars.

SLIDE 7:
The opportunity
The finance team that can prove recovery accuracy becomes part of asset strategy, not just back-office reporting.

SLIDE 8:
That is a different job. And a different career.
