One wrong cell reference. $247,000 in refunds. A major tenant who did not renew.

The formula =SUM(A12:A43,A45:A52) should have been =SUM(A12:A52). Cell A44. a $142,000 annual insurance premium. fell out of the sum.

The missing premium was not the final cost. The final cost was the controller's response to the missing premium: the recovery ratio dropped, the controller assumed occupancy was the cause, and insurance was grossed up more aggressively to compensate. That response was a second error. insurance is a fixed expense, exempt from gross-up regardless of occupancy.

Over three years, the overbilling from the gross-up on insurance ran $82,000 per year. Total refunds when a tenant auditor found both errors: $247,000. Five other tenants then retained the same firm. The largest tenant. 23% of the building. did not renew.

The number that matters in this scenario is not $247,000. It is the seven-point drop in the recovery ratio that was the early signal. A 7-point drop without a lease change or vacancy movement is not market conditions. It is a calculation problem.

Investigating a recovery ratio variance costs an hour. The hour that wasn't spent in year one became $247,000 in year three.

Two checks catch both errors: verify the CAM pool total matches the GL, and verify the gross-up was applied to variable expenses only. Neither check requires anything beyond the GL and the lease.
