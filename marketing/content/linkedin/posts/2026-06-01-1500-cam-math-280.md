Including a single excluded expense in a CAM pool doesn't create a dispute for that line item alone. It creates a right to audit the entire pool.

When a tenant's auditor finds an excluded item. a financing cost, a depreciation charge, an above-market management fee overage. the standard next step is to request the full GL backup for all reconciliation years within the audit window. The auditor's logic: if one excluded item got through, the methodology was flawed. What else got through?

The refund exposure is the excluded item. The audit exposure is the full pool.

This is why BOMA exclusions, GAAP-defined CapEx, and above-market management fee provisions matter beyond the specific dollar amount. A $12,000 depreciation charge in a $600,000 pool represents $1,680 in overbilling for a 14% pro-rata tenant. But it triggers a review of the full $600,000 for three years.

The proactive fix is an exclusion audit before the reconciliation runs. Pull the list of accounts in the pool. Compare each account against the exclusion language in each tenant's lease. If a lease says "capital expenditures as defined under GAAP" are excluded, any account that contains CapEx. even partially. needs to be cleaned before the pool is finalized.

The exclusion list in the lease governs. Not the ERP configuration. Not what prior owners did. Not what's been there for six years without a dispute.
