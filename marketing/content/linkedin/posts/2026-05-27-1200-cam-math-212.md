The error had been in the system since 2020. A cumulative CAM cap, configured in the ERP as non-cumulative.

The distinction: non-cumulative caps reset each year against the prior year's actual billed amount. Cumulative caps compound from the base year. Both types use the same cap rate. The difference is the baseline.

For the first three years, the portfolio manager never noticed. When actual expenses tracked below the cap ceiling, both cap types produced the same result. the tenant paid actual expenses. The cap type only matters when actual expenses approach or exceed the ceiling. In low-inflation years, the ceiling is never tested.

2024 was not a low-inflation year.

Insurance premiums increased 31% at renewal. Utility costs increased 18% from the prior year. Actual CAM on the property: $9.85/SF.

Non-cumulative ceiling calculation: $8.87/SF (prior year actual $8.45/SF × 1.05). Cumulative ceiling calculation: $9.26/SF (base year $8.00/SF × 1.05^4).

The lease said cumulative. The system calculated non-cumulative. The tenant was billed $8.87/SF.

The correct amount was $9.26/SF on a 15,000 SF suite: $138,900. The billed amount: $133,050. The landlord's annual underrecovery: $5,850.

The cap type had been wrong for four years without consequence. In year five, when the cap actually mattered, the landlord lost $5,850 in recoverable income it was contractually entitled to.

The tenant's auditor found the same configuration error and raised a different concern: since the non-cumulative ceiling had been used in prior years, the unused carryforward bank had never been properly tracked. The auditor argued. correctly. that if the cumulative type had been applied throughout, the bank balance and ceiling calculations differed from what was billed in years two through four as well.

The reconciliation required rebuilding five years of cap calculations from scratch. Not because the amounts were large in most years, but because the correct ceiling required knowing the correct base and the correct carryforward state for each year in sequence.

Total adjustment across five years: $18,400 in additional landlord recovery, offset by a $1,200 credit in year three where the non-cumulative calculation had been slightly higher than the cumulative one. Net: $17,200 in landlord under-recovery from a configuration error that took 20 minutes to make and five years to surface.

The fix is mechanical: every year, before the cap calculation runs, pull the cap type from the signed lease and confirm the system setting matches. Cumulative and non-cumulative look identical in the system UI. The configuration field is a dropdown. A five-minute check.
