When a private equity firm underwrites a commercial property acquisition, CAM reconciliation accuracy is a direct input to valuation.

The math is straightforward. At a 6% cap rate, every $1 of stabilized NOI is worth $16.67 in asset value. A $100,000 annual CAM recovery gap. expenses the lease allows to be billed but aren't being billed correctly. represents $1.67 million in suppressed value.

What PE due diligence teams look for in the reconciliation records:

Recovery ratio trends. If the recovery ratio dropped 3-5 points over two years without a corresponding vacancy change, something is wrong with the reconciliation. The team will find out what before the deal closes, or they will discount the price to account for it.

GL account mapping. Are the accounts in the CAM pool actually supported by the lease? Accounts that have been included for years without anyone checking the lease definition are a standard finding. At acquisition, they become a liability.

Gross-up application. In a building with below-threshold occupancy, was the gross-up calculated correctly? Applied to variable expenses only? A miscalculated gross-up on a $2M pool is not a rounding error in a valuation model.

Cap bank history. If the lease has a CAM cap, was it tracked correctly? Over-cap charges that weren't corrected create both a refund obligation and a documentation gap that acquirers price negatively.

The value of clean CAM records at disposition is not theoretical. A 3-8 point improvement in recovery ratio on a $2M expense pool represents $60,000-$160,000 in annual NOI and $1-2.7 million in value at a 6% cap rate.
