A 10-point self-audit of a CAM reconciliation takes 2-4 hours. Most property managers have never done one.

The ten checks are not complicated. They require the lease, the GL, and the reconciliation statement. documents that should already be on hand before the statement is finalized.

1. Does the CAM pool total on the statement match the GL? Tie them out to the penny. 2. Is actual occupancy at or above the gross-up threshold? If yes, the gross-up factor should be 1.0. 3. Were property taxes excluded from the gross-up base? 4. Was insurance excluded from the gross-up base? 5. Is the management fee calculated on the correct base? Check the lease definition. 6. Are there any capital expenditures in the pool that should have been excluded? 7. Do any GL accounts contain expenses the lease explicitly excludes? 8. Is the tenant's square footage correct per the lease exhibit? 9. Is the building denominator consistent with prior years? If it changed, is there a lease amendment or remeasurement to support it? 10. If the lease has a CAM cap, was the cap bank calculated correctly from the base year?

Most of these checks take five minutes each. The gross-up check takes 10 minutes if the calculation was documented. The cap bank check takes 30 minutes if the history goes back more than three years.

The errors that generate tenant disputes and audit findings almost always show up on this list. Most have been present for multiple years before a tenant or an auditor asks about them.

Run the list before the statement goes out, not after the demand letter arrives.
