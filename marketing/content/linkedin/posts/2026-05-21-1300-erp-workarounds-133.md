"The Yardi export doesn't balance" does not mean the Yardi calculation is wrong.

It usually means the export is wrong. These are different problems with different fixes.

When a Yardi GL export fails to tie to the financial statements, teams often jump straight into tenant-level analysis. That is backward. The problem is almost always upstream in the export configuration or close process.

Five causes account for most balancing failures. Date basis mismatch: the export uses a different date logic than the statements. Account scope gaps: a saved export filter quietly drops accounts added mid-year after a chart restructuring. Accounting basis mismatch: the export is on a different basis than the statements even when the dates match. Intercompany structure: the export is entity-level and the statements are consolidated. Pre-close timing: the export was pulled before final journal entries posted, and the statements were refreshed later.

The triage sequence that saves hours: compare the export period to the statement period first, then check the included account set against the current chart of accounts, then confirm whether the export was pulled after final close. Only after those three checks should the analysis move to recovery pools or tenant-level logic.

Pattern recognition cuts diagnosis time: a large, abrupt gap suggests account scope or wrong entity. A small, persistent gap suggests date basis or late close adjustments. A gap that appears only at the consolidated level suggests intercompany treatment.

Do not debug a stale export like it is a live data issue.
