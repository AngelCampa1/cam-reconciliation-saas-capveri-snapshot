MRI can run a reconciliation export correctly and still be operationally incomplete.

The report finishes. The totals look reasonable. But the numbers are wrong.

Four causes account for most failures: the wrong entity was selected (the property is correct but the operating expenses sit in a different legal entity), period-13 year-end adjustments were omitted (they belong in the reconciliation year but were never included), recovery output was summary-level instead of detail-level (which strips out the account-by-account breakdown you need when something does not tie), and tenant identifiers are missing (name-only matching breaks down quickly when tenant naming is inconsistent across leasing, billing, and recovery views).

The three core exports for a complete MRI reconciliation: Recovery Reconciliation (what MRI thinks each tenant owes, by recovery type), GL Transaction Detail (the line-item transactions feeding the recovery totals), and Rent Roll (tenant roster, square footage, lease timing). Skip any of the three and the validation is incomplete.

The QA sequence that saves time runs in this order: verify entity and property selection, verify GL totals against the same-period financials, verify adjustment period treatment, verify detail-level recovery output, verify tenant ID and square footage in the rent roll. Only after those five checks should you start inspecting gross-up, caps, or allocation math.

One source of error specific to MRI: recovery setup that drifted after a mid-year chart of accounts change. The account was added. The mapping to the recovery type was never updated. MRI is calculating from an incomplete pool, and the report gives no indication that anything is missing.

Fix the export set before you trust the reconciliation.
